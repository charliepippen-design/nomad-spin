## Is living in Aarhus worth it for digital nomads in 2026?

Aarhus is a rational Denmark base if you want a compact university-and-culture city, safe streets, and good reported internet, and you can absorb a high monthly budget and a small nomad scene. The [Aarhus](/destinations/aarhus) row gives the planning numbers; treat them as Nomad Spin estimates, not a rental quote:

- **Cost:** about **$3,100** a month solo. The row does not publish a separate long-stay or nightly-rental figure, so this guide does not invent one.
- **Internet:** **120 Mbps** average, reliability not separately specified in the row, and coworking density **Low**. Test the actual apartment connection before relying on it.
- **Safety:** **9/10**, female safety **9/10**.
- **Stay length:** the destination row's visa label is **Startup Visa** and its duration field is **730 days**. Do not read that as a general digital-nomad visa entitlement. Short-stay Schengen rules and work authorization depend on your passport and activity; check the official Danish authorities before booking.
- **Skip it** if you need a large international nomad crowd, low costs, warm winters, or a high-density coworking fallback.

Nomad Spin tags Aarhus **workhub** and **adventure**, in **Europe**, with **Danish** as the local language and **UTC+1** as the listed time zone. Worth it, with conditions. Aarhus offers a walkable cultural core, university energy, and access to the coast, but it is not a cheap or automatically social base. ## Visas and stay length: check Danish rules before you work

The Aarhus row lists “Startup Visa” and **730 days**. Do not apply that label to your own circumstances without verifying the specific Danish residence scheme. A startup route is not a generic digital-nomad visa. Eligibility, permitted activities, and the right to work depend on the route and the applicant.

For a short stay, Denmark's official immigration authority explains the Schengen maximum as **90 days in any 180-day period** for visa holders and visa-exempt visitors, with days counted across the relevant Schengen area. The same authority notes that some work-related activities may be allowed during a short visit without a residence and work permit; it does not say every remote-work arrangement qualifies. If you are unsure whether your planned work activity is allowed, seek guidance from SIRI before travel. Rules can change, and your passport, prior Schengen days, employer relationship, and activity matter.

| Route or question | What to verify | Official reference |
|---|---|---|
| Short-stay visa or visa-free entry | Eligibility and your rolling Schengen day count | [New to Denmark: short-stay duration](https://www.nyidanmark.dk/en-GB/Words-and-concepts/US/Visum/Visa-%28short-term%29-%E2%80%93-How-long-are-you-allowed-to-stay-in-Denmark-and-the-other-Schengen-countries%27?anchor=3400727D0BC148F7B6C080D9825C10E5) |
