## Is living in Bangkok still worth it for digital nomads in 2026?

A straight answer on living in Bangkok as a digital nomad: still a rational yes in 2026 if you want a Southeast Asian megacity that can hold a work week, and a bad default if heat, traffic, or air will decide the month for you. The row on [Bangkok](/destinations/bangkok) is the spine: solo month **$1,100**, longer stay **$800**, nightly Airbnb median **$45**, internet **120 Mbps** with reliability **8/10**, power stability **8**, coworking density **High**, safety **7.8**. The same page tags it `party`, `foodie`, and `workhub`, on an urban landscape, in Asia, in Thai, at **UTC+7**. Best months are **Nov, Dec, Jan, Feb, Mar**. Rainy months are **Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct**. Annual average temperature is **29°C**. The visa field is **Visa Exemption**, up to **60 days**.

How much does it cost to live in Bangkok as a digital nomad? Nomad Spin lists about **$1,100** a month solo and about **$800** long-term, with a median Airbnb around **$45** a night. Condos near a BTS station cost more. An On Nut-style pin cuts rent. Street food keeps food spend flexible if you stay out of tourist-only zones.

Is Bangkok internet good enough for video calls? Yes for most condo fiber and coworking setups. Destination data shows about **120 Mbps** average, reliability **8/10**, power **8/10**, and High coworking density. Still test the unit Wi-Fi and keep an eSIM backup for building outages.

The pros are why people stay: world-class street food, amazing temples, great nightlife, modern infrastructure, and a hub for SE Asia travel. The cons are why a casual booking goes wrong: extreme heat, traffic nightmares, air pollution, tourist scams, and chaotic city planning. "Modern infrastructure" means the trains and the fiber exist. It does not mean a condo far from a station will feel modern at 6pm.

The fork most readers are actually making is [Chiang Mai](/destinations/chiang-mai), which already has a [living guide](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai). Chiang Mai's solo month is **$850**, safety is **8.2**, nightlife is **5**, and headline internet is **95 Mbps**. Bangkok's nightlife is **9** and the pipes are faster. You are choosing a megacity, not a louder Nimman.

If budget, internet, and safety are not locked yet, start with [How to Choose Your Next Nomad Base](/guides/how-to-choose-next-nomad-base). If the open question is the month, use [Where to Go Next by Season](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season). The wider shortlist is [Best Places for Digital Nomads in 2026](/guides/best-places-digital-nomads-2025). Use that list to find candidates. Use this page, and the live destination row, before you book.

Worth it, with conditions. The rest of the guide is the conditions.

## Who Bangkok is for (and who should skip it)

Bangkok fits people who want a work city with trains, food, and a real nightlife product. It does not fit people who wanted Chiang Mai and booked the bigger name.

**It tends to fit if you:**

- Want the dataset's solo baseline (about **$1,100**) or the longer-stay figure (**$800**), and you will actually live like that
- Want food and a social week at nightlife **9** and community **8**, with coworking density **High** when the home line fails
- Can work **UTC+7**. That overlaps Australia and much of Europe in the afternoon. A US East Coast morning lands in the Bangkok evening
- Can put the trip inside **Nov-Mar**, or you have a real plan for the **Jun-Oct** rains
- Will pick a walk to a BTS or MRT station before you pick a view
- Can work with safety **7.8**, female safety **7**, and `lgbtFriendly` **7**
- Are fine with English proficiency **5**: the scene works in English, a lease and an immigration counter often do not

Who should skip living in Bangkok? Skip it if you hate extreme heat, traffic, or dense megacity chaos. Air pollution and tourist-scam awareness matter. Prefer Chiang Mai for a cooler mountain pace and lower friction, or a beach base if you want quiet.

**Also skip it, or pick a different city, if you:**

- Need the ocean. This row is urban. [Bali](/destinations/bali) and the [Bali living guide](/guides/living-in-bali) are the beach comparison, with a different internet and visa story. [Da Nang](/destinations/da-nang) is the cheaper beach comparison
- Need the **$800** figure while living on Thonglor and eating every meal in Thonglor cafes
- Need a safety floor above **7.8**, or a female-safety floor above **7**. [Chiang Mai](/destinations/chiang-mai) is **8.2** with female safety **8**. [Lisbon](/destinations/lisbon) is **8.8** with female safety **8**
- Need US morning calls and will not work in the evening. **UTC+7** is not a US clock. [Mexico City](/destinations/mexico-city) is **UTC-6**. The [Mexico City living guide](/guides/living-in-mexico-city) is the on-the-ground version of that row
- Need a year of legal stay and will not assemble a real visa file. The destination row says visa exemption, **60 days**, and the rule published in 2026 is shorter than that field. The visa section is the correction
- Need a high LGBTQ+ score as a hard filter above this row. `lgbtFriendly` here is **7** (Lisbon's is **9**). Overall safety **7.8** does not answer that question

If you are still sorting those filters, the decision order is [how to choose a base](/guides/how-to-choose-next-nomad-base), not another neighborhood roundup.

## Real monthly cost bands (solo, long-term, short Airbnb)

Nomad Spin does not publish a fake "comfortable" total for Bangkok. It publishes three money fields. Those are the bands. Do not replace them with a blogger's spreadsheet.

| Band | Figure | Field on the Bangkok row | How to read it |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Longer stay | $800 / month | `financials.costLongTerm` | A local-rate month: monthly housing, local food, modest transport |
| Solo nomad baseline | $1,100 / month | `costUSD` (same as `costNomadSingle`) | The planning number for a solo remote worker |
| Nightly scouting | $45 / night | `financials.airbnbMedian` | What a short test stay tends to cost per night, not a monthly budget |

`rentIndex` is **22** (Chiang Mai **15**, Da Nang **10**, Bali **25**, Mexico City **28**, Lisbon **55**). That is relative rent pressure inside the dataset, not a dollar rent. Bangkok is the expensive Thailand option next to Chiang Mai. It is not a Western European capital.

### The nightly median will quietly beat the monthly baseline

Thirty nights at the published **$45** median is **$1,350** in lodging before food. That is already above the entire **$1,100** solo month. If you "try the city" on nightly Airbnb for a month, you are not on the $1,100 band, however cheap the pad thai is. The $800 and $1,100 figures assume a monthly place.

### What moves you off the band

No extra dollar totals here. These are the levers, in the order they actually matter.

| Choice | Stays near $800 to $1,100 | Leaves the baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Housing | A monthly condo within a walk of one BTS or MRT station, often further out (On Nut and similar) | Nightly Airbnb at the $45 median, or a new Thonglor or Ekkamai condo as the default |
| Food | Street food, markets, and local restaurants most days | Malls and Thonglor restaurants as the lunch plan |
| Work | Home fiber, plus coworking on call days | All-day cafe hopping, plus a premium desk you do not use |
| Transport | BTS or MRT for the hops you repeat, walking the last block | Grab as the way you cross town twice a day in traffic |
| Calendar | One neighborhood, **Nov-Mar** | A non-refundable lease through April heat, or through rains you did not mean to book |

Annual average temperature on the row is **29°C**. That is a coarse yearly figure, not a forecast. It does not tell you April will be fine.

### Reality checks

- The **$1,100** number is a city baseline, not a Sukhumvit lifestyle quote.
- Imported groceries, rooftop bars, and "just one more" Grab are how people blow a city that looks cheap next to Lisbon's **$2,200**.
- A cheaper room that needs a taxi to the train is not cheaper. The traffic con eats the savings.
- Insurance is not inside those three fields. Budget it separately. This guide does not invent a premium.

## Neighborhoods on the BTS/MRT: Ari, Sukhumvit / Thonglor / Ekkamai, On Nut, Silom (trade-offs)

Bangkok is too large to "live in Bangkok" in the abstract. Traffic is a listed con. The pin that matters is a walk to a station, not a neighborhood name on a blog. There are no separate Nomad Spin destination pages for these areas. The canonical numbers live on [Bangkok](/destinations/bangkok). The trade-offs below are how people actually use the trains.

| Base | Best for | You give up |
|---|---|---|
| Ari | Cafes, residential streets, a work week that is not a party | Thonglor nightlife on the doorstep, the lowest rent |
| Sukhumvit / Thonglor / Ekkamai | Restaurants, bars, condos, the easy social week | The $800 band, quiet nights, a local feel |
| On Nut | Value on the same Sukhumvit line, a station walk | Cafe density and the feeling of being in the middle |
| Silom | Offices, lunch sets, BTS plus MRT, a calmer evening | The food-and-bar scene outside the door |

### Ari

Ari sits on the Sukhumvit BTS line, north of the main tourist strip. It is the neighborhood long-stayers recommend when they want cafes and condos without sleeping above a bar. Streets are more residential. Food is a mix of local shops and laptop cafes. You can do a work week on foot if your days stay inside Ari, and you can ride the train south when you want Thonglor.

**Trade-off:** Ari is popular, so "quiet residential" is not the same as cheap. A new condo here will not automatically land on the **$800** longer-stay figure. Ask whether the bedroom faces the main road, and whether anyone is building on the soi. A cheap unit next to a site is a productivity tax.

Pick Ari if you will use the cafes and you want to sleep. Skip it if the only reason you chose Bangkok over Chiang Mai is nightlife **9**. You can visit Thonglor for dinner. You do not have to sleep there.

### Sukhumvit / Thonglor / Ekkamai

Sukhumvit, from the Nana and Asok stretch through Phrom Phong to Thonglor and Ekkamai, is the default "I want the city without trying" base. BTS stations are close together. Condos, gyms, restaurants, and bars are the product. Thong Lo and Ekkamai are the lifestyle end of that line: later nights, more restaurants, higher demand. If you arrive alone and want a social calendar in the first week, this corridor is the easiest place in the city to get one.

**Trade-off:** this is where the **$1,100** baseline goes to die if every meal is a restaurant meal and the condo is new. The main road is also where the traffic actually is. Inside a soi near a station it can be pleasant. On Sukhumvit at peak hour it is the traffic nightmare the row already lists.

Asok is the interchange (BTS plus MRT), which is useful, and it is also one of the louder, more tourist-scam-aware stretches. A room over a bar on a busy soi will not be a call studio. Test the night before you test the Wi-Fi.

### On Nut

On Nut is further east on the same Sukhumvit BTS line. It is the compromise people use when they want the train and they want the **$800** to **$1,100** bands to survive contact with rent. More residential, more ordinary Bangkok, fewer laptop cafes at the door. You ride west for Thonglor or for a coworking day. You do not step out into twenty cafes.

Best for a second month, or for a first month if you already know you do not need the Thonglor scene to feel oriented. Weaker if your first week has to be walkable social life. Pick the building for the station walk, not for a pin that says "On Nut" and is a Grab ride from the stairs.

### Silom

Silom and the Sathorn edge are the business district. BTS (Sala Daeng, Chong Nonsi, and the stations around them) meets the MRT. Lunch sets, offices, and a calmer evening than Thonglor. Lumpini is the park on this side of town. You can run a meeting-heavy week here if the condo fiber holds and you like the feeling of a city that goes to work.

**Trade-off:** the nights are quieter because the neighborhood is an office district, not because you found a secret village. Weekend Silom is a different density from Monday Silom. If you chose Bangkok for nightlife **9**, this is a base you leave in the evening, not the evening itself.

## Internet, power, and coworking for video-call work

The city average is **120 Mbps**, reliability **8/10**, power stability **8**, coworking density **High**. That is a different product from Bali, where the dataset shows **50 Mbps** and reliability **5**, and the [Bali living guide](/guides/living-in-bali) is blunt about street-by-street fiber. It is also faster on the headline than Chiang Mai's **95 Mbps**, at the same reliability **8**. Bangkok's 8/10 is still an average. It is not a promise about the condo you have not tested.

Power is not the Cape Town problem. [Living in Cape Town](/guides/living-in-cape-town) has to plan around `powerGridStability` **4**. Bangkok's power score is **8**. Keep a power bank for cafes and for the odd building outage. You do not need a personal inverter strategy to have a work week here.

### A setup that holds calls

1. **Home line you have tested.** In a condo, ask which fiber is already in the unit (AIS and True are the lines people actually find in Bangkok buildings) and run a speed test from the room at the hour you take calls. A lobby screenshot is not the room.
2. **A local SIM with real data.** AIS and True are the practical tourist SIMs, at the airport or a mall. If a call would hurt you to drop, keep a second SIM or eSIM on the other network. Learn the hotspot before the day you need it.
3. **One coworking room you have already used for a call.** Not a cafe you liked for breakfast.

### Coworking that is actually listed

**The Hive Thonglor** is a real, currently published workspace on Sukhumvit 49, a short walk from BTS Thong Lo. Its own site lists hot desks, dedicated desks, and shorter passes, and it runs a free try-out day that you book ahead. Read the live membership page for the price. This guide does not copy a baht figure into the dataset. Do the trial with a real call before you tell a client the room is your office.

Treat other famous names (WeWork, Spaces, Glowfish, and the old Hubba roundups) as open only when the operator's current site lists that branch. Spaces in this city move, rebrand, and disappear. A trial morning beats a 2019 list.

### Cafes

Ari and Thonglor will let you open a laptop in a lot of rooms. That is not the same as a 45-minute call. Lunch rush, blenders, and a playlist you do not control will lose to a booked room every time. Do the call once, at the hour you actually meet, before you rely on the cafe.

Malls along the BTS (the big ones at Phrom Phong, Asok, and Siam) are a daytime backup when you need air conditioning and a stable connection. They are not a home office, and they are not a late-night plan.

### Checklist for a rental you might keep

- Speed test in the room, not a screenshot from the lobby
- Which provider, and whether anyone else on the booking can knock the line over
- A quiet corner with a door, if you talk for a living
- A named fallback (a confirmed coworking branch, or a second SIM) written down before you need it

## Visas and stay length: 60-day exemption vs longer options including DTV (verify official rules)

This is operational orientation, not legal advice. Thai entry rules changed during 2026. The number on our city page did not.

What visa or stay options do digital nomads use in Bangkok? Nomad Spin lists Visa Exemption for up to **60 days** (**UTC+7**). Longer stays often use a different visa, including Thailand's Destination Thailand Visa (DTV). Rules change. Verify official immigration sources before you plan past 60 days.

### What Nomad Spin currently shows

The Bangkok row says **Visa Exemption**, **60 days**. Chiang Mai's row says the same pair. That is what you will see on [/destinations/bangkok](/destinations/bangkok) and [/destinations/chiang-mai](/destinations/chiang-mai). Use it as the product field. Do not use it as the rule.

### What the government published

On 4 September 2026 the Government Public Relations Department summarized a revision already published in the Royal Gazette on 31 August 2026, effective **15 September 2026**. The measures include revoking the **60-day** visa exemption scheme, a **30-day** tourism exemption for **60** countries and territories, a **15-day** tourism exemption for two countries and territories, and a shorter visa-on-arrival list (three countries and territories). The notice says this review covers those exemption and visa-on-arrival schemes for tourism, and does not by itself rewrite other visa categories. Stays already stamped before the change run to the date on the stamp.

If the destination page says 60 and the stamp says 30, believe the stamp. Read the [government summary](https://thailand.prd.go.th/en/content/category/detail/id/48/iid/538547) and the Thai mission for your passport before you pay for a non-refundable month. The Department of Consular Affairs publishes the country list. This guide will not copy all 60 countries, because that list is the government's and it can be amended. Your passport may be on the shorter scheme, on visa on arrival, or on "visa in advance."

Since 1 May 2025, foreigners have had to complete the Thailand Digital Arrival Card before entry. The Immigration Bureau's own site, [tdac.immigration.go.th](https://tdac.immigration.go.th/), says the card is required, that you submit it up to three days before arrival, and that it is not a visa. Fill it in yourself on that site. Do not pay a random site to do it.

The same official page restates two Immigration Act points that matter if you stay longer than a holiday: if you stay in Thailand longer than **90 days**, you notify the nearest immigration officer in writing of your address, and you do that on the 90-day cycle; and you may not work unless you have been granted a work permit. A remote-work visa category is not a Thai employment contract.

Do not assume the old habit of extending a 60-day exemption by another 30 days inside the country survived September 2026. The published review replaced that exemption. If an extension still exists for your stamp, immigration has to be the source, not a forum post. Do not build the season on border runs.

### Tourist visa, if the exemption is too short

A tourist visa, applied for before you fly, is a different product from the exemption stamp. Stay length, whether an extension exists, the fee, and "we will not issue this if you are already in Thailand" are on the checklist of the mission that receives the file. The [Chiang Mai living guide](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) records one consulate's published version of that visa as orientation. Use the live page for your passport the week you apply. Processing times are real. A visa that starts counting when it is issued is a bad thing to buy six months early.

### Destination Thailand Visa (DTV)

The DTV is the category people mean by "Thailand's digital nomad visa." It is not a program this guide is inventing, and it is not a work permit for a Thai employer.

On 30 May 2024 the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, in its weekly press briefing, described the DTV as a visa for long-term travel, digital nomads, and participants in certain cultural activities, with a period of stay up to **180 days** per visit, multiple-entry, within **5 years**. The ministry's DTV infographic (July 2024) says the same shape: five years, multiple entries, up to 180 days per entry, extendable once through immigration for a period not exceeding 180 days, after which you leave and re-enter within the visa's validity. Purposes on that sheet are workcation (remote work, freelance, foreign talent), specified activities such as Muay Thai, cooking, sport, medical treatment, seminars, and festivals, and a spouse or dependent child of a DTV holder.

Financial evidence on that same infographic is an amount of no less than **500,000 THB**, shown for example with bank statements, payslips, or a sponsorship letter. Workcation applicants show an employment contract or employment certificate from outside Thailand, or a professional portfolio. That is a balance the ministry printed, not a monthly income floor this guide is adding. The public e-Visa channel the infographic names is [thaievisa.go.th](https://www.thaievisa.go.th/visa/dtv-visa). Apply from outside Thailand, through the post that actually accepts your file.

The September 2026 exemption notice says it does not rewrite other visa categories. That is not a promise that every embassy's document list froze in July 2024. Posts add documents (residence where you apply, a criminal-record certificate, and similar). Fees on the e-Visa screen are what you pay; a figure printed for one post is not automatically another post's fee. Copy the current checklist the week you apply. Do not copy a 2024 screenshot from a Facebook group, and do not treat a comment as the rule.

Other long-stay categories exist (education visas, Thailand Privilege, the Long-Term Resident visa). They have their own thresholds. This guide does not explain them. If you will be in Thailand most of the year, also get real tax advice at home and, if needed, in Thailand. A long stay can create tax questions. Nothing here answers them.

### A simple decision frame

- **A few weeks, and your passport qualifies:** the current exemption, after you confirm the days and the conditions, with the TDAC done yourself.
- **A season longer than that stamp:** a tourist visa from the correct mission, with the extension rules read in advance.
- **Many months, working for foreign clients, and you can meet the file:** the DTV, using the live e-Visa checklist, including the 500,000 THB evidence the MFA materials state.
- **A year via border runs:** not a plan.

## Best months, rainy season, extreme heat, and air quality

The weather fields are short, and they are enough to avoid the expensive mistake.

| Window | What the Bangkok row says | Practical read |
|---|---|---|
| Nov, Dec, Jan, Feb, Mar | `bestMonths` | The work season. Cooler and drier than the rest of the year, higher demand for the good condos near stations |
| Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct | `rainyMonths` | Hot, wet, storms. Fine if you chose it. Not the postcard month |
| Apr, May | On neither list | The gap. Best months have ended. Rains have not started. This is where extreme heat sits |
| Year-round cons | Extreme heat, air pollution | Not a one-week event. The 29°C annual average hides the bad weeks |

When are the best months to live in Bangkok? Friendliest months in Nomad Spin data: **Nov, Dec, Jan, Feb, Mar**. Rainy months: **Jun through Oct**. The cooler dry season is the sweet spot. April heat, outside both lists, is the hardest stretch for many remote workers.

Chiang Mai's hard stop is dated: burning season in Mar-Apr, and its best months end in February. Bangkok's best months include March, and the row does not put a burning-season label on April. What it does put in `cons` is extreme heat and air pollution, next to a **29°C** annual average. People feel April and May as the hot, hazy gap. Some years are milder. You do not get to know which year you booked. The [season guide](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season) is the calendar version of this check: read both month arrays, then read the cons.

### What heat and air do to a work month

This is not medical advice, and this guide does not invent an air-quality index. Extreme heat means the walk to the station in the afternoon is the thing that wrecks a day of calls, not the temperature on a yearly average. Air pollution means some weeks you will not want the outdoor breakfast that made the city sound romantic. Sensitive lungs notice first.

Practical responses that work: work in air conditioning during the hot hours, a purifier in the room if you are sensitive, a mask on the bad commute, and a look at a public air-quality reading the week you are there. The response that does not work is a non-refundable Thonglor lease from 1 April because the flight was cheap. Rainy season is a different trade: wet, still hot, often easier to book. It is not a cool season. Do not mix the two up to talk yourself into April.

A best month is also not a clean-air guarantee. The air-pollution con is not limited to April the way Chiang Mai's burning season is limited to Mar-Apr. If clean air is a hard filter, Bangkok can lose in November too.

## Daily life: street food, transit, nightlife, SE Asia hub logistics

### Food

Bangkok is a food city (the row is tagged `foodie` for a reason). Street food is also how the **$1,100** month stays honest. Markets and ordinary shophouses do that work. A default of mall food courts and Thonglor restaurants does not. You can tell which life you are living by where lunch happens. Temples are a real pro on the row, and they are a weekend, not a housing plan.

Water: drink filtered or bottled. That is normal here, not a special warning.

### Transit

The neighborhood section is the transport plan. Sukhumvit line and Silom line (BTS), the MRT, and the Airport Rail Link from Suvarnabhumi are how you stop paying the traffic con every day. A Rabbit card or the current stored-value equivalent covers the trains you repeat. Grab, or a metered taxi, is the right tool when the train does not cover the hop, especially at night. It is the wrong tool as the way you cross the city twice a day. Rush hour is the "traffic nightmares" line on the row, not a surprise.

You can live without a scooter, which is the main logistical difference from a Bali villa month. A scooter in Bangkok traffic is how people turn a work trip into a clinic trip. If you ride anyway, know what your insurance actually covers before you take the keys.

Two airports matter. Suvarnabhumi (BKK) is the long-haul airport, with the rail link toward the BTS. Don Mueang (DMK) is where a lot of low-cost flights actually land. Read the ticket. "Bangkok" on a boarding pass is not one building.

### Nightlife, safety, and healthcare

Nightlife **9** is the product, next to Chiang Mai's **5**. If you need to sleep, do not put the bedroom on a bar soi and then blame the city. The social week is easy if you go back to the same rooms. It is also easy to spend a month in Sukhumvit and only meet people who are leaving on Friday.

Safety **7.8** sits level with Bali's **7.8** and below Chiang Mai's **8.2** and Da Nang's **8.5**. Female safety on the row is **7**. `lgbtFriendly` is **7**. The practical risks nomads actually hit are tourist scams (a closed-temple pitch, a gem shop, a stranger with a plan that is too smooth), a phone on a cafe table, and ordinary nightlife common sense. This is not a city that forbids a walked evening near a busy station. It is a city with a scam pattern aimed at new arrivals, which is why "tourist scams" is a listed con and not a footnote.

Private hospitals in town are the regional reason people fly here for care. Bumrungrad, Bangkok Hospital, and Samitivej are the names people use. You are a cash patient until an insurer says otherwise. Sort that before you need the desk, using the insurance slot above, not a policy you buy from a popup.

### Community and the hub

Community **8** shows up as coworking, recurring dinners, and the fact that a lot of people pass through. It is not Chiang Mai's **9**, and it does not have to be. English at **5** means the scene is easy and the bureaucracy is not. A little Thai, or a patient agent, matters for a lease more than for a menu.

The "hub for SE Asia travel" pro is real: Chiang Mai, Vietnam, and Malaysia are ordinary flight plans from here, not expeditions. That is a reason to base in Bangkok if you will actually take those hops. It is not a reason to live in an airport hotel. Book the onward trip after the visa stamp and the neighborhood are real, not before.

**Stay with Bangkok** when you want nightlife **9**, food at this scale, **120 Mbps** with reliability **8**, and a SE Asia flight hub, and you will live on a train line inside **$1,100** (or **$800** if that is the real ceiling). You accept heat, traffic, air, and a visa field our page still shows as 60 days.

**Choose Chiang Mai** when you want community **9** at **$850**, safety **8.2**, and you can take Nov-Feb. Read the [Chiang Mai living guide](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) before a March arrival. The visa field in our data is the same 60-day exemption, which means Spin will not show you the September 2026 change for either city. Check the embassy either way. Pick Chiang Mai if the megacity cons are the thing you keep negotiating with yourself about.

**Look at Bali** when you want the beach and community **9**, and you accept **$1,200**, internet reliability **5**, and a visa-on-arrival field of **30 days**. Read [Living in Bali](/guides/living-in-bali) before you assume the island is Bangkok with sand. [Canggu](/destinations/canggu), [Ubud](/destinations/ubud), and [Seminyak](/destinations/seminyak) are different pins on [Bali](/destinations/bali). None of them are a 120 Mbps city average.

**Look at Da Nang** when you want a beach, a lower solo month (**$700**), and safety **8.5**, and you can live with a smaller scene (community **5**, nightlife **3**, coworking **Med**). Best months are **Feb-Jul**. Rainy months are **Sep-Dec**, and typhoon season is in the cons on [Da Nang](/destinations/da-nang). The visa field is an e-visa, **90 days**. Same **UTC+7** clock as Bangkok, different city.

**Look at Mexico City** when you need US hours (**UTC-6**), food and nightlife **9**, and a visa field of up to **180 days**, and you accept safety **6.0**, altitude, and air. The solo month is **$1,300**. Read [Living in Mexico City](/guides/living-in-mexico-city) before you treat it as Bangkok on a US clock. It is the right comparison if the question is "megacity" and the question is not "Asia."

If the alternative is not a peer in that table, [Cape Town](/guides/living-in-cape-town) is another full living guide in this set: solo **$1,400**, safety **5.5**, power stability **4**. [Lisbon](/destinations/lisbon) is the premium megacity contrast: solo **$2,200**, **200 Mbps**, safety **8.8**, a digital nomad visa field of **365 days**, **UTC+0**. Read [Living in Lisbon](/guides/living-in-lisbon) for the neighborhoods and the D8. Different budgets. Different guides.

## First-week checklist + compare Bangkok on Nomad Spin

### Common mistakes

- Treating the **60-day** exemption field as the current rule after 15 September 2026
- Paying the **$45** nightly median for thirty nights and calling the month **$1,100**
- Choosing Thonglor, then acting surprised that the long-stay **$800** figure did not survive contact with restaurants
- Booking a condo that needs a taxi to the BTS, then blaming Bangkok traffic
- Using cafe Wi-Fi for the client call you cannot drop
- Hunting a coworking branch that is not on the operator's current site
- Booking April because it was not on the rainy-month list, and ignoring the heat con
- Using border runs as the long-stay plan
- Comparing Bangkok only with Chiang Mai's photos, and never opening Bali, Da Nang, or Mexico City

### First week

**Days 1-2**

- SIM in the phone, hotspot tested
- Thailand Digital Arrival Card already done before you landed, not in the immigration line
- One confirmed coworking trial, including a real call
- Walk the neighborhood at night, including the walk to the station, so you know whether you can sleep there and whether you can work there

**Days 3-4**

- See more than one monthly place. Run the speed test in the room
- Write down the stay limit from the stamp or visa, and the date you must extend, switch visas, or leave
- If the air or the heat is already the day, do not sign through the months the cons already name

**Days 5-7**

- Commit to one station for two to four weeks, not to "Bangkok" in the abstract
- Fix the work loop: home deep work, coworking on call days, one cafe that passed the call test
- Only then book the longer stay, the onward flight, and insurance

### Booking placeholders

Still no partner IDs. These slots are last, after the week above, not before the flight is emotional. Use the modules on the [Bangkok destination page](/destinations/bangkok).

| Slot | What to book |
|---|---|
| Stays | The monthly place that passed the speed test, the noise test, and the station walk |
| Flights | The onward or return date your visa stamp actually supports, into the airport the ticket names |
