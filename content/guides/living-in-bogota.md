## Is living in Bogotá worth it for digital nomads in 2026?

Bogotá is a practical work base if you want a large Colombian city, a cool high-altitude climate, and a lower monthly baseline than many North American or European hubs—and if you are prepared for traffic, Spanish-first daily life, and a modest safety score. The [Bogotá destination row](/destinations/bogota) is the source for the city figures:

- **Cost:** about **$1,000** a month solo, **$750** for a longer stay, and a **$35** median nightly Airbnb. A nightly month is a different budget.
- **Internet:** average **75 Mbps**, reliability **7/10**, power stability **7/10**, and **High** coworking density. Test the specific apartment before relying on calls.
- **Safety:** **5.8** overall and **4** female safety. Treat neighborhood and after-dark transport choices as part of your plan.
- **Stay length:** the dataset lists visa exemption and **90 days**. This is not a promise for every passport or a long-stay permit. Check Colombia’s official [visa information](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/tramites_servicios/visa) and [Migration Colombia](https://www.migracioncolombia.gov.co/) for your nationality and current procedures.
- **Skip it** if you need high safety scores, low-traffic commuting, or a warm low-altitude climate.

Bogotá’s row tags it **workhub**, **foodie**, and **mountain**, with **Spanish** as the language and **UTC-5** as the time zone. The listed friendlier months are **Dec, Jan, Feb, Jul, and Aug**; rainy months are **Apr, May, Oct, and Nov**. Its annual average temperature is **14°C**. Those are planning signals, not a forecast: check the weather for your dates.

Worth it with conditions. Bogotá offers cultural depth and a substantial city workday, but its safety figure is the hard trade-off. ### How much does it cost to live in Bogotá?

Nomad Spin lists about **$1,000** a month solo, **$750** for a longer stay, and **$35** a night for the median Airbnb. These are distinct planning fields, not a guarantee of your personal spend. Thirty nights at $35 is already $1,050 in lodging before food or transport. A monthly lease and local routine are more consistent with the longer-stay figure than a month of nightly accommodation.

### Is Bogotá internet good enough for remote work?

The city-level average is **75 Mbps**, with reliability **7/10** and power stability **7/10**. Coworking density is **High**, so a fallback desk may be available, but averages cannot tell you what a particular building delivers. Test upload speed from the room, ask whether the connection is shared, and try a real call before committing. Keep mobile data as a backup.

### What visa do digital nomads use in Bogotá?

The dataset’s **90-day visa-exemption** label is a starting point, not individualized immigration advice. Colombia’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs says eligible visa-exempt digital nomads may enter with a migration permission for up to **90 days**, extendable to a maximum of **180 days** in a calendar year, provided they do not receive payments from Colombian companies. The same ministry lists a **Visa V Nómadas digitales**, valid for up to **two years**, for qualifying remote work exclusively for foreign companies; it publishes documentary, income, and health-insurance requirements. Eligibility depends on passport and current rules. Read the [official digital-nomad visa requirements](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/node/26950) and [Migration Colombia](https://www.migracioncolombia.gov.co/) before making a long booking. Do not assume a tourist stay authorizes local employment. These rules can change.

## Who Bogotá is for (and who should skip it)

Bogotá tends to fit remote workers who want a major Latin American cultural center, can work on **UTC-5**, and will choose a neighborhood carefully. It is a poor fit if safety is your non-negotiable, or if you expect a small, easy-to-cross city.

**It tends to fit if you:**

- Can plan around the **$1,000** solo baseline—or genuinely secure a longer-stay setup near the **$750** dataset figure.
- Need **UTC-5** and a city with **High** coworking density.
- Are comfortable with Spanish as the day-to-day language; the row’s English proficiency is **4**.
- Can adapt to altitude and a cool climate, and make indoor plans for rainy stretches.
- Understand that safety is **5.8** overall and female safety **4**, rather than treating those numbers as a neighborhood guarantee.

**Skip it, or compare another city, if you:**

- Need a high safety or female-safety rating as a firm floor.
- Want to avoid traffic and long cross-city journeys.
- Need consistently warm weather or dislike altitude adjustment.
- Expect English to handle leases, appointments, and ordinary errands.
- Need a long stay but have not verified the current route for your passport.

## Internet, power, and coworking

Bogotá’s dataset averages **75 Mbps**, reliability **7/10**, power stability **7/10**, and **High** coworking density. Treat these as city-level comparisons, not a service-level promise. Confirm the provider and run a speed test at the desk, including upload speed. If calls matter, identify a coworking backup and test a local SIM’s hotspot before you need it.

A rental checklist:

- Test the connection inside the unit at a normal work hour.
- Ask whether the router or line is shared with other apartments.
- Check for a quiet room or a door you can close during calls.
- Confirm the nearest coworking option is open and offers the access you need.
- Keep mobile data available for outages or transport delays.

## Best months, rain, and altitude

The Bogotá row lists **Dec, Jan, Feb, Jul, and Aug** as best months, and **Apr, May, Oct, and Nov** as rainy months. Average temperature is **14°C**. Bogotá sits at about **2,600 metres** above sea level, and altitude adjustment is one of the listed cons. The city’s official information also describes a year-round mean near **14°C**. Give yourself time to acclimate and avoid assuming the mild-looking average describes every day. See [Bogotá’s official city information](https://bogota.gov.co/informacion-general-de-bogota).

## Daily life: culture, food, traffic, and safety

Bogotá’s row lists a cultural-capital feel, coffee culture, and an art scene among its pros. Museums, markets, restaurants, and neighborhood routines offer plenty to explore between workdays. Spanish will make practical errands easier; the dataset’s English proficiency score is **4**, so do not assume every landlord, clinic, or service desk can work in English.

Traffic is a listed con. Choose accommodation around your actual weekly routine, not a map pin that looks central. Walk the immediate area at different times, ask trusted local contacts about routes, and use a reliable transport option after dark. Avoid displaying valuables, keep devices secure, and make arrival plans before reaching an unfamiliar address. These are prudent city habits, not a guarantee that a particular area is safe.

The row’s safety score is **5.8** and female safety score is **4**. Scores help compare destinations; they cannot predict an individual experience or substitute for current local advice. If those scores fall below your comfort threshold, choose a different base rather than rationalizing them away.
