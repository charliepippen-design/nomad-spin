## Is living in Da Nang worth it for digital nomads in 2026?

A straight answer on living in Da Nang as a digital nomad: still a rational yes in 2026 if you want a cheap, safe beach and you can live with a small scene and a hard stop in September. It is a no if you need nightlife, a Chiang Mai-sized community, or a year on one stamp. The short answers come from the [Da Nang](/destinations/da-nang) row, not from a beach photo:

- **Cost:** about **$700** a month solo, about **$500** on a longer stay. A short Airbnb sits near **$25** a night, which is a different budget.
- **Internet:** usually yes for video calls. The average is **80 Mbps**, reliability **7/10**, power stability **7/10**, coworking density **Med**.
- **Stay length:** the row says **E-Visa**, up to **90 days**, time zone **UTC+7**. Vietnam does not publish a dedicated digital nomad visa. Verify the current e-visa on the official portal before you treat 90 days as permission.
- **Skip it** if you need a big social calendar. Nightlife on the row is **3**. Community size is **5**. The page's trade-offs are typhoon season, limited nightlife, and a smaller city.
- **Best months:** **Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul**. Rainy months are **Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec**. Average temperature on the row is **26°C**, a yearly figure, not a forecast.

The same page tags the city **beach**, **workhub**, and **family**, on a **seaside** landscape, in **Asia**, language **Vietnamese**. Safety is **8.5** (female safety **8**). Pros are stunning beaches, ultra affordable, very safe, a growing nomad scene, and beautiful scenery.

Worth it, with a calendar. Feb-Jul is the work season. Sep-Dec is when storms, flooding, and mold stop being a footnote. If budget, internet, and safety are not locked yet, start with [How to Choose Your Next Nomad Base](/guides/how-to-choose-next-nomad-base). If the open question is the month, use [Where to Go Next by Season](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season). The shortlist that still treats Southeast Asia as a region, not a single beach, is [Best Places for Digital Nomads in 2026](/guides/best-places-digital-nomads-2025).

### Straight answers

**How much does it cost to live in Da Nang as a digital nomad?** Nomad Spin lists about $700 a month for a solo nomad and about $500 a month on a long-term basis, with a median Airbnb around $25 a night. Beach-side An Thuong costs more than Hai Chau. Western food, a pool building, and coworking push you above the baseline.

**Is Da Nang internet good enough for video calls?** Usually yes. Nomad Spin shows about 80 Mbps, reliability 7/10, power 7/10, and coworking density Med. Test the upload in the actual unit before you sign, and keep a mobile-data backup for storm days in the Sep-Dec rainy season.

**What visa do digital nomads use in Da Nang?** Nomad Spin lists an E-Visa for up to 90 days. Vietnam has no dedicated digital nomad visa, so a longer stay is a new e-visa from outside the country, or a sponsored visa if the law allows it. Fees and entry type change. Verify on the official portal before you book.

**Who should skip living in Da Nang?** Skip it if you want big-city nightlife, a large scene, or zero paperwork. The page flags typhoon season, limited nightlife, and a smaller city. English proficiency on the row is 3. If you cannot work indoors or leave during Sep-Dec, pick another base for those months.

**What are the best months to live in Da Nang?** Nomad Spin's friendliest months are Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, and Jul. Rainy months are Sep, Oct, Nov, and Dec, when storms can disrupt work. Average temperature on the row is 26°C. August is on neither list. Arrive in February, and leave before September if storms would stop your work.

## Who Da Nang is for (and who should skip it)

Da Nang fits people who want a beach they can walk to, a bill that can actually land near **$700**, and a city that is calm on a Tuesday. It is a poor fit if the social scene is the product, or if Sep-Dec is the only window you have.

**It tends to fit if you:**

- Can fund the solo baseline (about **$700**) or will actually live inside the longer-stay figure (**$500**)
- Need calls to work, and you will test the room. **80 Mbps** and reliability **7/10** are enough for most video days, with a backup SIM for storms
- Want safety **8.5** and female safety **8**, and you do not need nightlife above **3**
- Can put the serious months in **Feb-Jul**, or you have a real plan for **Sep-Dec**
- Like the **family** tag: a smaller seaside city, not a party island
- Can work **UTC+7**. Vietnam does not shift clocks, so the overlap stays put. US East Coast mornings are Da Nang evenings. US West Coast overlap is later still. It is the same clock as [Chiang Mai](/destinations/chiang-mai) and [Bangkok](/destinations/bangkok)

**Skip it, or pick another city, if you:**

- Cannot clear **$500** even on a monthly lease with local food. The **$25** nightly median will not save you
- Want community **9** and a cafe full of other nomads by default. That is [Living in Chiang Mai](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) or [Living in Bali](/guides/living-in-bali), not this row (community **5**)
- Want nightlife. The score is **3**, and "limited nightlife" is a listed trade-off, not a modest way of saying the bars are a short walk
- Need English for the lease, the clinic, and the landlord. English proficiency is **3**. The dataset also carries a language-barrier con. The short trade-off list on the page leads with typhoon season, nightlife, and city size. The language friction is still real
- Cannot leave or work indoors from **Sep** through **Dec**. Typhoon season is a listed con. It is the reason to pick another base for those months, including [Cape Town](/guides/living-in-cape-town) if you want a different beach and you have read that city's safety and power scores
- Need a year of legal stay with no new application. The field is **90 days**, and there is no nomad visa to stretch it

`lgbtFriendly` on this row is **4**. That is a weaker score than Chiang Mai (**6**) or Bangkok (**7**). Read it before you treat "very safe" as a claim about every part of daily life. Female safety **8** is strong in this dataset. It is not a promise about an empty beach road at 1am.

If you are still sorting those filters, the decision order is [how to choose a base](/guides/how-to-choose-next-nomad-base), not another viewpoint list.

`rentIndex` is **10** (Ho Chi Minh City **14**, Chiang Mai **15**, Bangkok **22**, Bali **25**). That is relative rent pressure inside the dataset, not a dollar rent. Da Nang is the cheap beach in this set. "Ultra affordable" is a listed pro, and the **$700** figure is how the dataset prices it. Do not add a nostalgia discount, and do not subtract the beach.

### The nightly median will quietly wreck the monthly baseline

Thirty nights at the published **$25** median is **$750** in lodging before food. That is already above the entire **$700** solo month, and it is **$250** above the **$500** longer stay. If you "try the city" on nightly Airbnb for a month, you are not on either monthly band, no matter how cheap the noodle soup is. The $500 and $700 figures assume a monthly place.

A 2-4 week landing stay near An Thuong is a scout. It is not the month. Use it to see leases. Do not call it the **$500** life.

### What moves you off the band

No extra dollar totals here. These are the levers, in the order they actually matter.

| Choice | Stays near $500 to $700 | Leaves the baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Housing | A monthly lease in Hai Chau, or a local building set back from My Khe | Thirty nights at the $25 median, or a beachfront serviced apartment as the default |
| Food | Com, bun, markets, and seafood that is priced for the city | An Thuong brunch, imported groceries, and "just one more" Western dinner as the meal plan |
| Work | Home fiber you have tested, plus coworking on call days | A cafe day-pass habit on top of a beach apartment you already overpaid for |

| Calendar | **Feb-Jul**, one neighborhood | **Sep-Dec** in a ground-floor unit, then acting surprised by leaks, mold, and a week you cannot work |

### Reality checks

- The **$700** number is a city baseline, not an An Thuong pool-villa quote.
- The growing nomad scene is a listed pro. It is also how Western menus show up. The dataset cannot see your receipt.
- A cheaper room on a bar street in An Thuong is not cheaper if you cannot sleep or take a call. Nightlife is thin citywide. It is not thin on every block of the beach strip.
- Insurance is not inside those three fields. Budget it separately. This guide does not invent a premium.

## Neighborhoods that work: An Thuong, My Khe, Hai Chau, Son Tra, and Ngu Hanh Son

Da Nang is a beach city split by the Han River. The wrong side of the river, or the wrong block of the same beach, wastes the month. There are no separate Nomad Spin destination pages for these areas. Do not use a Da Nang outskirts URL as a stand-in. The canonical numbers live on [Da Nang](/destinations/da-nang). The trade-offs below are how people actually use the city.

| Base | Best for | You give up |
|---|---|---|
| An Thuong / My Khe | The beach, cafes, the easiest first week, other foreigners | The $500 band, and quiet if the room faces a bar or a construction site |
| Hai Chau | A local monthly lease, the city center, food priced for residents | Walking out onto sand. The beach is a bridge or a Grab away |
| Son Tra | Quieter peninsula blocks, closer to nature, some coworking | The An Thuong scene, and a flat walk to everything |
| Ngu Hanh Son, off the strip | A beach-adjacent month that is not inside the cafe cluster | Stepping outside into a ready-made social scene |

### An Thuong and My Khe

My Khe is the long sandy beach on the east side of the river. An Thuong is the cafe and restaurant grid just inland from the middle of that beach, the blocks around An Thuong streets where most nomads land first. You can live without a vehicle if your days stay inside that grid: coffee, a coworking trial, the beach, dinner. Week one feels obvious here in a way Hai Chau does not.

**Trade-off:** obvious costs more. Beach-side An Thuong sits above Hai Chau for the same bedroom, and Western food is how the **$700** month leaks. Some streets are lively late even though the city's nightlife score is **3**. Ask which way the bedroom faces, and walk the block after 9pm before you pay.

Pick An Thuong for a 2-4 week landing stay while you tour monthly leases. Skip it as the default home if you already know you will eat every meal on that grid and you came for the **$500** figure.

**Housing tell:** confirm fiber in the unit, not "the building has internet." A speed test in the room beats a listing adjective. Ground-floor rooms are the ones that flood first when **Sep-Dec** arrives.

### Hai Chau

Hai Chau is the city center on the west side of the Han River: markets, offices, the denser Vietnamese city, and rents that are more likely to recognize the longer-stay band. You are choosing a local month, not a discount code printed on a beach photo. Food is better and cheaper. English gets thinner, which matches a city whose English score is **3**.

**Trade-off:** you do not wake up on the sand. The bridge is a real commute if your social life and your coworking both sit in An Thuong. Do that trip once at the hour you would do it before you sign. A Hai Chau lease plus two Grab crossings a day can erase the rent you thought you saved.

Best for people who will cook or eat locally, and who can treat the beach as a plan rather than a view. A weaker fit for someone who wants the first night to feel like a nomad arrival.

### Son Tra

Son Tra is the peninsula north of My Khe: residential blocks, the mountain, quieter evenings, and a longer ride back to An Thuong. Enosta Space publishes coworking on An Nhon 3 in Son Tra, so "quieter" does not have to mean "no desk." It does mean the scene will not come to you.

**Trade-off:** if your week is calls plus a beach walk, Son Tra works. If your week is "I will meet people by walking outside," it does not. Check the actual ride to the beach you think you are buying. The peninsula is large. A pin that looks coastal on a map can be a hillside road.

Pick Son Tra when you have already spent a few days in the city and you want sleep. Skip it as a blind first booking.

### Ngu Hanh Son, off the strip

Ngu Hanh Son is the wider district that contains An Thuong, My Khe, and the Marble Mountains, and then keeps going into local wards. ACE Coworking and Coworking Danang both publish rooms in this district, just outside or beside the cafe grid. The blocks away from An Thuong are where a monthly lease can still see the beach without paying for the brunch streets.

**Trade-off:** you have to know which block you booked. "Ngu Hanh Son" on a listing is not the same product as a room on An Thuong 4. Ask for the street, walk it, and run the speed test. Flooding in the rainy months is a street-level question, not a district-level one.

## Internet, power, and coworking for video-call work

The city average is **80 Mbps**, reliability **7/10**, power stability **7/10**, coworking density **Med**. That is a yes for ordinary video calls. It is not Chiang Mai, where the [Chiang Mai living guide](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) is working with **95 Mbps**, reliability **8**, and coworking **High**. It is a better headline than Bali, where the dataset shows **50 Mbps** and reliability **5**, and the [Bali living guide](/guides/living-in-bali) is blunt about street-by-street fiber. Power at **7** means you do not import a Cape Town load-shedding plan. Storm weeks in **Sep-Dec** can still drop a call. Keep a charged laptop and a second connection.

The 7/10 is an average. A fibered Hai Chau flat and a guesthouse Wi-Fi network on the beach are not the same product. Test the room at the hour you take calls.

### A setup that holds calls

1. **Home fiber you have tested.** Viettel, VNPT, and FPT are the ordinary apartment providers. Ask which one is already in the unit, and run a speed test from the desk. "Wi-Fi included" in a short let is not that test. Serviced apartments often bundle a line. A local monthly lease often does not. Confirm which one you signed.
2. **A local SIM with real data.** Viettel, MobiFone, and VinaPhone sell prepaid SIMs in the city and at the airport, and eSIM is widely offered. If a call would hurt you to drop, keep the second line. Learn the hotspot before the storm day you need it.
3. **One coworking room you have already used for a call.** Not a beach cafe you liked for the view. Coworking density **Med** means you have options. It does not mean a booth on every corner.

### Coworking that is actually listed

These are spaces whose own sites still publish Da Nang rooms. Hours and day-pass rules change. Believe the operator's page the week you go, not this paragraph. This guide does not freeze a dong price.

- **ACE Coworking** publishes a room at 12 Tran Van Du, Bac My An, Ngu Hanh Son, which the operator describes as just outside the heart of An Thuong. Day, week, and monthly packages are on their site.
- **Coworking Danang** publishes a room at 28 Tran Van Thanh, Khue My, Ngu Hanh Son. Their page describes day passes, monthly memberships, and call booths. Visitor hours and after-hours member access are theirs to change.
- **Enosta Space** (formerly Enouvo Space) publishes coworking at 16-18 An Nhon 3, Son Tra, with weekday hours that end in the early evening. Use it if you live on the peninsula. Do not plan an An Thuong week around a Son Tra desk.

Treat any famous name that is not on the operator's current page as closed or moved. A trial morning beats a 2022 roundup.

### Cafes

An Thuong will let you open a laptop in a lot of rooms. That is not the same as a 45-minute call. Do the call once, at the hour you actually meet, before you tell a client the cafe is your office. A tiled room with a blender and no door will lose to a booked booth every time. My Khe at sunset is a walk, not an office.

### Checklist for a rental you might keep

- Speed test in the room, upload included, at call hour
- Which provider, and whether the building's Wi-Fi is shared with other guests
- A quiet corner with a door, if you talk for a living
- Which floor, if you might still be there in **Sep-Dec**
- A named fallback (one of the spaces above, or a second SIM) written down before you need it

## Visas and stay length: 90-day e-visa, no dedicated nomad visa (verify official rules)

This is operational orientation, not legal advice. Vietnamese immigration rules move. The number on our city page is not the law. When this page and the official portal disagree, believe the portal and the stamp in your passport.

### What Nomad Spin currently shows

The Da Nang row says **E-Visa**, **90 days**, **UTC+7**. Ho Chi Minh City, Hoi An, Hanoi, and every other Vietnam row use the same **E-Visa**, **90 days** pair. One national rule, one label. Use the Da Nang field on [the city page](/destinations/da-nang): an e-visa measured in days, up to 90, not a residence card.

### What the Immigration Department publishes

Vietnam's Immigration Department describes the e-visa on its portal. The current application domains it has announced are [evisa.gov.vn](https://evisa.gov.vn/) and thithucdientu.gov.vn. The Department's own description, on the [national immigration portal](https://immigration.gov.vn/trang-chu-ttdt), says an e-visa is valid for a **maximum of 90 days**, **single or multiple entry**, for foreigners who are **outside Vietnam**, hold a valid passport, and are not barred from entry. The fee is paid online and is **not refunded** if the application is refused.

The Department's application instructions still publish the fee and the processing line in plain text: **$25** for a single-entry e-visa, **$50** for a multiple-entry e-visa, and processing in **3 working days**. Those figures are the official instructions, not a Nomad Spin price. They can change. Read them on the live portal the week you pay. Do not copy a blog's service fee and call it the government charge.

Print the approval before you fly. A screenshot on a dead phone is a bad arrival plan. Da Nang Airport has appeared on Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists of air gates that accept e-visa entry. Confirm the live port list on the portal before you book a ticket into DAD, because gates are a government list, not a travel blog.

### There is no digital nomad visa

The Immigration Department does not publish a dedicated digital nomad visa. The product on the official portal is the e-visa. A sponsored visa, for people invited or guaranteed under the law on entry, exit, transit, and residence, is a different procedure with a different sponsor. It is not a nomad visa you can buy because you work online. If a site sells you a branded nomad visa for Da Nang, check the portal before you pay.

Some passports have a separate visa-exemption route. It is often shorter than 90 days, and it is not the field on our city page. The list of nationalities and the length move. Check the current government notice for your passport. Do not skip the e-visa because a 2023 article said your country was exempt.

### Staying longer than the stamp

The same immigration portal says e-visa holders already in Vietnam **may be considered** for a new visa if an authority, organization, or individual invites or guarantees them, under the foreigners' residence law. That is not a promise, and it is not a form you file because the beach was nice. The ordinary published path for another e-visa is to apply again from **outside** Vietnam.

A border run is a trip out of the country plus a new application. It is not a same-day stamp trick, and it is not guaranteed. Agents who say they can "extend the e-visa" without you leaving, and without a real sponsor, are not the Immigration Department. Read the official note yourself: [immigration.gov.vn](https://immigration.gov.vn/trang-chu-ttdt).

### Tax, at a high level only

A stay that covers most of a year can raise tax-residence questions at home and in Vietnam. People often cite a 183-day threshold. Do not plan a year on that sentence. This guide is not tax advice. If Da Nang is going to be most of your year rather than a Feb-Jul season, pay for advice at home and, if needed, in Vietnam.

### A simple decision frame

- **The trip fits inside 90 days, and your passport can use the e-visa:** apply on the official portal, from outside Vietnam, and read the entry type (single or multiple) before you pay.
- **Your passport has a current exemption:** use that only after you have read the official notice, and do not assume it lasts 90 days.
- **You need longer than the e-visa maximum:** a sponsored route under the residence law, or a new e-visa applied for from outside. Not a nomad-visa product, because that product is not on the portal. A different country with a **365-day** visa-free field is [Living in Tbilisi](/guides/living-in-tbilisi), solo **$800**, **UTC+4**. That is a different base, not a Vietnam extension.
- **"I will just stay and sort it out":** not a plan.

The weather fields are short, and they are enough to avoid the expensive mistake. Da Nang's season is the point of the city. It is also the opposite of Chiang Mai's.

A year that puts Da Nang in Feb-Jul and Chiang Mai in Nov-Feb is the pairing the rows actually support. A year that stacks Da Nang's typhoon months on top of Chiang Mai's burning season is the pairing the rows do not support.

### What Sep-Dec actually does to a work month

Typhoon season is a listed con. In practice that means heavy rain, street flooding in low blocks, power blips that the yearly **7/10** does not describe, and apartments that grow mold if they were already damp. Ground floors and basements are the units that fail first. Outdoor calls die. A beach cafe is not a backup office in a storm week.

You can still work if you chose this window on purpose: a higher floor, fiber you tested, a coworking room that stays open, a second SIM, and a ticket you can move. You should not book a non-refundable beach month in October because February's photos were good. Oct and Nov are the heart of the rainy list. If a storm would stop your work, leave before September.

This is not a reason to sensationalize the coast. It is a reason to read `rainyMonths` before you wire rent. The pros (stunning beaches, beautiful scenery) describe Feb-Jul more honestly than they describe a flooded street in November.

### August

August is on neither list. People use it as a soft ending to a good summer. Treat it as a shoulder: fine if your lease can end, a poor month to start a non-refundable stretch into September.

## Daily life: Grab, food, Vietnamese, and the family-friendly side

### Food

"Ultra affordable" survives contact with Vietnamese food and dies in An Thuong brunch. Markets, com tam, bun, and ordinary seafood are how you eat inside **$500** to **$700**. The beach grid is how you eat like the month is a holiday. You can tell which life you are living by where lunch happens. Hai Chau and the local wards of Ngu Hanh Son make the local version easier. An Thuong makes the leak easier.

Water: drink filtered or bottled. That is normal here.

### Getting around

Grab, car or motorbike with a licensed driver, is the default. It covers the airport, the river crossing, and any night you are carrying a laptop. Walking covers An Thuong and the My Khe sand. It does not cover Hai Chau to the beach as a daily habit unless you have timed that ride.

Riding yourself is a different legal question. Circular No. 29/2015/TT-BGTVT is the Ministry of Transport rule on international driving permits: a person using a permit issued by a state that is party to the 1968 Vienna Convention has to carry that permit **and** the national licence for the category of vehicle, and follow Vietnam's road law. A rental shop that hands over keys without looking is not that rule. If your licence does not cover a motorbike, or your country does not issue a 1968-convention permit Vietnam recognizes, do not ride. Unlicensed riding is a common mistake here, not a strategy, and it is how insurance arguments go badly after a crash. This guide will not walk you through an unlicensed rental.

Da Nang International (DAD) is a city airport. Use Grab or the official taxi desk the airport currently publishes. Skip the unsolicited offer at the curb.

### Vietnamese

English proficiency on the row is **3**. Menus in An Thuong cope. Leases, clinics, immigration questions, and a lot of landlords do not. A little Vietnamese, or a patient friend, matters more for a monthly contract than for ordering lunch. The dataset's language-barrier con is this fact. The short list of trade-offs on the city page does not lead with it. You should still plan for it. The growing nomad scene does not translate the city for you.

### Community, nightlife, and the family tag

Community **5** and the "growing nomad scene" pro can both be true. You can meet people in the first week if you go back to the same coworking room. You can also spend a month in An Thuong cafes and only meet people who are leaving on Friday. This is not Chiang Mai's community **9**, and it is not Bali's. If the scene is why you travel, [read Chiang Mai](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) or [read Bali](/guides/living-in-bali) before you pretend Da Nang will become them.

Nightlife **3** means the evenings are beach, food, and an early city. Limited nightlife is a listed trade-off. Do not book Da Nang to fix a social life that wants Bangkok. [Bangkok](/destinations/bangkok) is the same time zone, nightlife **9**, and a solo month of **$1,100**.

The **family** tag matches the safety score, the beach, and a city you can cross without megacity traffic. It is not a school-seat guarantee, and this guide will not invent tuition. If you are moving with children, verify schools and clinics yourself. Safety **8.5** and female safety **8** are why a lot of people pick the city over louder beaches. They are scores, not a promise about a specific unlit road.

Private clinics in the central and beach districts handle the usual nomad problems. You are a cash patient until an insurer says otherwise. Sort that before you need the desk, using the insurance placeholder above.

[Hoi An](/destinations/hoi-an) is the close day trip and the quieter alternative: charming, smaller, safety **9.0** on its row, and not a work city (coworking **Low**, community **3**, flooding in the cons). The [Hoi An living guide](/guides/living-in-hoi-an) covers Old Town versus An Bang, and why that row is a 1-3 month base rather than a second Da Nang. [Ho Chi Minh City](/destinations/ho-chi-minh-city) is the big Vietnamese work city if Da Nang feels too small. The [Ho Chi Minh City living guide](/guides/living-in-ho-chi-minh-city) covers District 1 versus Thao Dien, and why its Dec-Apr dry season does not match this Feb-Jul beach window. [Hanoi](/destinations/hanoi) is the food-and-density version, solo **$1,050**, not a beach.

**Stay with Da Nang** when you want the **$700** beach, safety **8.5**, and you can take **Feb-Jul** plus a **90-day** e-visa field. You accept community **5**, nightlife **3**, coworking **Med**, and a real **Sep-Dec** storm season.

**Pick Chiang Mai** when you want community **9**, coworking **High**, **95 Mbps**, and you do not need a beach. The solo month is **$850**. Best months are **Nov-Feb**, the opposite window, which is why the two cities pair in one year. The visa field is a **30-day** tourism exemption. It depends on your passport, and it is tourism only. The [Chiang Mai living guide](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) explains the Destination Thailand Visa as the long-stay path, and why Mar-Apr is a hard stop there. Same clock: **UTC+7**.

**Pick Bali** when the social beach is the product and you will pay **$1,200**, with internet reliability **5** and a visa field of **30 days**. Best months **Apr-Sep** overlap Da Nang's, so Bali is a rival for the same season, not a complement. Read [Living in Bali](/guides/living-in-bali) before you assume the island is "Da Nang with more cafes." It is a different bill, a different connection, and **UTC+8**.

**Pick Hoi An** when you want the same coast, a higher safety score (**9.0**), and a smaller life. The solo month is **$900**, best months **Feb-May**, rainy **Sep-Nov**, coworking **Low**. It is a beautiful week and a thin work month. Flooding is in the cons. It is not the upgrade for someone who found Da Nang too quiet. Neighborhoods and the e-visa are in [Living in Ho Chi Minh City](/guides/living-in-ho-chi-minh-city).

**Look at Bangkok** when limited nightlife is the thing you keep circling. [Bangkok](/destinations/bangkok) is **$1,100** solo, **120 Mbps**, safety **7.8**, nightlife **9**, best **Nov-Mar**. ## First-week checklist and how to compare Da Nang on Nomad Spin

### Common mistakes

- Treating the **90-day** e-visa field as a nomad visa, or as a promise that an agent can extend it inside Vietnam
- Paying the **$25** nightly median for thirty nights and calling the month **$700**
- Choosing An Thuong, then acting surprised that the long-stay **$500** figure did not survive contact with brunch
- Using cafe Wi-Fi for the client call you cannot drop
- Booking **Sep-Dec** because a best-month photo was nearby and the typhoon con went unread
- Signing a ground-floor beach unit for October
- Riding a motorbike with no licence Vietnam recognizes
- Comparing Da Nang only with Bali's photos, and never opening Chiang Mai's opposite season

### First week

**Days 1-2**

- SIM in the phone, hotspot tested
- E-visa printed, and the last legal day written down from the approval and the entry stamp
- One coworking trial, including a real call, if the home line is still unknown
- Walk the block at night before you decide you can sleep there

**Days 3-4**

- See more than one monthly place, including one that is not on the An Thuong grid
- Run the speed test in the room at call hour, upload included
- Ride the actual river crossing once at the hour you would do it
- If the dates touch **Sep-Dec**, look at the floor, the drains, and the mold before you sign

**Days 5-7**

- Commit to one neighborhood for two to four weeks, not to "Da Nang" in the abstract
- Fix the work loop: home deep work, coworking on call days, one cafe that passed the call test
- Only then book the longer stay, the onward flight, and insurance
