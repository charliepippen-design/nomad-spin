## Is living in Ho Chi Minh City worth it for digital nomads in 2026?

A straight answer on living in Ho Chi Minh City as a digital nomad: yes in 2026 if you want Vietnam's energetic city base, street food, and a startup scene, and you can live with traffic, air, and a 90-day stamp. It is a no if you came for a quiet beach or clean air. Locals still say Saigon. The hub, and every link in this guide, is [Ho Chi Minh City](/destinations/ho-chi-minh-city). The short answers come from that row:

- **Cost:** about **$800** a month solo, about **$600** on a longer stay. A short Airbnb sits near **$30** a night, which is a different budget.
- **Internet:** usually yes for video calls. The average is **85 Mbps**, reliability **7/10**, power stability **7/10**, coworking density **High**.
- **Stay length:** the row says **E-Visa**, up to **90 days**, time zone **UTC+7**. Vietnam does not publish a dedicated digital nomad visa. Verify the current e-visa on the official portal before you treat 90 days as permission.
- **Skip it** if you need quiet, clean air, or zero paperwork. The row's cons are intense traffic, air pollution, visa complexity, extreme humidity, and noise. Traffic, air, and the visa are the go or no-go. Humidity and noise are how you judge the apartment.
- **Best months:** **Dec, Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr**. Rainy months are **Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct**. Average temperature on the row is **28°C**, a yearly figure, not a forecast.

The same page tags the city **foodie**, **workhub**, and **adventure**, on an **urban** landscape, in **Asia**, language **Vietnamese**. Safety is **7.5** (female safety **7**). Pros are extremely affordable, incredible street food, an energetic atmosphere, fast internet, and a growing startup scene.

The calendar is the decision most competitors bury under a rooftop. ## Who Ho Chi Minh City is for (and who should skip it)

Ho Chi Minh City fits people who want a real city, a bill that can land near **$800**, and food that is the point of the week. It is a poor fit if the product you want is a beach, clean air, or a year on one stamp.

**It tends to fit if you:**

- Can fund the solo baseline (about **$800**) or will actually live inside the longer-stay figure (**$600**)
- Need calls to work, and you will test the room. **85 Mbps**, reliability **7/10**, and coworking **High** are enough for most video days, with a backup SIM for rain
- Want energy. Nightlife on the row is **7**. Community size is **7**. The growing startup scene is a listed pro, not a slogan you have to invent
- Can put the serious months in **Dec-Apr**, or you have a real plan for **Jun-Oct**
- Like an **urban** food city. The vibe tags are **foodie**, **workhub**, and **adventure**. There is no beach tag
- Can work **UTC+7**. Vietnam does not shift clocks, so the overlap stays put. It is the same clock as [Chiang Mai](/destinations/chiang-mai), [Bangkok](/destinations/bangkok), and [Da Nang](/destinations/da-nang)

**Skip it, or pick another city, if you:**

- Cannot clear **$600** even on a monthly lease with local food. The **$30** nightly median will not save you
- Want a beach and a smaller scene. That is [Living in Da Nang](/guides/living-in-da-nang) (solo **$700**, safety **8.5**, best **Feb-Jul**), not this row
- Want community **9** and a cafe full of other nomads by default. That is [Living in Chiang Mai](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) or [Living in Bali](/guides/living-in-bali)
- Need clean air as a non-negotiable. Air pollution is a listed con. It is not a modest way of saying the skyline looks hazy in photos
- Cannot work with traffic and noise. Both are on the cons. A District 1 room facing a bar street fails the month even when the city average looks fine
- Need English for the lease, the clinic, and the landlord. English proficiency is **4**. Thao Dien and parts of District 1 cope. A local contract often does not
- Need a year of legal stay with no new application. The field is **90 days**, and there is no nomad visa to stretch it

`lgbtFriendly` on this row is **5**. That is weaker than Bangkok (**7**) and Chiang Mai (**6**), and a bit stronger than Da Nang (**4**). Read it before you treat "energetic" as a claim about every part of daily life. Female safety **7** is solid in this dataset. It is not a promise about an empty street at 1am, and it is not a reason to ignore bag-snatching from a passing motorbike. Keep the phone in a pocket and the laptop off the curb.

## Real monthly cost bands (solo $800, long-term $600, short Airbnb $30/night)

Nomad Spin does not publish a fake "comfortable" total for Ho Chi Minh City. It publishes three money fields. Those are the bands.

| Band | Figure | Field on the Ho Chi Minh City row | How to read it |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Longer stay | $600 / month | `financials.costLongTerm` | A local-rate month: monthly housing, local food, modest transport |
| Solo nomad baseline | $800 / month | `costUSD` (same as `costNomadSingle`) | The planning number for a solo remote worker |
| Nightly scouting | $30 / night | `financials.airbnbMedian` | What a short test stay tends to cost per night, not a monthly budget |

`rentIndex` is **14** (Da Nang **10**, Chiang Mai **15**, Bangkok **22**). That is relative rent pressure inside the dataset, not a dollar rent. "Extremely affordable" is a listed pro, and the **$800** figure is how the dataset prices the city. The row does not publish a District 1 rent or a Thao Dien rent. Anyone who quotes you a street price as if it were this row is using a different source.

### The nightly median will quietly wreck the monthly baseline

Thirty nights at the published **$30** median is **$900** in lodging before food. That is already **$100** above the entire **$800** solo month, and it is **$300** above the **$600** longer stay. If you "try the city" on nightly Airbnb for a month, you are not on either monthly band, no matter how cheap the street food is. The $600 and $800 figures assume a monthly place.

A 2-4 week landing stay in District 1 or Thao Dien is a scout. It is not the month. Use it to see leases. Do not call it the **$600** life.

### What moves you off the band

No extra dollar totals here. These are the levers, in the order they actually matter. District 1 and Thao Dien sit above quieter local districts for the same bedroom. That is how people use the city. It is not a second Nomad Spin price.

| Choice | Stays near $600 to $800 | Leaves the baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Housing | A monthly lease in District 3 or Binh Thanh, or a local building set back from the nightlife street | Thirty nights at the $30 median, or a pool condo in Thao Dien or District 1 as the default |
| Food | Com, bun, pho, and markets priced for the city | District 1 brunches, imported groceries, and "just one more" Western dinner as the meal plan |
| Work | Home fiber you have tested, plus coworking on call days | A cafe day-pass habit on top of a serviced apartment you already overpaid for |

### Reality checks

- The **$800** number is a city baseline, not a District 1 pool-condo quote.
- The growing startup scene is a listed pro. It is also how Western menus show up. The dataset cannot see your receipt.
- A cheaper room on a bar street is not cheaper if you cannot sleep or take a call. Noise is on the cons.
- Insurance is not inside those three fields. Budget it separately. This guide does not invent a premium.

## Neighborhoods that work: District 1, District 2 (Thao Dien), District 3, Binh Thanh (trade-offs)

The wrong district wastes the month. District 1, Thao Dien, District 3, and Binh Thanh are different products that share one city row. There is no separate Nomad Spin page for any of them. The canonical numbers live on [Ho Chi Minh City](/destinations/ho-chi-minh-city). The trade-offs below are how people actually use the city. Ask for the street. A district name on a listing is not a floor plan.

| Base | Best for | You give up |
|---|---|---|
| District 1 | The first week, food, walkable blocks, the densest coworking | The $600 band, quiet, and a bedroom that does not face a bar or a construction site |
| Thao Dien (District 2) | Cafes, residential streets, a calmer landing than District 1 | A walk to Ben Thanh. The river is a real commute. Still not the local price |
| District 3 | A local monthly lease next to District 1, better food prices | The Thao Dien cafe bubble, and English on every sign |
| Binh Thanh | A local month nearer the river, a better shot at the longer-stay band | Stepping outside into a ready-made nomad scene |

### District 1

District 1 is the core: Ben Thanh, Nguyen Hue, the backpacker grid, the business towers, and the easiest first week. You can live without a vehicle if your days stay inside that core: coffee, a coworking trial, dinner. Week one feels obvious here in a way Binh Thanh does not.

**Trade-off:** obvious costs more, and it is loud. Noise is a dataset con. Some streets are lively late even though you came to work. Ask which way the bedroom faces, and walk the block after 9pm before you pay. Air pollution does not stop at the district line, and a low floor on a choked street is the worst version of a citywide con.

Pick District 1 for a 2-4 week landing stay while you tour monthly leases. Skip it as the default home if you already know you will eat every meal on that grid and you came for the **$600** figure.

**Housing tell:** confirm fiber in the unit, not "the building has internet." A speed test in the room beats a listing adjective. Ground-floor rooms are the ones that flood first when **Jun-Oct** arrives.

### Thao Dien (District 2)

Thao Dien is the residential pocket east of the Saigon River that most nomads mean when they say District 2: cafes, quieter lanes than the District 1 core, and other foreigners. It is the landing if District 1 felt like a night market you have to sleep inside. The Hive publishes a room at 94 Xuan Thuy in Thao Dien, so "quieter" does not have to mean "no desk."

**Trade-off:** you are across the river from the core. Do the trip once at the hour you would actually do it before you sign. A Thao Dien lease plus two Grab crossings a day can erase the calm you thought you bought. It is also not the local price. Western food is how the **$800** month leaks, the same way An Thuong leaks a Da Nang month.

Pick Thao Dien when you want sleep and cafes and you have already seen the river crossing. Skip it as a blind way to "save money versus District 1." You might not.

### District 3

District 3 sits against District 1: markets, local food, and rents that are more likely to recognize the longer-stay band. You are choosing a local month, not a discount code printed on a skyline photo. Food is better and cheaper. English gets thinner, which matches a city whose English score is **4**. cirCO publishes rooms on Dien Bien Phu and Hai Ba Trung in District 3, so a local month can still have a call room.

**Trade-off:** you do not wake up inside the expat cafe grid. If your social life and your favorite desk both sit in Thao Dien, District 3 is a commute, not a shortcut. Walk the actual street. "District 3" on a pin can be a calm block or a noisy arterial.

Best for people who will eat locally and who can treat Thao Dien as a plan rather than a view. A weaker fit for someone who wants the first night to feel like a nomad arrival.

### Binh Thanh

Binh Thanh is the district between the river and the northeast of the city: denser local streets, some blocks that flood, and a monthly lease that is more likely to look like **$600** than a District 1 serviced apartment is. Metro Line 1 runs from Ben Thanh toward Suoi Tien through this side of the city. It is one line. It is not a reason to skip the street check.

**Trade-off:** the scene will not come to you. Community **7** is a city score, not a promise that your ward has a nomad breakfast. Ask for the street, walk it at night, and run the speed test. Flooding in **Jun-Oct** is a street-level question, not a district-level one. A ground floor on a low block is the unit that fails first.

## Internet, power, and coworking for video-call work

The city average is **85 Mbps**, reliability **7/10**, power stability **7/10**, coworking density **High**. That is a yes for ordinary video calls. It is a step under Chiang Mai, where the [Chiang Mai living guide](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) is working with **95 Mbps**, reliability **8**, and coworking **High**. It is ahead of Da Nang (**80 Mbps**, coworking **Med**) and well ahead of Bali, where the dataset shows **50 Mbps** and reliability **5**, and the [Bali living guide](/guides/living-in-bali) is blunt about street-by-street fiber. Power at **7** means you do not import a Cape Town load-shedding plan. The [Cape Town living guide](/guides/living-in-cape-town) is the one that spends real space on power at **4**. Storm weeks in **Jun-Oct** can still drop a call. Keep a charged laptop and a second connection.

The 7/10 is an average. A fibered flat and a guesthouse Wi-Fi network in District 1 are not the same product. Test the room at the hour you take calls.

### A setup that holds calls

1. **Home fiber you have tested.** Viettel, VNPT, and FPT are the ordinary apartment providers. Ask which one is already in the unit, and run a speed test from the desk. "Wi-Fi included" in a short let is not that test. Serviced apartments often bundle a line. A local monthly lease often does not. Confirm which one you signed.
2. **A local SIM with real data.** Viettel, MobiFone, and VinaPhone sell prepaid SIMs in the city and at the airport, and eSIM is widely offered. If a call would hurt you to drop, keep the second line. Learn the hotspot before the storm day you need it.
3. **One coworking room you have already used for a call.** Not a sidewalk cafe you liked for the iced coffee. Coworking density **High** means you have options. It does not mean a booth on every corner, and it does not mean the cafe is an office.

### Coworking that is actually listed

These are spaces whose own sites still publish Ho Chi Minh City rooms. Hours and day-pass rules change. Believe the operator's page the week you go, not this paragraph. This guide does not freeze a dong price.

- **The Hive District 1** publishes a room at 26 Huynh Khuong Ninh, District 1, with hot desks, dedicated desks, and private offices on its own site.
- **The Hive Thao Dien** publishes a room at 94 Xuan Thuy, Thao Dien, District 2. Use it if you live east of the river. Do not plan a District 1 week around a Thao Dien desk.
- **Dreamplex** publishes its Nguyen Trung Ngan space at 21 Nguyen Trung Ngan, District 1 (levels 9 to 11 of the Miss Ao Dai building). The operator calls it a founding coworking room in the city. Treat any other Dreamplex pin as a separate check.
- **cirCO** publishes rooms in District 1 (including 63A Nam Ky Khoi Nghia and 67 Nguyen Thi Minh Khai) and in District 3 (including 222 Dien Bien Phu and 177 Hai Ba Trung). Read which branch you are booking. The brand is not one room.

Treat any famous name that is not on the operator's current page as closed or moved. A trial morning beats a 2022 roundup.

### Cafes

District 1 and Thao Dien will let you open a laptop in a lot of rooms. That is not the same as a 45-minute call. Do the call once, at the hour you actually meet, before you tell a client the cafe is your office. A tiled room with a blender and no door will lose to a booked booth every time. Nguyen Hue at sunset is a walk, not an office.

### Checklist for a rental you might keep

- Speed test in the room, upload included, at call hour
- Which provider, and whether the building's Wi-Fi is shared with other guests
- A quiet corner with a door, if you talk for a living
- Which floor, if you might still be there in **Jun-Oct**
- Which way the bedroom faces, if noise is already on your list of cons
- A named fallback (one of the spaces above, or a second SIM) written down before you need it

## Visas and stay length: 90-day e-visa, no dedicated nomad visa, border runs (verify official rules)

This is operational orientation, not legal advice. Vietnamese immigration rules move. The number on our city page is not the law. When this page and the official portal disagree, believe the portal and the stamp in your passport.

### What Nomad Spin currently shows

The Ho Chi Minh City row says **E-Visa**, **90 days**, **UTC+7**. Da Nang's row says the same visa pair. Hoi An's row says **Tourist Visa**, **90 days**, and Hanoi's row says **Tourist Visa**, **90 days**. Same country, two labels in the dataset. Use the Ho Chi Minh City field as the product on [the city page](/destinations/ho-chi-minh-city): an e-visa measured in days, up to 90, not a residence card. Do not treat the label gap between cities as two different laws.

### What the Immigration Department publishes

Vietnam's Immigration Department describes the e-visa on its portal. The current application domains it has announced, from 08:00 on 11 November 2024 (GMT+7), are [evisa.gov.vn](https://evisa.gov.vn/) and thithucdientu.gov.vn. The Department's own description, on the [national immigration portal](https://immigration.gov.vn/trang-chu-ttdt), says an e-visa is valid for a **maximum of 90 days**, **single or multiple entry**, for foreigners who are **outside Vietnam**, hold a valid passport, and are not barred from entry under Article 21 of the law on foreigners' entry, exit, transit, and residence. The fee is paid online and is **not refunded** if the application is refused.

The Department's application instructions, on the [e-visa steps page](https://immigration.gov.vn/en_US/web/guest/khai-thi-thuc-dien-tu/cap-thi-thuc-dien-tu), still publish the fee and the processing line in plain text: **$25** for a single-entry e-visa, **$50** for a multiple-entry e-visa, and processing in **3 working days**. Those figures are the official instructions, not a Nomad Spin price. They can change. Read them on the live portal the week you pay. Do not copy a blog's service fee and call it the government charge.

Print the approval before you fly. A screenshot on a dead phone is a bad arrival plan. The Immigration Department publishes a list of ports that accept e-visa entry. Confirm Tan Son Nhat (SGN) on that live list before you book a ticket, because gates are a government list, not a travel blog.

### There is no digital nomad visa

The Immigration Department does not publish a dedicated digital nomad visa. The product on the official portal is the e-visa. A sponsored visa, for people invited or guaranteed under the law on entry, exit, transit, and residence, is a different procedure with a different sponsor. It is not a nomad visa you can buy because you work online. If a site sells you a branded nomad visa for Ho Chi Minh City, check the portal before you pay. An e-visa is also not permission to take a job with a Vietnamese employer. That is a separate work-permit file. This guide is not that process.

Some passports have a separate visa-exemption route. It is often shorter than 90 days, and it is not the field on our city page. The list of nationalities and the length move. Check the current government notice for your passport. Do not skip the e-visa because a 2023 article said your country was exempt.

### Staying longer than the stamp

The same immigration portal says e-visa holders already in Vietnam **may be considered** for a new visa if an authority, organization, or individual invites or guarantees them, under the foreigners' residence law. That is not a promise, and it is not a form you file because the street food was good. The ordinary published path for another e-visa is to apply again from **outside** Vietnam.

A border run is a trip out of the country plus a new application. It is not a same-day stamp trick, and it is not guaranteed. Agents who say they can "extend the e-visa" without you leaving, and without a real sponsor, are not the Immigration Department. Read the official note yourself: [immigration.gov.vn](https://immigration.gov.vn/trang-chu-ttdt).

### Tax, at a high level only

A stay that covers most of a year can raise tax-residence questions at home and in Vietnam. People often cite a 183-day threshold. Do not plan a year on that sentence. This guide is not tax advice. If Ho Chi Minh City is going to be most of your year rather than a Dec-Apr season, pay for advice at home and, if needed, in Vietnam.

### A simple decision frame

- **The trip fits inside 90 days, and your passport can use the e-visa:** apply on the official portal, from outside Vietnam, and read the entry type (single or multiple) before you pay.
- **Your passport has a current exemption:** use that only after you have read the official notice, and do not assume it lasts 90 days.
- **You need longer than the e-visa maximum:** a sponsored route under the residence law, or a new e-visa applied for from outside. Not a nomad-visa product, because that product is not on the portal. A different country with a **365-day** visa-free field, at the same **$800** solo baseline, is [Living in Tbilisi](/guides/living-in-tbilisi), **UTC+4**. That is a different base, not a Vietnam extension.
- **"I will just stay and sort it out":** not a plan.

## Best months (Dec-Apr) vs rainy months (Jun-Oct) and humidity reality

The weather fields are short, and they are enough to avoid the expensive mistake. The dry season is the point of choosing this city over Da Nang in the same year.

|---|---|---|
| Dec, Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr | `bestMonths` | The work season. Drier, and the months to arrive |
| Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct | `rainyMonths` | Rain, street flooding in low blocks, the humidity the cons already name |
| May and Nov | Not listed as best, and not listed as rainy | Shoulders. Often still workable. Not a promise that June will behave |
| Year-round | `tempAvgC` **28** | A coarse yearly average. It does not mean December is cool, and it does not mean October is a rooftop month |

The [season guide](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season) is the calendar version of this check. [Chiang Mai](/destinations/chiang-mai) is best **Nov, Dec, Jan, Feb**. [Bangkok](/destinations/bangkok) is best **Nov, Dec, Jan, Feb, Mar**. Ho Chi Minh City's **Dec-Apr** sits in that same dry window. [Da Nang](/destinations/da-nang) is best **Feb-Jul**, with rainy months **Sep-Dec**. The beach city's good months start as this city's dry season is ending, and Da Nang's storm months overlap the start of Ho Chi Minh City's best stretch. A year that puts Ho Chi Minh City in Dec-Apr and Da Nang in May-Jul is the Vietnam pairing the rows support. A year that stacks both cities into September is the pairing they do not support. [Hanoi](/destinations/hanoi) lists best months **Oct, Nov, Dec, Mar, Apr**, solo **$1,050**, not this **$800**. Use it as a different city on the same e-visa question, not as a cheaper Ho Chi Minh City.

### What Jun-Oct actually does to a work month

Rainy months are on the row. In practice that means heavy rain, street flooding in low blocks, power blips that the yearly **7/10** does not describe, and apartments that grow mold if they were already damp. Extreme humidity is a dataset con in every month, and the rainy list is when it stops being abstract. Ground floors and basements are the units that fail first. Outdoor calls die. A sidewalk cafe is not a backup office in a storm week.

You can still work if you chose this window on purpose: a higher floor, fiber you tested, a coworking room that stays open, a second SIM, and a ticket you can move. You should not book a non-refundable District 1 month in September because January's photos were good. If a storm week would stop your work, leave before June, or arrive in December.

This is not a reason to sensationalize the rain. It is a reason to read `rainyMonths` before you wire rent. The pros (street food, energy, fast internet) describe a Dec-Apr work month more honestly than they describe a flooded lane in August. Air pollution does not take those months off. A best month is not a clean-air month.

### May and November

May and November are on neither list. People use November as the door into the dry season and May as a soft ending. Treat both as shoulders: fine if your lease can end, a poor month to start a non-refundable stretch into the rainy list.

## Daily life: Grab vs motorbike, street food, Vietnamese basics, and the startup scene

### Food

"Extremely affordable" and "incredible street food" survive contact with com, bun, pho, and markets, and they die in a District 1 brunch habit. The **$600** to **$800** month is a Vietnamese lunch. The startup-cafe month is a different receipt. You can tell which life you are living by where lunch happens. District 3 and local Binh Thanh make the local version easier. District 1 and Thao Dien make the leak easier.

Water: drink filtered or bottled. That is normal here.

### Getting around

Grab, car or motorbike with a licensed driver, is the default. It covers the airport, the river crossing, and any night you are carrying a laptop. Walking covers a District 1 core day and a Thao Dien lane. It does not cover Thao Dien to Ben Thanh as a daily habit unless you have timed that ride.

Metro Line 1 is real. The Ho Chi Minh City People's Committee announced commercial operation of the Ben Thanh to Suoi Tien line on 22 December 2024 ([the city notice](https://tphcm.chinhphu.vn/chinh-thuc-van-hanh-metro-so-1-101241222121342133.htm)). JICA, which financed the line, describes it as **19.7 km** with **14 stations** ([JICA's note](https://www.jica.go.jp/english/information/press/2024/20241225_21.html)). It is one line from the District 1 core toward the northeast, not a network that replaces Grab. Confirm your pin is actually on it before you call it a commute. Hours and fares are the operator's to change.

Riding yourself is a different legal question. Circular No. 29/2015/TT-BGTVT is the Ministry of Transport rule on international driving permits: a person using a permit issued by a state that is party to the 1968 Vienna Convention has to carry that permit **and** the national licence for the category of vehicle, and follow Vietnam's road law. A rental shop that hands over keys without looking is not that rule. If your licence does not cover a motorbike, or your country does not issue a 1968-convention permit Vietnam recognizes, do not ride. Unlicensed riding is a common mistake here, not a strategy, and it is how insurance arguments go badly after a crash. This guide will not walk you through an unlicensed rental. Traffic is already a listed con. Adding an unlicensed bike does not fix it.

Tan Son Nhat (SGN) is a city airport with serious traffic on the way in. Use Grab or the official taxi desk the airport currently publishes. Skip the unsolicited offer at the curb. Build the ride into the first meeting of the day. "Twenty minutes" is not a promise at 8am.

### Vietnamese

English proficiency on the row is **4**. Menus in District 1 and Thao Dien cope. Leases, clinics, immigration questions, and a lot of landlords do not. A little Vietnamese, or a patient friend, matters more for a monthly contract than for ordering lunch. The growing startup scene does not translate the city for you.

### Safety, without the crime-tour version

Safety **7.5** and female safety **7** are why a lot of people pick the city and then stay careful. The ordinary pattern nomads actually hit is a phone or a bag taken from the curb by someone on a motorbike, plus the usual big-city sense after midnight. Keep the bag on the building side of the pavement, put the phone away before you step off a curb, and take Grab when you are carrying a laptop at night. This is not a reason to write a thriller about the city. It is a reason not to walk District 1 at 1am performing "I just arrived."

Private clinics in District 1 and Thao Dien handle the usual nomad problems. You are a cash patient until an insurer says otherwise. Sort that before you need the desk, using the insurance placeholder above.

### Startup energy and the community score

Community **7** and the "growing startup scene" pro can both be true. You can meet people in the first week if you go back to the same coworking room. You can also spend a month in Thao Dien cafes and only meet people who are leaving on Friday. This is not Chiang Mai's community **9**, and it is not Bali's. Nightlife **7** is a real evening city, not Da Nang's **3**, and not Bangkok's **9**. If the scene is why you travel, read [Living in Chiang Mai](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) or [Living in Bangkok](/guides/living-in-bangkok) before you pretend this row will become them. If you want the beach and you can take Feb-Jul, read [Living in Da Nang](/guides/living-in-da-nang).

[Hoi An](/destinations/hoi-an) is the charming, smaller coast: safety **9.0**, solo **$900**, coworking **Low**, community **3**, flooding in the cons. It is a beautiful week and a thin work month. It is not the upgrade for someone who found Ho Chi Minh City too loud.

**Pick Chiang Mai** when you want community **9**, **95 Mbps**, and you do not need this city's noise. The solo month is **$850**. **Look at Bali** when the social beach is the product and you will pay **$1,200**, with internet reliability **5** and a visa field of **30 days** (visa on arrival). Best months **Apr-Sep** do not match this Dec-Apr window, so Bali is a different season, not a winter substitute. Read [Living in Bali](/guides/living-in-bali) before you assume the island is "Saigon with a beach." It is a different bill, a different connection, and **UTC+8**.

**Look at Tbilisi** when the constraint is stay length, not a city. [Living in Tbilisi](/guides/living-in-tbilisi) is the year-long contrast at the same solo baseline: **Visa Free**, **365** days, **$800**, **UTC+4**. It is not a way around the Vietnam e-visa.

**Look outside Asia** when you want another coast and you have a different budget. [Living in Cape Town](/guides/living-in-cape-town) is the non-Asia contrast: solo **$1,400**, safety **5.5**, power stability **4**, best months **Oct-Mar**. Different ocean, different risk list.

## First-week checklist and how to compare Ho Chi Minh City on Nomad Spin

### Common mistakes

- Treating the **90-day** e-visa field as a nomad visa, or as a promise that an agent can extend it inside Vietnam
- Paying the **$30** nightly median for thirty nights and calling the month **$800**
- Choosing District 1 or Thao Dien, then acting surprised that the long-stay **$600** figure did not survive contact with brunch
- Using cafe Wi-Fi for the client call you cannot drop
- Booking **Jun-Oct** because a best-month photo was nearby and the rainy list went unread
- Signing a ground-floor unit on a low street for September
- Riding a motorbike with no licence Vietnam recognizes
- Comparing Ho Chi Minh City only with Da Nang's beach photos, and never putting Dec-Apr next to Feb-Jul
- Linking a Saigon, HCMC, or ho-chi-minh shortcut URL. The destination is [Ho Chi Minh City](/destinations/ho-chi-minh-city)

### First week

**Days 1-2**

- SIM in the phone, hotspot tested
- E-visa printed, and the last legal day written down from the approval and the entry stamp
- One coworking trial, including a real call, if the home line is still unknown
- Walk the block at night before you decide you can sleep there

**Days 3-4**

- See more than one monthly place, including one that is not in District 1 or Thao Dien
- Run the speed test in the room at call hour, upload included
- Ride the actual river crossing once at the hour you would do it, if one of the places is across the river
- If the dates touch **Jun-Oct**, look at the floor, the drains, and the mold before you sign

**Days 5-7**

- Commit to one neighborhood for two to four weeks, not to "Saigon" in the abstract
- Fix the work loop: home deep work, coworking on call days, one cafe that passed the call test
- Only then book the longer stay, the onward flight, and insurance
