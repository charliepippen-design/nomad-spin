## Is living in Kotor worth it for digital nomads in 2026?

The case for living in Kotor as a digital nomad is a setting decision first and a work decision second. You get a walled old town at the head of a fjord-like bay, mountains straight up behind the roofs, and one of the safest scores in the Balkans, in exchange for a small town, thin nomad infrastructure, and cruise-ship days in season. The short answers come from the [Kotor](/destinations/kotor) row, not from a view from the fortress.

**How much does it cost to live in Kotor as a digital nomad?** Nomad Spin lists about **$1,250** a month for a solo nomad. The long-term **$938** and the median Airbnb around **$57** a night are estimates on this row. Old Town short lets and July or August dates push you above the baseline.

**Is Kotor internet good enough for video calls?** Usually yes from a tested flat, with a backup. Nomad Spin shows about **70 Mbps** average. Reliability **7/10** and power **9/10** are estimates. Coworking density is Low. Test upload speed in the actual unit before signing, and keep a second SIM as the fallback.

**What visa do digital nomads use in Kotor?** Up to 90 days in any 180 for visa-free passports, counted from first entry; Montenegro is not in Schengen. For a longer remote-work stay, Montenegro issues a temporary residence permit for digital nomads of up to two years. Verify your passport on the Government of Montenegro site before you book.

**Who should skip living in Kotor?** Skip it if you need a nomad community, a coworking campus, or a lively evening scene. Nomad Spin flags very touristy, small, and expensive as the trade-offs. Community size is **2**, coworking is **Low**, and nightlife is **4**, so most weeks are quiet and self-built.

**What are the best months to live in Kotor?** Nomad Spin's friendliest months are **May, Jun, Jul, Aug, and Sep**. Rainy months to plan around are **Nov and Dec**. Average temperature in the dataset is **15°C**, a yearly figure and not a forecast. Cruise calls crowd the Old Town on many of those good-weather days.

**Which neighborhood should I pick in Kotor?** The Old Town is the postcard but loud, steep, and short-let priced. Dobrota is the long residential strip along the bay to the north. Prcanj is a quieter village across the bay. Skaljari is practical, behind the walls near the bus station, and Muo sits just across the water.

**What time zone is Kotor in?** Nomad Spin lists **UTC+1**. Montenegro moves its clocks to **UTC+2** from the last Sunday of March to the last Sunday of October, like most of Europe. That keeps Kotor on the same clock as Split, Dubrovnik, Tirana, and Budapest, and one hour ahead of London.

Worth it, with conditions. Safety is **9** (female safety **8**), the strongest number on the row. English proficiency is **5**, community is **2**, nightlife is **4**, and `lgbtFriendly` is **4**. The city is tagged **adventure** and **beach**, on a **seaside** landscape, in **Europe**, language **Montenegrin**. The row's own pros are the Bay of Kotor, UNESCO, and stunning. Its cons are very touristy, small, and expensive. All three cons are true at the same time as the pros.

The honest comparison is with [Split](/destinations/split), which costs **$550** more a month (**$1,800**), has faster internet and Med coworking, and sits inside Schengen. Kotor's case against Split is the bill, the bay, and a stay that does not eat your Schengen days. If budget, internet, and safety are not locked yet, start with [How to Choose Your Next Nomad Base](/guides/how-to-choose-next-nomad-base). If the open question is the month, use [Where to Go Next by Season](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season). The shortlist that still has a Europe line is [Best Places for Digital Nomads in 2026](/guides/best-places-digital-nomads-2025). Use that list to find candidates. Use this page, and the live destination row, before you book.

## Who Kotor is for (and who should skip it)

Kotor fits people who want a very safe, very beautiful small town on the Adriatic, a monthly bill near **$1,250**, and a work routine they can run alone. It is a poor fit if the month depends on other nomads, on a desk you can walk into, or on a quiet July in the Old Town.

**It tends to fit if you:**

- Can fund the solo baseline (about **$1,250**) or will actually live inside the longer-stay estimate (**$938**)
- Do mostly async work, or calls from a flat you have tested. **70 Mbps** holds ordinary video. Reliability **7/10** is an estimate, and coworking is **Low**
- Want safety **9** and female safety **8**, and are happy with nightlife **4** and community **2**
- Like hiking, swimming off a concrete platform, and a town you can learn in a week
- Can put the serious months in **May, Jun, or Sep**, and treat July and August as cruise-and-crowd months you planned for
- Can work **UTC+1**, or **UTC+2** in summer. UK calls sit an hour behind. US East Coast calls land in the Kotor afternoon and evening

**Skip it, or pick another city, if you:**

- Cannot clear the **$938** longer-stay estimate on a monthly lease with market food. The **$57** nightly estimate will not save you. [Tirana](/destinations/tirana) and [Podgorica](/destinations/podgorica) both sit at **$1,150** solo
- Need a nomad campus. Community here is **2**. [Living in Chiang Mai](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) (community **9**) or [Living in Lisbon](/guides/living-in-lisbon) are the campus answers
- Need coworking **High** and a booth for every call. Kotor is **Low**. [Living in Budapest](/guides/living-in-budapest) and [Living in Prague](/guides/living-in-prague) are **High** at **200 Mbps**
- Need a night out most nights. Nightlife is **4**. [Budva](/destinations/budva), half an hour down the coast, scores **7** and is tagged party
- Need more than **90 days in any 180** and are not ready to file a residence application. The default is a visa-free stay, and the digital nomad permit is a separate process with an in-person filing. The visa section lays it out
- Hate stairs. The Old Town and the slopes behind it are stone steps and steep lanes, and the most-photographed walk in town is a climb

`lgbtFriendly` on this row is **4**. That is above Budva, Podgorica, and Tirana (all **3**) and below Split and Dubrovnik (**5**). Read it before you treat safety **9** as a claim about every part of daily life. Female safety **8** is strong in this dataset. It is not a promise about an empty bay road at 2am. Walk the route home at night before you sign.

If you are still sorting those filters, the decision order is [how to choose a base](/guides/how-to-choose-next-nomad-base), not another photo of the bay.

## Neighborhoods that work: Old Town, Dobrota, Prcanj, Skaljari, and Muo

Kotor is small, but it is long. The town wraps the inner corner of the bay, and the difference between a flat walk along the water and a climb with groceries decides the month. There are no separate Nomad Spin destination pages for these areas, so the canonical numbers live on [Kotor](/destinations/kotor). The trade-offs below are how people actually use the town.

| Base | Best for | You give up |
|---|---|---|
| Old Town | A first few nights, walking everywhere, the atmosphere | Quiet days in season, the $938 estimate, and a car anywhere near the door |
| Dobrota | A residential month on the water, a flat promenade into town | A short walk to everything. Some addresses are a real walk or a bus |
| Prcanj | A quiet village month across the bay, swimming at the door | Walking into town. You live on the bus or a bike |
| Skaljari | Supermarkets, the bus station, everyday errands, local leases | Bay views from most flats, and a pretty street |
| Muo | A small waterfront village a short trip from the Old Town | Shops at the door. It is a place to sleep and swim |

### Old Town

The walled Old Town is part of the [Natural and Culturo-Historical Region of Kotor](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/125), a UNESCO World Heritage site. Inside the walls it is car-free stone lanes, small squares, churches, cats, and cafes, with the fortress walls climbing the mountain behind it. Week one feels obvious here.

**Trade-off:** obvious costs more, and the cruise days are not a rumor. When ships are in, the lanes fill from mid-morning, and "very touristy" is the row's first con. The stone carries sound, upper floors mean stairs and no lift, and you carry everything in. Thick walls are also bad for Wi-Fi from the next room.

Pick the Old Town for a few landing nights while you tour monthly leases. Skip it as the default home if you came for the **$938** estimate, for calls, or for sleep in July.

**Housing tell:** confirm the internet in the unit, not "the building has internet." A speed test at the desk beats a listing adjective.

### Dobrota

Dobrota is the long residential strip along the bay north of the Old Town: old stone captains' houses, newer apartment blocks, swimming platforms, and a waterfront path that is the flattest walk in Kotor. It is where a lot of longer-stay people end up, because it is still Kotor and it is not the Old Town.

**Trade-off:** "Dobrota" covers a few kilometres. The end near the Old Town is a short walk. The far end is a bus ride or a long walk, and the bay road is narrow and busy in summer. Ask for the street and the distance to the Old Town gate, not the district. Check whether the flat faces the water or the road.

Pick Dobrota for a month of work, a swim before a call, and an evening walk into town. Skip the far end if you will not use the bus.

### Prcanj

Prcanj is a village on the opposite shore of the inner bay, on the coastal road toward Tivat. It is quieter, greener, and lower-key than anything on the Old Town side, with stone houses on the water and a big church above the village.

**Trade-off:** you are not walking into Kotor every day. The local bus runs along the bay road between Dobrota and Stoliv through Prcanj, and summer traffic can make it slow and late. A night out becomes a trip. Groceries are a plan, not an errand. Check the timetable on the operator's page the week you go, and ride it once before you sign.

Best for people who will work at home, swim every day, and treat the Old Town as a weekly visit. A weaker fit if the month has to feel social.

### Skaljari

Skaljari is the residential district behind and south of the Old Town, toward the bus station and the bigger supermarkets. It is the practical answer: local leases, errands on foot, and a short walk to the Old Town gates.

**Trade-off:** it is not pretty, and it is uphill in places. You give up the bay view for convenience, and some blocks sit on the road out of town. Check the street at rush hour and the stairs with groceries.

Pick Skaljari if the month is about routine and price. Skip it if the view was the reason you came.

### Muo

Muo is the small waterfront settlement just across the water from the Old Town, the first village on the road toward Prcanj. It gives you a quieter base with the Old Town a short trip away.

**Trade-off:** there is not much at the door except the water. It is a place to sleep, swim, and work, not a neighborhood with errands. Price it against Dobrota before you assume quiet is cheaper.

### Booking order

After you pick the neighborhood, these three slots are the booking order. The live stay, flight, and insurance options are on the [Kotor destination page](/destinations/kotor).

| Slot | What to book |
|---|---|

## Internet, power, and coworking for video-call work (70 Mbps, reliability 7 estimate, power 9 estimate, Low coworking)

The town average is **70 Mbps**. Reliability **7/10** and power stability **9/10** are estimates, filled from a formula on this row. Coworking density is **Low**. That is a yes for ordinary video calls from a tested flat and a poor default for a call-heavy job that needs a shared desk. It matches [Budva](/destinations/budva) (**70 Mbps**), sits below [Podgorica](/destinations/podgorica) (**80 Mbps**) and [Tirana](/destinations/tirana) (**90 Mbps**), and well below [Split](/destinations/split) (**100 Mbps**) and [Dubrovnik](/destinations/dubrovnik) (**120 Mbps**).

The power estimate of **9** is a European-row default, not a measured grid score. Treat it as a reason not to panic, not as a promise. Old stone houses can have old wiring, and a summer storm can still drop a line.

The reliability **7/10** is an estimate and an average. A newer Dobrota block with a fresh line and a holiday let inside the Old Town walls are not the same connection. Test the room at the hour you take calls.

### A setup that holds calls

1. **Home internet you have tested.** Ask which provider is already in the unit, and run a speed test from the desk, upload included, at the hour you take calls. "Wi-Fi included" in a short let is often a shared router. A local monthly lease may not include a line at all. Ask which of those you are buying.
2. **A local SIM or eSIM with real data.** Montenegro's mobile operators sell prepaid plans in town and at the airport. Compare the current price on the operator's own page the day you buy, and learn the hotspot before the day you need it.
3. **One place to work that you have already used for a call.** Not a cafe you liked for the view. Coworking density **Low** means the list is short and changes.

### Coworking that is actually listed

Coworking is **Low** on the row, and in a town this size that can mean one room, a seasonal pop-up, or nothing open in the month you arrive. Spaces in a cruise town open, move, and close, and a roundup written for another year will send you to a locked door.

Do this instead. Search for coworking in Kotor the week you plan to go, then read the operator's own page for the address, the opening hours, the day pass, and the rules on calls. Message the space and ask whether it is open in your month. Book one trial morning and take a real call from it. Treat any name you cannot find on a current operator page as closed or moved. [Budva](/destinations/budva) and [Podgorica](/destinations/podgorica) are bigger towns and the next places to look if Kotor has nothing open, but both are **Low** on their rows too.

Cafes along the bay will let you open a laptop. That is a quiet afternoon, not a booth with a door. If the work depends on a room full of other freelancers every day, [Split](/destinations/split) (**Med**) or [Budapest](/destinations/budapest) (**High**) is the honest answer.

### Checklist for a rental you might keep

- Speed test in the room, upload included, at call hour
- Which provider, and whether the connection is shared with other units
- A quiet corner with a door, if you talk for a living
- Which way the windows face, if the street is the bay road or an Old Town square
- Heating that you have switched on if the stay touches **Nov-Dec**, and cooling if it touches July or August
- A named fallback (a second SIM, a trial-tested desk) written down before you need it

## Visas and stay length: visa-free 90 days in 180 by default, then the digital nomad residence permit (verify official rules)

The default entry is visa-free for listed nationalities, for **90 days within 180** counted from the date of first entry, per the Government of Montenegro's page on the [stay of up to 90 days](https://www.gov.me/en/article/stay-of-up-to-90-days). Montenegro's temporary residence permit for digital nomads comes second, and it is an application, not a stamp. This is operational orientation, not legal advice. Montenegrin rules move, and your passport decides which rule applies. When this page and the official source disagree, believe the source and the stamp in your passport.

### Montenegro is not in Schengen

Montenegro is not a member of the Schengen area, and it is not in the EU, although it uses the euro. Days in Montenegro do not count toward a Schengen 90/180 allowance, and Schengen days do not count toward Montenegro's, as the UK government's [Montenegro entry requirements](https://www.gov.uk/foreign-travel-advice/montenegro/entry-requirements) page puts it. That is the practical difference from [Split](/destinations/split) or [Dubrovnik](/destinations/dubrovnik), which sit under Croatia's Schengen rules. A weekend in Dubrovnik is a Schengen entry and counts there. Do not paste Croatia's rules onto Kotor, or Kotor's onto Croatia.

### What the official sources publish

The Government of Montenegro's [stay of up to 90 days](https://www.gov.me/en/article/stay-of-up-to-90-days) page says a foreigner is entitled to a stay of up to 90 days on a short-stay (C) visa or without a visa, that the stay is **90 days during a period of 180 days** counted from the date of first entry, and that a person who has used the 90 days may re-enter after the 180-day period has run out. The government's [visas and visa regime](https://gov.me/en/article/visas-and-visa-regime) page says the visa regime is set by the Decree on visa regime, and that a short-stay visa may not exceed 90 days in a period of six months.

Whether your passport is on the visa-free list, and for how many days, is on the government's [diplomatic missions and visa regimes](https://www.gov.me/en/diplomatic-missions) pages, country by country. Some nationalities get a shorter visa-free period than the default, and some need a visa before travel. Read the entry for your own passport before you buy a non-refundable month.

Two practical rules sit next to the day count:

- **Register your stay within 24 hours.** The Police Directorate's page on [registration of residence for foreign nationals](https://gov.me/en/article/registration-and-cancellation-of-residence-for-foreign-nationals-who-do-not-use-the-services-of-the-accommodation-provider) who do not use an accommodation provider says registration is filed within 24 hours of arrival, at the police or the local tourist organisation, and again if you move to another place for more than 24 hours. The UK government page says hotels and some holiday lets usually do it for you, and that in private accommodation it is on you. That government page is marked as archived, so confirm the current procedure at the tourist organisation office. Ask your landlord, in writing, who registers you
- **Passport validity.** The UK government page says a British passport must have been issued less than 10 years before entry and be valid for at least 3 months after the day you plan to leave. Check the rule for your own passport on the official page

### The temporary residence permit for digital nomads

The government's [Digital Nomads Montenegro portal](https://digitalnomads.gov.me/article/legal-status-for-nomads) describes a **temporary residence permit for a digital nomad**, for a foreigner who is employed or works electronically for a foreign company, or for their own company, that is **not registered in Montenegro**. The portal lists the legal basis as the Law on Foreigners ("Official Gazette of Montenegro" nos. 12/18, 3/19 and 86/22) and a separate Rulebook on the permit. The law has been amended since, so read the current text before you rely on any one detail.

| Point | What the portal says |
|---|---|
| Length | The permit is issued for up to **two years** |
| Extension | It can be extended for up to **two more years**. The extension request is filed in person, no later than **30 days** before the current permit expires |
| Reapplying | A new permit can be issued **six months** after the previous temporary residence expired |
| Conditions | Proof of financial means of support, accommodation, health insurance, a travel document valid at least three months longer than the permit, no prison sentence of more than six months in the country of origin, and an employment contract or other document proving the work is done electronically |
| Where to apply | In person, at the Ministry of the Interior in the place where you live. Photo, fingerprints, and signature are taken at the counter |
| Decision | Within **40 days** of a proper application |
| Family | Spouses and minor children can join through family reunification |

The portal does not print an income figure or a fee. Do not trust a euro threshold, a fee, or a tax rate quoted on a relocation blog. Ask the Ministry of the Interior office where you will live what proof of means it accepts, and what the current fee is, before you pay anyone for advice.

Two practical consequences follow from the wording. The filing is in person at the office for your place of residence, so the usual sequence is to enter visa-free, sign a lease, register, and then apply. And a visa-free stay that runs out while the file is open is a risk you carry, so ask the office how your stay is treated while the application is pending, and build in time.

### Tax, at a high level only

A long stay can raise tax-residence questions at home and in Montenegro. The government's 2021 [programme for attracting digital nomads](https://www.gov.me/en/article/programme-for-attracting-digital-nomads-in-montenegro-adopted) said tax relief for nomads still had to be defined, and the permit page above says nothing about tax. This is not tax advice. If Kotor will be most of your year rather than a summer season, pay for advice at home and in Montenegro before a permit, a lease, and a bank account accumulate.

### A simple decision frame

- **The trip fits inside 90 days, and your passport is visa-free:** enter, register within 24 hours, and write the last legal day down.
- **Your passport needs a visa, or gets fewer days:** read the government's page for your country first, and plan around that number.
- **You want longer, and you work for a company outside Montenegro:** read the digital nomad portal, line up insurance, the lease, the work contract, and the criminal-record document, and file in person at the Ministry of the Interior where you live.
- **You would be working for Montenegrin clients or a Montenegrin employer:** the nomad permit is not built for it. Ask the Ministry.
- **"I will just stay and sort it out":** not a plan.

A different base with a **365**-day visa-free stay for many passports is [Living in Tbilisi](/guides/living-in-tbilisi), solo **$800**, **UTC+4**. That is a different country, not a Montenegrin extension. For Albania's version of the 90-day default and a one-year permit, see [Living in Tirana](/guides/living-in-tirana).

The weather fields are short, and they are enough to avoid the expensive mistake.

The [season guide](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season) is the calendar version of this check. Kotor's best-month list matches [Split](/destinations/split) (**May-Sep**). [Budva](/destinations/budva) is **Jun-Sep**. [Dubrovnik](/destinations/dubrovnik), [Podgorica](/destinations/podgorica), and [Tirana](/destinations/tirana) list **Apr-Jun** and **Sep-Oct** instead.

### Cruise days

Cruise ships dock at the quay outside the Old Town walls or anchor in the bay and tender passengers in, and on a busy day the Old Town fills from mid-morning to late afternoon. Calls are heaviest in the summer and run well into autumn, so October is not automatically quiet. That is the "very touristy" con in practice.

The fix is simple. Check the Port of Kotor's published cruise schedule for your dates before you book, do Old Town errands early or late on ship days, and live outside the walls. Dobrota, Prcanj, and Muo barely notice a ship day. The Old Town notices nothing else.

### July and August

July and August are on the best-month list and also the months where every con on the row is loudest: crowded lanes, slow traffic on the bay road, buses running late, and short-let prices that undercut the monthly market. If you have a choice, treat **May, June, and September** as the working season and July and August as a decision you make on purpose, with a lease outside the walls and a booked desk or a second SIM.

### Rain and winter

November and December are the rainy months on the row, and the mountains around the bay make the rain serious when it comes. A winter month can be a good heads-down stretch if you came for quiet, a calmer town, and a lower bill, and you did not come for beaches or a social calendar. Plan the practical layer: heating that works, a flat that is not damp, and a lease that does not assume summer prices. Some restaurants and short lets close for the season.

## Daily life: steep streets, buses, airports, Montenegrin, and the bay

### Steep streets and walking

The Old Town is flat inside the walls and steep everywhere behind them. The fortress walk above the town is a long stair climb, best done early before the heat and the crowds. Dobrota's waterfront is the flat walk. Skaljari and the slopes behind the Old Town are stairs and short, steep lanes. Before you sign, carry a full bag of groceries from the nearest supermarket to the door once.

### Buses, taxis, and the bay road

The local bus runs along the bay between Dobrota, the Old Town, and the Prcanj side toward Stoliv, and longer-distance buses leave from the Kotor bus station for Budva, Tivat, Podgorica, and further. Check the current timetable and fare on the operator's own page, and expect summer delays: the bay road is one road, and it fills with traffic in July and August. Taxis are the tool for the airport, a late night, and a suitcase. Agree the price first or use a licensed company.

A car is a weekend tool, not a daily one. Parking near the Old Town is scarce and the bay road is slow in season.

### Airports

Tivat Airport (TIV) is the closest, a short drive from Kotor. Taxis are the simple way in. Podgorica Airport (TGD), Montenegro's other international airport, is a much longer road transfer, and it is worth checking when Tivat has no route in your month. Dubrovnik Airport (DBV) across the Croatian border is a third option, but it means a border crossing, and the days on the Croatian side count toward Schengen. Check routes, transfers, and border times the week you travel.

### The bay, the water, and weekends

The pros on the row are the Bay of Kotor, UNESCO, and stunning, and that is the weekend plan: a swim off a bay platform, a boat to the small islands off Perast, a drive up the serpentine road toward Lovcen, a day on the open coast at [Budva](/destinations/budva). The bay water is calmer than the open sea, and the beaches are mostly pebble or concrete platforms, not sand. "Beach" on the vibe tags means swimming, not a long sand strip.

### Food and money

Supermarkets, the market by the Old Town walls, bakeries, and cooking at home are how you stay near the **$938** estimate. Old Town terraces are how a month becomes a holiday. Seafood priced by weight deserves a question about the price before you order. Montenegro uses the euro. Check your own bank's fees for ATM withdrawals and card payments, and carry some cash for small places.

### Montenegrin and English

The dataset language is **Montenegrin**, and English proficiency is **5**. The tourism trade copes in English. Landlords, utility offices, banks, and government counters may not. A translation app is not optional, and for a lease, a residence application, or a clinic, bring a friend who speaks the language or pay for a translator.

### Safety and health

Safety on the row is **9**, female safety **8**, `lgbtFriendly` **4**. Those are dataset scores, not a street-by-street map. The ordinary risks here are the road, the stairs, and the heat, not crime: watch the narrow bay road on foot after dark, carry water on the fortress climb, and take summer midday heat seriously. Sort insurance before you need a clinic, and ask how a private clinic bills a visitor before you rely on a policy.

## First-week checklist and how to compare Kotor on Nomad Spin

### Common mistakes

- Assuming a visa-free entry means a year. The default is 90 days in any 180, counted from first entry
- Forgetting the 24-hour registration in a private flat
- Paying the **$57** nightly estimate for thirty nights and calling the month **$1,250**
- Booking an Old Town flat for July and expecting quiet days
- Signing a lease without a speed test, a heating answer, and a walk home with groceries
- Using cafe Wi-Fi for the client call you cannot drop, in a town whose coworking score is **Low**
- Counting a Dubrovnik weekend as a Montenegro day. It is a Schengen day
- Waiting until the last two weeks of your 90 days to find out how the digital nomad permit works

### First week

**Days 1-2**

- SIM or eSIM in the phone, hotspot tested
- Registration done, by the host or at the tourist organisation, and the receipt kept
- Passport validity checked, and the last legal day written down from the entry stamp
- The Port of Kotor cruise schedule checked for the rest of the month

**Days 3-4**

- See more than one monthly place: one in Dobrota, one in Skaljari or across the bay
- Run the speed test in the room at call hour, upload included
- Switch on the heating or the cooling the listing claims
- Ride the bus once along the bay at the hour you would use it

**Days 5-7**

- Commit to one area for two to four weeks, not to "Kotor" in the abstract
- Fix the work loop: home deep work, a tested fallback for call days, a second connection
- If you need longer than 90 days, read the digital nomad portal and ask the Ministry of the Interior office what it needs, before the allowance is nearly spent
- Only then book the longer stay, the onward flight, and insurance

### Booking order

These slots are last, after the week above, not before the flight is emotional. Book them from the [Kotor destination page](/destinations/kotor).

| Slot | What to book |
|---|---|
| Stays | The monthly place that passed the speed test, the noise test, the stairs test, and the heating or cooling test |
| Flights | The onward or return date your stay actually supports, into or out of TIV |
| Insurance | Travel and health cover you have read, including any gap before a permit |
