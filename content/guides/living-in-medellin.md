## Is living in Medellin still worth it for digital nomads in 2026?

A straight answer on living in Medellin as a digital nomad: still a rational yes in 2026, with a short list of hard stops. The row on [Medellín](/destinations/medellin) is a spring-climate LATAM base on a clock that overlaps the US East Coast: solo month **$1,100**, longer stay **$800**, nightly Airbnb median **$45**, internet **80 Mbps** with reliability **7/10**, power stability **7**, coworking density **High**, safety **6.5**. The same page tags it `party`, `mountain`, and `adventure`. Landscape is mountain. Language is Spanish. Region is LATAM. Best months are **Jan, Feb, Mar, Jul, Aug, and Dec**. Rainy months are **Apr, May, Oct, and Nov**. The visa field is **Visa Exemption**, up to **90** days. Time zone is **UTC-5**. Annual average temperature is **22°C**.

The pros on that row are why people book: low cost of living, vibrant nightlife, friendly locals, and a growing tech scene. The cons are why a casual booking goes wrong: safety concerns in some areas, altitude adjustment, language barrier, bureaucratic banking, and air quality issues. "Low cost of living" means the **$1,100** baseline is real. It does not mean a Provenza apartment and a Poblado brunch habit are inside that number.

Worth it if you want that mix and you will live inside it: a neighborhood you chose on purpose, basic Spanish, and a stay length you have checked against your passport. A bad default if you need a top-tier safety score, if you will not learn Spanish, or if air quality is a hard filter. The rest of this guide is how to read the row before you pay a deposit.

If budget, internet, and safety are not locked yet, start with [How to Choose Your Next Nomad Base](/guides/how-to-choose-next-nomad-base). If the open question is the month, use [Where to Go Next by Season](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season). The shortlist that still puts Medellín in the conversation is [Best Places for Digital Nomads in 2026](/guides/best-places-digital-nomads-2025). Use that list to find candidates. Use this page, and the live destination row, before you book.

## Who Medellin is for (and who should skip it)

Skip Medellín if you need a high safety score without neighborhood discipline. Overall safety is **6.5** and female safety is **5**. Also skip it if you will not learn basic Spanish. Altitude adjustment, bureaucratic banking, and air quality issues are listed cons. A higher-safety Europe hub is the better fit when that floor is non-negotiable.

Medellín fits people who want a warm mountain city, a real nightlife score, and hours that line up with the United States, and who will treat Spanish and neighborhood choice as part of the job.

**It tends to fit if you:**

- Want the dataset's solo baseline (about **$1,100**) or the longer-stay figure (**$800**), and you will actually live like that
- Need **UTC-5**. Colombia stays on that offset all year. A 9:00 call in US Eastern is 9:00 here in the winter months, and 8:00 here when the US is on daylight time. A 9:00 call in London is early afternoon or mid-afternoon, depending on UK summer time
- Want nightlife at **9** and a large scene (community **8**) in a mountain city, with coworking density **High** when the home line fails
- Can put the trip inside **Jan, Feb, Mar, Jul, Aug, or Dec**, or you have a real plan for the rainy months **Apr, May, Oct, and Nov**
- Can work with safety **6.5** and female safety **5**, which means the block you sleep on and the way you get home at night are part of the plan
- Will learn enough Spanish to handle a lease, a clinic form, and a government email. English proficiency on the row is **4**

**Skip it, or pick a different city, if you:**

- Need a safety floor above **6.5**, or a female-safety floor above **5**. [Chiang Mai](/destinations/chiang-mai) is **8.2** with female safety **8**. [Lisbon](/destinations/lisbon) is **8.8** with female safety **8**. Read [Living in Chiang Mai](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) and [Living in Lisbon](/guides/living-in-lisbon) if that floor is the decision
- Need the ocean. This row is mountain and party, and the [Bali living guide](/guides/living-in-bali) is the beach comparison, with a different internet and visa story
- Need the **$800** figure while living in Provenza and eating every meal in El Poblado
- Need Europe-morning deep work and will not take calls in the afternoon. **UTC-5** is a US clock
- Need a full year of legal stay and will not assemble a Cancillería file. The destination row says visa exemption, **90** days. Longer routes exist, and they are a real application
- Need a high LGBTQ+ score as a hard filter above this row. `lgbtFriendly` here is **6** (Lisbon's is **9**). Overall safety **6.5** does not answer that question

If you are still sorting those filters, the decision order is [how to choose a base](/guides/how-to-choose-next-nomad-base), not another neighborhood roundup.

## Real monthly cost bands (solo, long-term, short Airbnb)

Nomad Spin lists about **$1,100** a month solo and about **$800** long-term, with a median Airbnb around **$45** a night. Laureles and Envigado are how people stay near those bands. El Poblado and Provenza are where the same month becomes a tourist receipt. The low cost is real when you skip the most tourist-priced blocks.

Those three money fields are the bands. Nomad Spin does not publish a fake "comfortable" total on top of them. Do not replace them with a blogger's spreadsheet.

| Band | Figure | Field on the Medellín row | How to read it |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Longer stay | $800 / month | `financials.costLongTerm` | A local-rate month: monthly housing, local food, modest transport |
| Solo nomad baseline | $1,100 / month | `costUSD` (same as `costNomadSingle`) | The planning number for a solo remote worker |
| Nightly scouting | $45 / night | `financials.airbnbMedian` | What a short test stay tends to cost per night, separate from a monthly budget |

`rentIndex` is **22** (Buenos Aires **18**, Chiang Mai **15**, Mexico City **28**, Lisbon **55**). That is relative rent pressure inside the dataset, separate from a dollar rent. Medellín sits above Buenos Aires and Chiang Mai, under Mexico City, and far under Lisbon.

### The nightly median will quietly beat the monthly baseline

Thirty nights at the published **$45** median is **$1,350** in lodging before food. That is already above the entire **$1,100** solo month. If you "try the city" on nightly Airbnb for a month, you are off both published bands, however cheap the menu del día is. The **$800** and **$1,100** figures assume a monthly place.

### What moves you off the band

No extra dollar totals here. These are the levers, in the order they actually matter.

| Choice | Stays near $800 to $1,100 | Leaves the baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Housing | A monthly rental in Laureles, Envigado, or a simpler block of El Poblado away from the bars | Nightly Airbnb at the $45 median, or a Provenza apartment as the default |
| Food | Markets, menu del día, and neighborhood restaurants most days | El Poblado cafes as the lunch plan |
| Work | Home fiber, plus coworking on call days | All-day cafe hopping, plus a premium desk you do not use |
| Transport | Walking inside one neighborhood, metro for a planned hop, a registered ride app at night | Ride apps as the way you cross the valley twice a day |
| Calendar | One neighborhood, **Jan-Mar**, **Jul-Aug**, or **Dec** | A non-refundable lease through **Apr-May** or **Oct-Nov** that you did not mean to book |

Annual average temperature on the row is **22°C**. That is a coarse yearly figure. It does not tell you April is dry, and it does not tell you the air is clean.

### Reality checks

- The **$1,100** number is a city baseline. Provenza and the most photographed blocks of El Poblado are where that number goes to die if every meal is a cafe meal.
- The "low cost of living" pro is real next to [Lisbon](/destinations/lisbon) at **$2,200**. It is also the receipt that leaves the band when the landing month never becomes a lease.
- A cheaper room on a loud bar street, or on a block you will not walk at night, is a productivity and safety tax. The dataset cannot see your street.
- Insurance is outside those three money fields. Budget it separately. This guide does not invent a premium.

## Neighborhoods that work: El Poblado, Laureles, Envigado, Provenza

The valley is small enough to cross and different enough by area that the wrong pin wastes the month. There are no separate Nomad Spin destination pages for these places. The canonical numbers live on [Medellín](/destinations/medellin). The trade-offs below are how people actually use the city. This guide does not invent a dollar rent for each neighborhood.

| Base | Best for | You give up |
|---|---|---|
| El Poblado | Week-one landing, cafes, fiber apartments, the metro at Estación Poblado | The $800 band, quiet nights if you land near the bars, a local feel |
| Provenza | Short social landing inside El Poblado, restaurants, being in the middle of nightlife **9** | Sleep, the long-stay figure, a work apartment |
| Laureles | A monthly lease, local food, a street grid you can learn | Cafe density and English on every corner |
| Envigado | A quieter month still on the metro, longer stays | Spontaneous nomad plans, "I'll walk to the Poblado scene" |

### El Poblado

El Poblado is the default "I want the scene without trying" base, in the south of the city, in the hills above the river. Cafes, apartments, gyms, and coworking cluster here. Estación Poblado on Metro Line A is the practical landmark. If you arrive alone and want a social calendar in the first week, this is the easiest place in the valley to get one.

**Trade-off:** you pay for that in rent and in food. El Poblado is where the **$1,100** baseline goes to die if you treat every meal as a cafe meal. The hills also matter. A pin that looks close to the park on a map can be a steep walk with a laptop. Ask where the building sits relative to Estación Poblado before you call it walkable.

Pick El Poblado if you will use the scene for the first two to four weeks, then decide whether to stay. Skip it as a default lease if you came to write and you already know cafes wreck your day.

**Housing tell:** ask which street the bedroom faces, and whether you can hear Parque Lleras on a Friday. A cheap apartment on a bar street is a productivity tax.

### Provenza

Provenza is the social pocket inside El Poblado, around Parque Lleras and the restaurant streets people mean when they say the neighborhood "goes out." It is the fastest way to feel the nightlife score of **9**. It is a landing zone for a short stay, a dinner plan, and a reason to keep the first week social.

**Trade-off:** it is a weak place to sleep if your calls start early, and a weak place to hunt the **$800** figure. Noise, tourist pricing, and a street that is busiest when you want the laptop closed are the product. You can eat in Provenza from a quieter Poblado block, or from Laureles. You do not have to sleep on the bar street.

Best for a first long weekend inside a longer landing stay. A poor default for the month you meant to work.

### Laureles

Laureles, west of the river, is the compromise long-stayers keep recommending, and they are usually right. It is a residential grid, more local, often kinder to the **$800** to **$1,100** bands, with everyday restaurants that are not performing for a visitor. Estadio station on Metro Line B is the rail landmark on that side of the city. Many blocks are a ride or a walk from the station, so "Laureles" is not one doorstep metro product.

You will not step out of your door into a street of laptop cafes. You will eat better for less, and you will sleep. English at **4** shows up here faster than it does in El Poblado. If your work is meeting-heavy, identify the coworking room or the fiber plan you will actually use on call days before you sign anything.

Best for a second month, or for a first month if you already know you do not need the Poblado scene to feel oriented.

### Envigado

Envigado is the next municipality south, not a comuna of Medellín, and that is the point. It is calmer, more residential, and still on Metro Line A at Estación Envigado. Families and longer stays use it when they want the valley without living inside the nomad loop. The **$800** band is more plausible here than in Provenza, if the lease is monthly and lunch is local.

**Trade-off:** spontaneous plans in El Poblado become a metro ride or a car. The scene does not come to you. Test the internet in the unit. A pretty house with a weak upload is still a bad office.

Best for people who have already spent a few days in the city, or who will book a short test stay and only then sign a month.

## Internet, power, and coworking for video-call work

Fiber apartments and coworking in Medellín are generally enough for video calls. The row shows about **80 Mbps**, reliability **7/10**, power **7/10**, and coworking density **High**. That average is the city, so confirm the upload in the unit and keep a mobile backup for rainy-season outages.

Power is also a different product from Cape Town. [Living in Cape Town](/guides/living-in-cape-town) has to plan around `powerGridStability` **4**. Medellín's power score is **7**. Rain can still drop a line. Keep a hotspot. You do not need a personal inverter strategy to have a work week here. Bali's row, by comparison, shows **50 Mbps** and reliability **5**, which is why the [Bali living guide](/guides/living-in-bali) is blunt about street-by-street fiber.

### A setup that holds calls

1. **Home line you have tested.** Ask whether fiber from Tigo, Claro, or Movistar is already in the unit, and run a speed test from the room at the hour you take calls. A hallway network, or a screenshot from the lobby, is not the line you will use.
2. **A local SIM with real data.** Claro, Movistar, and Tigo are the practical prepaid networks (airport or a mall). If a call would hurt you to drop, keep a second SIM. Learn the hotspot before the day you need it.
3. **One coworking room you have already used for a call.** A cafe you liked for breakfast is a different test.

### Coworking that is actually listed

Coworking density **High** means a fallback room exists. It does not mean every name in a 2024 roundup is still open, and it does not mean a blog's day-pass price is this month's price. This guide does not copy those prices.

**Quokka** is a current El Poblado operator. Its own site lists two rooms: Astorga, at Calle 8 #43c 29, steps from Parque El Poblado and the metro station, and Patio Bonito, at Transversal 5a #45 91. The same page says you can book a flexible desk by the hour, and that Patio Bonito has soundproof booths for calls. The peso rates are on that page. Read them the week you go. A rate copied into a guide goes stale.

Treat other famous names (Selina, Tinkko, WeWork, Atom House, and the rest of the old roundups) as unconfirmed until you see them on the operator's own site this month. Spaces in this city open, rebrand, and disappear. A trial morning beats a recommendation.

### Cafes

El Poblado will let you open a laptop in a lot of rooms. That is a different product from a 45-minute call. Do the call once, at the hour you actually meet, before you tell a client the cafe is your office. Provenza in the evening will lose to a booth every time. Laureles is quieter and thinner on laptop cafes, which is a reason to have the coworking room named before you need it.

### Checklist for a rental you might keep

- Speed test in the room, upload included, at call hour
- Which provider, and whether anyone else on the booking can knock the line over
- A quiet corner with a door, if you talk for a living
- A named fallback (a confirmed coworking room, or a second SIM) written down before you need it

## Visas and stay length: 90-day exemption vs longer digital nomad routes

Nomad Spin lists **Visa Exemption** for up to **90** days, on **UTC-5**. Migración Colombia can extend a visa-free stay toward **180** days in the same calendar year, and Cancillería publishes a Visa V route for longer remote work. Income conditions change. Verify both sites for your passport before month four.

This is operational orientation, not legal advice. The number on our city page and the number on the government page answer different questions. When they disagree, the stamp and the official page win.

### What Nomad Spin currently shows

The Medellín row says **Visa Exemption**, **90 days**. That is what you will see on [/destinations/medellin](/destinations/medellin). Use it as the product field. Your passport may not be in the exempt group, and the officer can write fewer days than the maximum.

### What Migración Colombia publishes for a visa-free stay

The [Permiso de Ingreso y Permanencia (PIP)](https://portal.migracioncolombia.gov.co/tramites-y-servicios/tramites-generales/permiso-de-ingreso-y-permanencia-pip) is the permission Migración Colombia grants at the border to foreigners whose nationality does not need a visa. It is for a short stay, without the intention to settle and without local paid work. The published maximum is **90** days from entry. There is no separate application before you fly for the PIP itself. The officer assigns it if you qualify. Read the number on the permission before you leave the desk. That number is the stay you have.

A longer visa-free stay, where it exists, is a different procedure. The [Permiso Temporal de Permanencia (PTP)](https://www.migracioncolombia.gov.co/tramites-y-servicios/tramites-generales/prorroga/permiso-temporal-de-permanencia-ptp-/-prorroga), also called a prórroga, is for people who already hold a PIP and whose nationality does not require a visa. Migración Colombia says the PTP cannot exceed **180** calendar days, continuous or not, inside the same calendar year, and that you must still have a valid PIP when you apply. For a tourism PIP, the same page lists an extension of up to **90** days. Apply on the Formulario Único de Trámites at least **5** business days before the PIP expires. Filing is not approval. The page publishes a 2026 tariff and a table of payment exceptions, including some nationalities at zero. Read that table for your passport. Check it for the current amount before you apply.

Cancillería publishes its own note on the digital-nomad visa at the pages linked below. Check those pages for the current stay length before you apply. The **90**-day and **180**-day figures above are Migración Colombia's, from the PIP and PTP pages.

[Check-Mig](https://www.migracioncolombia.gov.co/tramites-y-servicios/aplicativos/checkmig) is Migración Colombia's free pre-registration. The current page says it is not mandatory. It lets you preload the flight, the reason for travel, and where you will stay, from 72 hours before departure until one hour before. Use the official app at [apps.migracioncolombia.gov.co/pre-registro](https://apps.migracioncolombia.gov.co/pre-registro/es). Do not pay a random site to file it.

Which passports need a visa before they fly is Cancillería's list, not this guide's. Start at the [visa ABC](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/atencion-y-servicio-al-ciudadano/tramites-y-servicios/visa/abece-de-visas) and the resolution it points to. This page will not copy the country list, because it can change.

### Visa V for digital nomads

The longer remote-work route is the **Visa V Nómadas digitales**, published by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs at [cancilleria.gov.co/node/26950](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/node/26950). The page describes it as permission to do remote work or telework from Colombia, through digital means, exclusively for foreign companies, as a contractor or an employee, or to start a digital or information-technology venture of interest to the country. Check that page for the current length before you apply. Beneficiaries of the main applicant can be requested. The visa does not allow paid work for a person or a company domiciled in Colombia.

The specific requirements on that page, as checked for this guide, include:

- A passport from a country or territory that is exempt from a short-stay visa, under the resolution they cite
- A letter in Spanish or English from the foreign company or companies, stating the type of link and the type of pay, plus the contract if you have one, or proof you are a partner and that the work is remote
- For founders, a letter explaining the venture and the financial and human resources it has or expects
- Bank statements for the income test on that page, set in current monthly legal minimum wages. Check that page for the current amount before you apply.
- A health policy covering Colombia for accident, illness, maternity, disability, hospitalization, death, or repatriation, for the time you plan to stay

That income line is the Cancillería page's own condition, written in Colombian minimum wages. The wage moves. This guide does not convert it into dollars, and it does not invent a second threshold. Copy the checklist the week you apply. A forum screenshot of a dollar figure is how people build a file the ministry will not accept.

Apply online through the channel that page describes, and only while your status in Colombia is regular if you apply from inside the country. Consulates can ask for more. Discretion is theirs.

### Tax, at the level this guide will go

A stay that runs through much of the year can raise tax-residence questions, including the 183-day idea you will see quoted. This guide does not tell you whether it applies to you. Ask a tax professional where you are tax resident today and, if needed, in Colombia. [Paraguay tax residency for remote workers](/guides/paraguay-tax-residency-remote-workers) is a different country and a different system. It is not a Colombia plan.

### A simple decision frame

- **A few weeks to the days on your PIP, and your passport qualifies:** the visa exemption, after you read the stamp.
- **Longer than that PIP, still inside the same calendar year, and you qualify:** the PTP, filed before the PIP expires, with the 180-day cap read first.
- **Many months, foreign clients, and you can meet the file:** Visa V, using the live Cancillería checklist, including the health policy.
- **A year via border runs, or local clients on a tourist stamp:** not a plan.

## Best months, rainy season, altitude, and air quality

The friendliest months on the Medellín row are **Jan, Feb, Mar, Jul, Aug, and Dec**. Rainy months are **Apr, May, Oct, and Nov**. The annual average is **22°C**. The row does not list a year-round weather pro. Rain weeks still need an indoor plan for calls.

| Window | What the Medellín row says | Practical read |
|---|---|---|
| Jan, Feb, Mar | `bestMonths` | One of the two friendlier windows. Higher demand for the good apartments |
| Jul, Aug | `bestMonths` | The other friendlier window, between the two rainy stretches |
| Dec | `bestMonths` | On the friendlier list. Holiday demand is a housing problem, separate from the weather field |
| Apr, May | `rainyMonths` | First rainy window. Fine if you chose it. A bad surprise on a lease you meant to be dry |
| Oct, Nov | `rainyMonths` | Second rainy window. Same rule |

"Eternal spring" is the sentence people repeat. The row is more specific than that sentence. Two rainy pairs are listed on the row, so the weather is not a year-round promise. The [season guide](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season) is the calendar version of that check. Use `rainyMonths` as a veto when you care about afternoon call plans, not as a vibe to talk yourself out of.

### Altitude and air

Altitude adjustment is a listed con. The city sits in a high valley, and El Poblado adds hills on top of that. The first days can mean getting winded on stairs and sleeping worse than you expected. Keep the first workday short. If a doctor has already told you to be careful with altitude, ask that doctor before a non-refundable month. This guide does not diagnose anyone. Mexico City's row lists "altitude sickness" as its own con. Medellín's wording is milder. Both cons are real, and neither one disappears in a best month.

Air quality issues are also a listed con, including in **Jan, Feb, Mar, Jul, Aug, and Dec**. The valley holds pollution. On a bad day the outdoor walk is the thing you skip, and the call happens indoors. A best-month headline does not cancel that con.

## Daily life: nightlife, metro, Spanish, banking bureaucracy, community

### Nightlife

Nightlife **9** is a real product, and Provenza is where a lot of it concentrates. The "vibrant nightlife" pro and the sleep you need for a call schedule are the same map. Eat there when you want the scene. Sleep on a quieter block if the laptop opens at 8. Laureles has evenings without that volume. Community **8** shows up as recurring coworking, language exchanges, football, and the same cafe twice, not as a single official nomad office. You can meet people in the first week if you go back to the same room. You can also spend a month in Poblado cafes and only meet people who are leaving on Friday.

### Metro

Metro de Medellín is useful, and it is not a door-to-door system for every pin. Line A runs north-south through the valley: Estación Poblado for El Poblado, Estación Envigado for Envigado. Line B branches west: Estadio is the station on the Laureles side, and many Laureles blocks are still a walk or a short ride from it. Provenza sits uphill from Estación Poblado. The metro is a good daytime hop between those points. It is a weak plan after midnight. Night transport is a registered ride app, which is also how you keep the safety score honest.

### Spanish

The language barrier is a listed con, and English proficiency **4** is the number under it. Service English in parts of El Poblado will get you a coffee. A lease, a clinic intake, a bank form, and an email from Migración are Spanish. Basic Spanish is a feature of a good month here, not a footnote you add if you have time. Friendly locals, which is a listed pro, are much easier to actually meet once you can hold a short conversation.

### Banking

Bureaucratic banking is a listed con. A tourist PIP is a thin file for a Colombian account. Many people run the month on foreign cards. A local account usually waits on a visa and a cédula. Sort the card you will actually use for rent before you arrive. This guide does not name a bank product or a fee, because those rules move and the dataset does not publish them.

### Safety and healthcare

Safety **6.5**, female safety **5**, and `lgbtFriendly` **6** are the scores to plan with. The practical version, without a crime essay: pick the area before the apartment, walk the block in daylight before you sign, keep the laptop with you, meet new people in a public place you already know, and use a registered ride app after dark. Dating-app meetings belong in that public place, not in an apartment you have not seen. A lower score is a set of habits. It is also a reason to choose [Lisbon](/guides/living-in-lisbon) or [Chiang Mai](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) if you wanted a city where those habits are optional.

Private clinics in El Poblado and Laureles handle the usual nomad problems. You are a cash patient until an insurer says otherwise. Sort that before you need the desk, using the insurance placeholder above.

### Common mistakes

- Paying the **$45** nightly median for thirty nights and calling the month **$1,100**
- Choosing Provenza or the busiest part of El Poblado, then acting surprised that the long-stay **$800** figure did not survive contact with cafes

- Copying a dollar income figure for Visa V instead of the Cancillería checklist, which states the test in minimum wages
- Taking paid work from a Colombian client on a PIP or on Visa V
- Using cafe Wi-Fi for the client call you cannot drop
- Booking **Apr, May, Oct, or Nov** because "eternal spring" was the headline and `rainyMonths` went unread
- Treating a best month as a clean-air month
- Using [Paraguay](/guides/paraguay-tax-residency-remote-workers) as if it answered a Colombia tax question

### First week

**Days 1-2**

- SIM in the phone, hotspot tested
- Check-Mig already done on the official app if you want the faster interview, not in a paid third-party tab
- One confirmed coworking trial, including a real call
- Walk the block in daylight and again at night before you decide you can sleep there
- Keep the first call load light while you see how the altitude feels

**Days 3-4**

- See more than one monthly place, in at least two areas. Run the speed test in the room
- Write down the date on your PIP, and whether a PTP or a Visa V is even relevant

**Days 5-7**

- Commit to one area for two to four weeks, not to "Medellín" in the abstract
- Fix the work loop: home deep work, coworking on call days, one cafe that passed the call test
- Only then book the longer stay, the onward flight, and insurance
