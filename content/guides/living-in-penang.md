## Is living in Penang worth it for digital nomads in 2026?

A straight answer on living in Penang as a digital nomad: yes if you want the best food bill in the region, a walkable heritage core, and the sea within a short ride, for about $800 a month. It is a no if you need a big nomad scene, nightlife after midnight, or a city you can cross without a car. The short answers come from the [Penang](/destinations/penang) row, not from a mural photo. A small, humid, multicultural island with a slow pace and a serious hawker culture. That is the decision.

- **Cost:** about **$800** a month solo. The longer stay is about **$600** (an estimate). A short Airbnb sits near **$30** a night, which is a different budget.
- **Internet:** good enough with a backup. The average is **80 Mbps**. Reliability is **7/10** and power stability is **8/10**. Coworking density is **Med**.
- **Stay length:** up to **90** days visa-free for many passports, as Malaysia sets 14 to 90 days by nationality and your entry stamp sets yours. For a longer stay, the DE Rantau Nomad Pass runs 3 to 12 months, renewable for 12 more. It is an application, not an arrival stamp. Time zone is **UTC+8**.
- **Skip it** if you need a nightlife city, a large community, or a car-free life outside George Town. The row's trade-offs are hot and humid, limited nightlife, car-dependent, monsoon season, and a smaller city. Community size is **4**. Nightlife is **4**.
- **Best months:** **Dec, Jan, Feb, Jun, Jul**. Rainy months are **Sep, Oct, Nov**. Average temperature on the row is **28°C**, a yearly figure, not a forecast.

The same page tags Penang **foodie**, **beach**, and **family**, on a **seaside** landscape, in **Asia**, language **Malay**. Safety is **8.0** (female safety **7**). English proficiency is **8**, the same as [Kuala Lumpur](/destinations/kuala-lumpur) and well above [Chiang Mai](/destinations/chiang-mai) at **5**. Pros are the best food in SE Asia, very affordable, multicultural, beach access, and a relaxed pace.

Worth it as a quieter Malaysian base that costs **$200** less than Kuala Lumpur and keeps the same English, the same clock, and the same entry rules. It is not the fastest connection in the region, and it is not a party island. ## Who Penang is for (and who should skip it)

Penang fits people who want daily life to be cheap, tasty, and easy in English, and who are happy with a slower island instead of a capital. It is a poor fit if you need a nomad scene that finds you, a late night out, or a commute you can do without Grab.

**It tends to fit if you:**

- Can fund the solo baseline (about **$800**) or will actually live inside the longer-stay estimate (**$600**)
- Eat out daily and want range. The **foodie** tag and "best food in SE Asia" are on the row
- Want the sea within a short ride. The row is **seaside**, and beach access is a listed pro
- Need English for the lease, the clinic, and the landlord. English proficiency on this row is **8**

- Can work **UTC+8**. Malaysia does not move its clocks. Europe's morning calls land in your afternoon and evening. US East Coast calls land late. The clock matches [Kuala Lumpur](/destinations/kuala-lumpur), [Bali](/destinations/bali), and [Singapore](/destinations/singapore)

- Need a bigger social scene by default. Community on this row is **4**. That is [Living in Chiang Mai](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) (community **9**) or [Living in Bali](/guides/living-in-bali) (community **9**)

## Neighborhoods that work: George Town, Pulau Tikus / Gurney, Tanjung Tokong / Tanjung Bungah, Batu Ferringhi, Bayan Lepas (trade-offs)

Penang is an island state with a mainland half. Almost every nomad lives on the island, which is where George Town, the north-coast beaches, and the airport sit. Seberang Perai on the mainland, around Butterworth, is a different commute and a different lease market, so treat it as a deliberate choice. The canonical numbers live on [Penang](/destinations/penang). The trade-offs below are how people actually use the island.

| Base | Best for | You give up |
|---|---|---|
| George Town (heritage core) | Walking to food, cafes, and the bus hub, week one without a ride | Quiet weekends, modern buildings, and a pool |
| Pulau Tikus / Gurney | Supermarkets, condos, the Gurney Drive food strip, a calmer routine | Shophouse charm and the cheapest leases |
| Tanjung Tokong / Tanjung Bungah | Condos near the sea between Gurney and the beaches, 3+ month stays | Walkability. You will use Grab for most errands |
| Batu Ferringhi | The beach on the north coast | Coworking, a short commute, and a local neighborhood feel |
| Bayan Lepas | The airport, the industrial zone, cheaper newer blocks | Everything that makes Penang worth visiting, unless you commute |

### George Town (heritage core)

George Town is the reason most people come. With Melaka, it was inscribed by UNESCO in 2008 as "Melaka and George Town, Historic Cities of the Straits of Malacca" ([whc.unesco.org](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1223)). Shophouses, clan jetties, temples, mosques, and churches sit inside a few blocks, and the food is a short walk in every direction. Armenian Street, Chulia Street, and Love Lane are the names you will hear first.

**Trade-off:** old buildings mean thin walls, damp, and lines that vary from unit to unit. Weekend tourist traffic is real on the famous streets. Walk the block after 9pm and ask which way the bedroom faces. The streets just outside the core, around Penang Road and Macalister Road, keep the food and lose some of the noise.

Pick George Town for a walkable first month. Skip a heritage shophouse as the default home if you take calls all day and have not tested its line.

**Housing tell:** confirm fiber in the unit, not "the building has Wi-Fi." Ask for a speed test from the desk, not a listing adjective.

### Pulau Tikus / Gurney

Pulau Tikus is the residential district just northwest of the core, with a wet market, older houses, and coffee shops. Gurney Drive is the seafront road beyond it, lined with condos, two malls, and the Gurney Drive hawker centre. It is the comfortable middle: close enough to George Town by a short ride, modern enough for a pool and a supermarket.

**Trade-off:** it costs more than the old core for the same space, and the condo towers are what push a month off the **$800** line. Common Ground's Penang site is in Pulau Tikus, which makes this the easiest area for a desk within a short ride.

Pick Pulau Tikus or Gurney if you want quiet evenings and modern buildings, and you can live with a Grab ride into the heritage streets.

### Tanjung Tokong / Tanjung Bungah

Past Gurney, the coast road runs through Tanjung Tokong (home to the Straits Quay marina and mall in Seri Tanjung Pinang) and Tanjung Bungah before it reaches the beaches. This is condo country: sea views, pools, gyms, and longer leases.

**Trade-off:** you are a ride from nearly everything, and this is where "car-dependent" bites. Community is a pool and a lobby, not a cafe full of other nomads. The **family** tag on the row fits it best.

Best for couples, families, and people staying 3+ months who want quiet. A weaker fit for a first month alone.

### Batu Ferringhi

Batu Ferringhi is the resort beach on the north coast, with hotels, a night market, and sand within a walk. It is the place the "beach access" pro points to.

**Trade-off:** coworking is thin here, and the ride into George Town is long at rush hour. Pick it for a slow, async month, not for a call-heavy job.

### Bayan Lepas

Bayan Lepas, in the southeast of the island, holds Penang International Airport (PEN) and the Bayan Lepas Free Industrial Zone, the electronics and manufacturing corridor that much of the local economy runs on. Newer blocks are cheaper and more practical.

**Trade-off:** it is a work district, not a neighborhood you visit for its streets. Pick it if you fly constantly or work with a company in the zone. Regus lists a serviced-office centre there.

## Internet, power, and coworking for video-call work (80 Mbps, reliability 7, power 8, Med coworking)

The island average is **80 Mbps**. Reliability is **7/10** and power stability is **8/10**. Coworking density is **Med**. That is a yes for most remote work with a backup plan, and a "test first" for call-heavy work. It matches [Da Nang](/destinations/da-nang) (**80 Mbps**, reliability **7**) and trails [Kuala Lumpur](/destinations/kuala-lumpur) (**100 Mbps**, **8**, **8**) and [Chiang Mai](/destinations/chiang-mai) (**95 Mbps**, **8**, **8**). Bali averages **50 Mbps** with reliability **5**, which is why the [Bali living guide](/guides/living-in-bali) spends so much time on street-by-street fiber. Penang's power stability of **8** means you do not plan around load shedding the way you would in [Cape Town](/guides/living-in-cape-town), where it is **4**.

The reliability **7/10** is an average. A new condo with its own fiber and a heritage shophouse sharing an old line are not the same product. Test the room at the hour you take calls.

### A setup that holds calls

1. **Home fiber you have tested.** Ask which provider is already in the unit and run a speed test from the desk. Unifi, Maxis, and CelcomDigi are the usual home providers. A serviced stay often bundles a line, and a local monthly lease often does not. Confirm which one you signed.
2. **A local SIM with real data.** Maxis (and its prepaid brand Hotlink), CelcomDigi, and U Mobile sell prepaid SIMs. Keep the second line for a call that would hurt to drop, and learn the hotspot before you need it. Check the current price on the operator's page the day you buy.
3. **One coworking room you have already used for a call.** Not a cafe you liked for the light. Med density means a short list, so use a trial day to pick.

### Coworking that is actually listed

These operators list Penang sites on their own pages. Read each current location page before you go, because spaces open, move, and close, and desks are theirs to price.

- **Common Ground** lists two Penang sites: one at Moulmein Rise in Pulau Tikus, and The Hive Seafront ([commonground.work](https://www.commonground.work/my-en/locations/penang/)).
- **INFINITY8 Reserve George Town** is in the former Standard Chartered building on Beach Street, inside the heritage core ([infinity8.com.my](https://www.infinity8.com.my/)).
- **Spaces** lists a George Town coworking page ([spacesworks.com](https://www.spacesworks.com/en/my/pulau-pinang/george-town/coworking)), and **Regus** lists several Penang centres, including around Gurney and Bayan Lepas ([regus.com](https://www.regus.com/en/my/pulau-pinang)).

Hin Bus Depot in George Town is an arts space with cafes, studios, and weekend markets. It is a good afternoon, not a desk. The **Penang Digital Library** on Green Lane is a public, free-to-enter library run by the state. Check its current hours and membership rules before you plan a workday there.

Treat any famous name that is not on an operator's current page as closed or moved. A trial morning beats a three-year-old roundup.

### Cafes

George Town cafes are air-conditioned and laptop-friendly, and the best ones fill on weekends. That is not the same as a 45-minute call. A shophouse cafe with tiled floors, a grinder, and a lunch rush loses to a booked booth every time. Do the call once, at the hour you actually meet, before you tell a client the cafe is your office.

### Checklist for a rental you might keep

- Speed test in the room, upload included, at call hour
- Which provider, and whether the line is shared with the building
- A quiet corner with a door, if you talk for a living
- How the air conditioning is billed
- A named fallback (a coworking desk or a second SIM) written down before you need it

## Visas and stay length: up to 90 days visa-free by passport, then the DE Rantau Nomad Pass (verify official rules)

Many passports enter Malaysia visa-free for up to **90** days. Malaysia's portal for non-citizens gives the short-term Social Visit Pass as 14 to 90 days based on nationality ([rai.malaysia.gov.my](https://rai.malaysia.gov.my/enter)), and the stamp at the counter sets your days. For a longer remote-work stay, the DE Rantau Nomad Pass runs 3 to 12 months, renewable for 12 more, so 24 months at most.

This is operational orientation, not legal advice. Malaysian immigration rules move. The number on our city page is not the law. When this page and the official portal disagree, believe the portal and the stamp in your passport.

### What the DE Rantau pass is

MDEC describes the DE Rantau Nomad Pass as a type of Professional Visit Pass. It is a pass with a term, not a residence permit, and not a visa you collect at the airport. It covers any state in Peninsular Malaysia, so Penang falls under the same entry rules and the same pass as [Kuala Lumpur](/guides/living-in-kuala-lumpur).

### What MDEC publishes

The pass is applied for online at [mdec.my/derantau](https://mdec.my/derantau). The terms below come from MDEC's [DE Rantau Pass FAQ](https://mdec.my/api/media/file/DE-Rantau_Pass_FAQ_v10.pdf), Version 10.0 dated 28 August 2026. Read the live document the week you apply, because the FAQ says programme requirements can change.

- **Duration:** the Professional Visit Pass is valid for **3 to 12 months**, with the option to renew for an additional 12 months, for a maximum stay of **24 months** in total.
- **Income test:** a minimum annual income of **USD 24,000** for tech and digital professions, and **USD 60,000** for the listed non-tech professions. The FAQ names the professions in each group.
- **Who qualifies:** a remote worker employed by a foreign-based company that is not registered in Malaysia, or a digital freelancer or independent contractor. Documents include contracts that started at least three months before you apply, three months of payslips or invoices, and three months of bank statements. Applications and documents must be in English.
- **Where you can stay:** the pass covers any state in Peninsular Malaysia and the Federal Territory of Labuan. Penang is covered. Sabah and Sarawak are entered on a tourist pass.
- **Processing:** the FAQ says six to eight weeks from a complete submission. Approval is not guaranteed, and from 1 August 2026 the FAQ says rejected applications cannot be appealed.
- **Fee:** a non-refundable processing fee of **RM1,080** per applicant and **RM540** per dependent, including 8% SST, regardless of outcome. Separate immigration pass fees (the FAQ lists **RM90** for three months or **RM360** for one year) are paid at endorsement.
- **Family:** a spouse or partner, children, and parents of the main holder can apply as dependants. The FAQ says a spouse cannot work on the pass.
- **Passport:** it must be valid for more than 14 months when you submit.

Those figures are the official FAQ, not a Nomad Spin price. They can change. Read the live document before you pay, and do not copy a service fee from an agent and call it the government charge.

### The steps after approval

The FAQ describes a second stage called endorsement.

1. The approval letter is valid for **six months** and cannot be extended.
2. You enter Malaysia using the visa or entry permission the approval letter states. If your nationality needs a Visa with Reference (VDR), get it before you travel.
3. On that first entry, use a normal immigration counter, not the autogate, so you get an entry stamp.
4. Complete endorsement within **one month** of that entry, and stay in Malaysia until the e-Pass is issued.
5. The documents include a valid health insurance policy covering Malaysia and an income tax e-registration slip from the Inland Revenue Board (LHDN), registered at [mytax.hasil.gov.my](https://mytax.hasil.gov.my/).

Two rules matter if you are already in the country. The FAQ says a tourist pass cannot be converted into a DE Rantau pass. And if your application is approved while you are in Malaysia, you must exit and re-enter on the approval. Plan the border crossing before you plan the lease. The same FAQ says most Malaysian banks do not accept the Professional Visit Pass for opening an account, so keep your home banking working.

### Entry without the pass

Most people will first arrive without a pass. The Immigration Department of Malaysia publishes a [visa requirement by country](https://www.imi.gov.my/index.php/en/main-services/visa/visa-requirement-by-country/) page that lists the passports that need a visa, the ones that need a Visa with Reference, and time-limited exemptions, so read the date beside your country. For everyone else, the days on your entry stamp are what count. Do not assume your last trip's number.

Malaysia also requires most foreign visitors to submit the [Malaysia Digital Arrival Card](https://imigresen-online.imi.gov.my/mdac/main) within three days before arrival, according to the government's entry portal. Check the MDAC page for what applies to you. Penang International (PEN) is an international checkpoint, so the same rule applies there as at KLIA.

### Tax, at a high level only

The DE Rantau FAQ carries its own tax table. Treatment turns on whether you are a freelancer or a remote worker and on day counts in Malaysia, including 60 and 182 days. Read it, then ask the Inland Revenue Board ([hasil.gov.my](https://www.hasil.gov.my/en/)) or a tax adviser. Your home country may tax you as well. That is not tax advice. If Penang is going to be most of your year, pay for advice at home and in Malaysia before you sign a lease.

### A simple decision frame

- **The trip fits inside the days your passport gets at the border:** use the entry stamp, and confirm it on the Immigration Department page.
- **You want a longer, legal work stay:** check the income test and document list on the FAQ, then apply on mdec.my from outside Malaysia if you can.
- **You are already inside on a tourist pass:** conversion is not allowed. Read the exit and re-entry rule first.
- **You need more than 24 months:** the pass does not give it. A visa-free year is a different base, such as [Living in Tbilisi](/guides/living-in-tbilisi) (**365** days, **$800**, **UTC+4**).
- **"I will just leave and come back every 90 days":** not a plan. Each entry is the officer's decision, and the pass exists for a reason.

## Best months (Dec-Feb, Jun-Jul) vs rainy months (Sep-Nov)

Penang is hot and humid all year. The row's **28°C** is a yearly average, not a forecast, and it is not the thing that decides your month. Rain and air do.

| Window | What the Penang row says | Practical read |
|---|---|---|
| Dec, Jan, Feb | `bestMonths` | The drier start of the year. Chinese New Year falls in Jan or Feb, so book early |
| Jun, Jul | `bestMonths` | The second dry spell. Check the haze reading before you book |
| Sep, Oct, Nov | `rainyMonths` | Monsoon season is a listed con. Plan indoor days and rides |
| Mar to May, Aug | On neither list | Shoulders. Not a promise, and not a reason to ignore the rain |
| Year-round | `tempAvgC` **28** | Hot and humid either way |

The row matches the official picture. The Malaysian Meteorological Department says most of the peninsula gets its main rainfall peak in October and November and a second, smaller peak in April and May, with the northwest driest in January and February and again in June and July ([met.gov.my](https://www.met.gov.my/en/pendidikan/iklim-malaysia/)). That is why **Mar-May** sits on neither list.

### Haze is the catch in Jun-Jul

Penang's cons do not list haze, but the region does not ask. MetMalaysia links transboundary haze to the southwest monsoon, which runs from about June to September. Some years are clear. Some years are not. Check a current air quality reading in the week you book a Jun-Jul month, and again before you extend. If you have a respiratory condition, treat an air purifier as part of the setup, or pick **Dec-Feb**.

### Pairing Penang with other bases

The [season guide](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season) is the calendar version of this check. Penang's two windows make it an easy pair. [Kuala Lumpur](/destinations/kuala-lumpur) is best **Jun-Aug** and rainy **Nov-Jan**, so Penang's **Dec-Feb** covers the capital's wet season. [Chiang Mai](/destinations/chiang-mai) is best **Nov-Feb** and rainy **Jun-Sep**, so Penang's **Jun-Jul** covers the Thai rain. [Da Nang](/destinations/da-nang) is best **Feb-Jul**, which overlaps Penang's summer window rather than covering it.

George Town's own calendar matters too. The George Town Festival is usually held in August, and Ramadan and Chinese New Year move each year and change opening hours, so look up the dates before you book.

## Daily life: Grab and Rapid Penang, hawker food, healthcare, English, day trips

### Getting around

"Car-dependent" is on the row, and it is fair outside George Town. Inside the heritage core you can walk, early or late in the day. Beyond it, most nomads use Grab, which has operated in Penang for years and handles the airport, the beaches, and the condo strip.

Rapid Penang runs the island's public buses. The free CAT (Central Area Transit) bus loops through George Town between Weld Quay and KOMTAR, the main bus hub. Buses also run out to Batu Ferringhi and the airport. They are cheap and slow, which is fine for a beach day and risky before a call. Check current routes and fares on the operator's page instead of trusting a printed number.

The island links to the mainland by two road bridges, the 13.5 km Penang Bridge and the 24 km Sultan Abdul Halim Muadzam Shah Bridge, and by the ferry from George Town to Butterworth, which carries foot passengers and motorcycles but not cars. Butterworth is also where KTMB's ETS trains leave for Kuala Lumpur Sentral. A light rail line, the Mutiara Line, is under construction, with completion reported for December 2031. Do not plan a lease around it.

Penang International Airport (PEN) is in Bayan Lepas in the south of the island, with regional flights to Kuala Lumpur, Singapore, Bangkok, and other Asian hubs. Long-haul trips usually connect through one of those.

### Food

"Best food in SE Asia" is the first pro on the row, and it is the reason a lot of people stay longer than planned. Hawker stalls, kopitiam breakfasts, and nasi kandar are how you eat inside the **$600** longer-stay estimate to **$800**. Char kway teow, asam laksa, and cendol are the dishes everyone sends you for. New Lane (Lorong Baru), the Gurney Drive hawker centre, and the night stalls around Chulia Street are the obvious starts. Hokkien, Peranakan, Malay, and Indian Muslim cooking all sit within a short walk of each other in George Town, which is what "multicultural" means at dinner.

Imported groceries and Western brunch are the leak. The **foodie** tag is real. It is also how a careful month becomes an expensive one.

### Healthcare

Penang is one of Malaysia's main destinations for medical travel, and the private hospitals are why. Gleneagles Penang, Island Hospital, Loh Guan Lye Specialists Centre, Lam Wah Ee, Penang Adventist, and Pantai Hospital Penang all operate on the island, and the public hospital is Hospital Pulau Pinang in George Town. English at the desk helps. You are a cash patient until an insurer says otherwise, so sort cover before you need it, using the insurance slot above.

### English and paperwork

English proficiency on the row is **8**. Menus, landlords, clinics, and coworking desks work in English. Hawker stalls and older landlords may default to Hokkien, Malay, or Mandarin, which is usually solved with pointing and patience. A DE Rantau application must be in English, which helps, but leases and utilities may not be.

### Working across the clock

The row says **UTC+8**. Your 9am in Penang is 2am in Central European winter time (**UTC+1**), 1am in London in winter, and 8pm the evening before on the US East Coast in winter (**UTC-5**). Summer clock changes in those regions move each figure by an hour. Europe's morning is your afternoon, so a European client roster works, and US calls mean late evenings.

### Day trips and neighbors

Penang Hill has a funicular up from Air Itam ([penanghill.gov.my](https://www.penanghill.gov.my/)), and Kek Lok Si temple sits nearby. Penang National Park at Teluk Bahang is the island's forest and beach walk at the far end of the north coast. Off the island, [Langkawi](/destinations/langkawi) is the resort island to the north (**$1,250** solo, coworking **Low**), and [Kuala Lumpur](/destinations/kuala-lumpur) is a short flight or a train from Butterworth. Time any trip yourself before you commit to a weekend plan.

**Stay with Penang** when you want the food, the sea within reach, English **8**, and an **$800** month you can live inside, in **Dec-Feb** or **Jun-Jul**. You accept heat, limited nightlife, a car-dependent island, and community **4**. Plan the legal stay as an application, not an assumption.

**Pick Kuala Lumpur** when work comes first: **100 Mbps**, coworking **High**, community **6**, and rail you can use, for **$1,000**. Same entry rules, same DE Rantau pass, same clock, no beach. Its best months **Jun-Aug** overlap Penang's summer window. Neighborhoods and the pass are in [Living in Kuala Lumpur](/guides/living-in-kuala-lumpur).

**Look at Langkawi** when the beach is the whole point and cost is not. [Langkawi](/destinations/langkawi) is **$1,250** solo, **70 Mbps**, coworking **Low**, community **2**, best **Dec-Mar**. It is a resort island, not a cheaper Penang.

**Pick Chiang Mai** when you want community **9**, coworking **High**, and **95 Mbps** for **$850**. The trade is no sea and a 30-day passport-dependent tourism exemption on **UTC+7**. Best months **Nov-Feb** overlap Penang's winter window. The [Chiang Mai living guide](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) covers the Destination Thailand Visa as the long-stay path.

**Pick Da Nang** when you want a cheaper beach city: **$700**, **80 Mbps**, safety **8.5**, **E-Visa**, **90** days, **UTC+7**. You take English **3** and a typhoon season in the cons. The [Da Nang living guide](/guides/living-in-da-nang) covers An Thuong versus Hai Chau.

**Look at Bali** when the social beach is the product and you will pay **$1,200**, with internet reliability **5** and a visa field of **30 days** (visa on arrival). Community is **9**, more than double Penang's. Read [Living in Bali](/guides/living-in-bali) before you assume the two islands are the same trip.

**Look at Tbilisi** when the constraint is stay length, not weather. [Living in Tbilisi](/guides/living-in-tbilisi) is the year-long contrast in this dataset: **Visa Free**, **365** days, solo **$800**, **UTC+4**. It is a different base, not a Malaysian extension.

## First-week checklist + spin the globe / compare Penang on Nomad Spin

### Common mistakes

- Treating the DE Rantau pass as a stamp you receive at the airport, or as a residence permit
- Entering on a tourist pass and expecting to convert it to a DE Rantau pass in place
- Paying the **$30** nightly median for thirty nights and calling the month **$800**
- Signing a sea-view condo lease in Tanjung Bungah, then taking a Grab to George Town every day
- Booking a heritage shophouse for a call-heavy job without testing the line
- Booking **Jun-Jul** without checking the haze, or **Sep-Nov** without a rain plan
- Assuming a Malaysian bank will open an account on the pass

### First week

**Days 1-2**

- SIM in the phone, hotspot tested
- Grab installed, and the CAT bus route checked
- Entry stamp checked, and the last legal day written down
- One coworking trial day, including a real call, if the home line is still unknown
- Walk the block at night before you decide you can sleep there

**Days 3-4**

- See more than one monthly place: one in or near George Town, one in Pulau Tikus, Gurney, or Tanjung Tokong
- Run the speed test in the room at call hour, upload included
- Ride the actual commute to your desk once at the hour you would do it
- Read the DE Rantau FAQ against your documents if the plan is longer than your entry stamp

**Days 5-7**

- Commit to one area for two to four weeks, not to "Penang" in the abstract
- Fix the work loop: home deep work, a coworking desk on call days, one cafe that passed the call test
- Only then book the longer stay, the onward flight, and insurance

### Booking order

These slots are last, after the week above. Use the buttons on the [Penang destination page](/destinations/penang) when you are ready to book.

| Slot | What to book |
|---|---|
| Stays | The monthly place that passed the speed test, the noise test, and the commute test |
| Flights | The onward or return date your stamp or pass actually supports, into or out of PEN |
| Insurance | Travel and health cover you have read. A DE Rantau endorsement needs a policy that covers Malaysia |
