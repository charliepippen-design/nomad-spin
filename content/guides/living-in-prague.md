## Is living in Prague worth it for digital nomads in 2026?

Living in Prague as a digital nomad is a specific trade in 2026: Lisbon-level safety for $500 less, plus a visa fork the other Europe guides on this site do not share. The row on [Prague](/destinations/prague) shows safety **8.8**, the same score as [Lisbon](/destinations/lisbon), at **$1,700** a month solo against Lisbon's **$2,200**. Coworking density is **High**. Internet is **200 Mbps**, reliability **9/10**, power **9/10**. Female safety is **8**, the same as Lisbon. The longer stay on the Prague row is **$1,300**. The nightly Airbnb median is **$80**.

That is the go. The no-go is the rest of the row, and it is mostly the calendar and the paperwork. Best months are **May, Jun, Jul, Aug, and Sep**. Rainy months are **Nov and Dec**. Average temperature is **9°C**. The live trade-offs are tourist crowds, cold winters, and rising prices. The visa field is **Freelance Visa**, up to **365 days**, time zone **UTC+1**, language Czech, region Europe. That 365 is not a stamp you collect at the airport, and it is not the same product as a digital nomad visa in Lisbon or Budapest. Czechia runs a trade-licence path and a separate Digital Nomad Program. They do not accept the same person. The section below separates them. Verify both on an official page before you book a year.

The vibe tags are `party`, `foodie`, and `workhub`, on an urban landscape. Pros on the page: stunning architecture, great beer culture, affordable for Europe, central location, rich history. "Affordable for Europe" means cheaper than Lisbon in this dataset. It does not mean cheap in the abstract. Rising prices are on the trade-off list because that gap has been closing.

Worth it if you want a European capital that can hold a video call, you can live near the **$1,700** band on purpose, safety **8.8** is the number you came for, and you will either spend Nov-Feb somewhere else or you already know you can work through a cold, short day. A weak default if winter mood, tourist-center noise, or a Czech office form is the thing you keep negotiating with yourself about.

If budget, internet, and safety are not locked yet, start with [How to Choose Your Next Nomad Base](/guides/how-to-choose-next-nomad-base). If the open question is the month, use [Where to Go Next by Season](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season). ### Plain answers

**How much does it cost to live in Prague as a digital nomad?** Nomad Spin lists about **$1,700** a month for a solo nomad and about **$1,300** a month long-term, with a median Airbnb around **$80** a night. Vinohrady costs more than Žižkov or an outer district. Coworking, eating out often, and a central flat push you above the baseline.

**Is Prague internet good enough for video calls?** Yes. Nomad Spin shows about **200 Mbps**, reliability **9/10**, power **9/10**, and High coworking density. Test upload speed in the actual unit before you sign a lease. Fiber is common in renovated buildings. Cafe wifi in Old Town can be congested.

**What visa do digital nomads use in Prague?** Nomad Spin lists a **Freelance Visa** for up to **365 days** (**UTC+1**). That label is not one product. A trade licence plus a long-term business visa is the freelancer path. A foreign employee may use the separate Digital Nomad Program. Verify Czech MFA, Interior, and Industry pages before booking.

**Who should skip living in Prague?** Skip it if you hate cold winters, tourist crowds, or paperwork. Nomad Spin flags tourist crowds, cold winters, and rising prices as the live trade-offs. Bureaucracy and the language barrier are the other reasons a year fails. If you need year-round sun, pick a southern base.

**What are the best months to live in Prague?** Nomad Spin's friendliest months are **May, Jun, Jul, Aug, and Sep**. Rainy months to plan around are **Nov and Dec**. Average temperature on the row is **9°C**. Arrive in May if you can. Winters are short-day and cold.

Prague fits people who want a European work city with architecture, beer, and a bill under a Western European capital, and who will pick a district that is not the postcard. **It tends to fit if you:**

- Want Lisbon's safety score (**8.8**, female safety **8**) and can spend **$500** a month less than Lisbon's **$2,200**

- The **$1,700** number is a city baseline, not an Old Town lifestyle quote.

## Neighborhoods that work: Vinohrady, Žižkov, Karlín, Holešovice; skip living in Old Town

Prague's work month is a district decision. The castle and Charles Bridge are why the photos exist. They are a poor default address. There is no separate Nomad Spin page for these neighborhoods. The canonical numbers live on [Prague](/destinations/prague). The trade-offs below are how people actually use the city. Rents move faster than this paragraph. Believe the flats you view, not a blog's district spreadsheet. This guide does not invent a koruna rent for any of them.

| Base | Best for | You give up |
|---|---|---|
| Vinohrady (mostly Prague 2) | A first month: cafes, parks, metro A, a door you can close | The lowest rent, and any flat that faces a tram trunk |
| Žižkov (Prague 3) | A lower bill, local pubs, a hill and a view | Quiet nights if you pick the bar street, an easy walk to Karlín |
| Karlín (Prague 8) | Newer buildings, offices, a flat walk, metro B | The village feel of Žižkov, and some of the Vinohrady park life |
| Holešovice (Prague 7) | Space, the market hall, galleries, trams to the center | Stepping out into the Old Town scene |
| Old Town and Malá Strana (Prague 1) | A few postcard days | The $1,700 band, sleep, and a normal Tuesday |

### Vinohrady

Vinohrady is the default "I want a nice month without living in the tour groups" base. Residential streets, cafes, Riegrovy sady when you need a park, and metro line A at Náměstí Míru and Jiřího z Poděbrad. You can walk to the edge of the center and still sleep. If you arrive alone and want a social calendar that is not a pub crawl, this is the easiest first pin.

**Trade-off:** demand is why people say Vinohrady costs more than Žižkov. That is a pattern in the listings, not a Nomad Spin rent field. A flat on a main tram street is a different month from a flat two streets off it. Ask which way the bedroom faces.

Pick Vinohrady if you will pay for the calmer version of central and you have viewed the unit. Skip it if the only flats in your budget are the ones over the loudest corner and you have not stood in the room at night.

### Žižkov

Žižkov is the hill to the east: more local, more pubs, often the flatter price, and less of a postcard. The TV tower is the landmark people use to orient. A lot of the neighborhood is tram, not metro. Jiřího z Poděbrad on line A is the useful edge, not the middle of the district.

**Trade-off:** nightlife **9** shows up here as a bedroom problem if you pick the wrong street. Light sleepers should drink in Žižkov and sleep in Vinohrady or Holešovice. The AEO line above is the practical version: Vinohrady asks more, Žižkov and outer districts ask less, and neither number is published in this dataset.

Pick Žižkov if you want the local city and you have checked the bar license under the flat. Skip sleeping there if you came to take calls at 9am and you already know pubs wreck your night.

### Karlín

Karlín sits east of the center, lower and newer than Žižkov, with metro B (Křižíkova, Invalidovna) and a lot of the city's newer offices. It is the practical "I want a work district" pin: flatter walks, newer buildings, cafes that expect a laptop on a weekday. You are still close to the center. You are not in the castle photo.

**Trade-off:** a main road and a courtyard are different products, and a new building is not automatically a quiet one. Ask which street, and run the speed test in the unit. This guide does not publish a commute-time score.

Pick Karlín for a monthly flat if your week is calls and you want newer stock. Skip it if your whole plan is "I will just go out in the old center" and you will resent the tram home.

### Holešovice

Holešovice is the bend of the river north of the center: the market hall at Pražská tržnice, galleries, more space, metro C at Vltavská and Nádraží Holešovice. It is where a lot of long-stayers land when Vinohrady feels tight and Old Town feels fake. You sleep. You still have trams back to the scene.

You will not step out of your door into the Charles Bridge. That is the point. If your work is meeting-heavy, name the coworking room you will actually ride to before you sign.

Best for a second month, or for a first month if you already know you do not need Prague 1 to feel oriented.

### Old Town and Malá Strana

Prague 1 is the postcard: Old Town square, Charles Bridge, the castle side in Malá Strana. It is pleasant for a weekend and a poor default for a work month. Tourist crowds are a listed trade-off, and this is where they live. Tourist pricing shows up in coffee, in short lets, and in wifi shared with a building full of weekend guests.

Use Prague 1 as a place you walk to. Sign a month there only if you have viewed the flat, tested the internet at call time, and you are honestly fine leaving the **$1,700** band.

## Internet, power, and coworking: 200 Mbps, reliability 9, power 9, High coworking

The city average is **200 Mbps**, reliability **9/10**, power stability **9**, coworking density **High**. That is a yes for video calls. It is the same headline stack as Lisbon and Budapest, which is why the cost gap and the winter are the decision and the pipes are not. It is a different product from Bali, where the dataset shows **50 Mbps** and reliability **5**, and the [Bali living guide](/guides/living-in-bali) is blunt about street-by-street fiber. It is also a different product from Cape Town, where [Living in Cape Town](/guides/living-in-cape-town) has to plan around power stability **4**. Prague's power score is **9**. You do not need a personal inverter strategy to have a work week here. Keep a charged laptop for cafes.

The 9/10 is still an average. A renovated flat in Karlín with its own line and a courtyard guesthouse in Old Town are not the same connection. Test the room.

### A setup that holds calls

1. **Home line you have tested.** Ask which provider is already in the unit, and whether the building's Wi-Fi is shared with other guests. O2, T-Mobile, and Vodafone are the ordinary retail networks landlords name. "Wi-Fi included" in a short let is not a speed test. Run one from the desk at the hour you take calls. Upload matters as much as the 200 Mbps headline. This dataset does not say internet is bundled into every lease. Ask.
2. **A local SIM with real data.** The same three networks sell prepaid and eSIM plans at the airport and in the city. If a call would hurt you to drop, keep a second line and learn the hotspot before the day you need it. The eSIM module on the [Prague destination page](/destinations/prague) is the site's booking path for that backup. This guide does not add a partner code.
3. **One coworking room you have already used for a call.** Not a cafe on the Old Town square you liked for the view.

### Cafes

Vinohrady, Karlín, and the calmer edges of Holešovice will let you open a laptop. That is not the same as a 45-minute call. Do the call once, at the hour you actually meet, before you tell a client the cafe is your office. Old Town wifi is often shared and congested. A tiled room with no door will lose to a booked booth every time.

## Freelancer or employee? Czech trade licence vs digital nomad programme vs Schengen short stay (verify)

This is operational orientation, not legal advice. Forms, fees, and processing times move. The number on our city page is not the law. When this section and a Czech government page disagree, the government page wins. Read it the week you apply.

The fork is the reason to read this page. In this comparison set, Prague is the city whose visa field says **Freelance Visa**. Budapest, Lisbon, Barcelona, and Valencia list a **Digital Nomad Visa**. Those labels are not the same paperwork. Prague's field is also not one Czech product. There are three different stays. Pick the one that matches how you actually earn money.

### What Nomad Spin currently shows

The Prague row says **Freelance Visa**, **365 days**, **UTC+1**. That is what you will see on [Prague](/destinations/prague). Use **365** as the dataset field. It lines up with the Foreign Ministry's maximum of one year for a long-term entrepreneurship visa. It is not a stamp you receive on arrival, and it is not a card you already hold.

### A short stay is Schengen, not a freelance visa

You do not need a Czech long-term visa for a short visit. The European Commission's [visa policy page](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/schengen-borders-and-visa/visa-policy_en) describes the Schengen short stay: up to **90 days in any 180-day period**, counted across the whole Schengen area, not 90 days in Czechia plus another 90 in Germany. Many passports enter visa-free. Others need a short-stay visa before they fly. Stays longer than 90 days are national procedures. The same page says visa-exempt travellers will need an ETIAS authorisation, tied to the Entry/Exit System, on the timetable the Commission publishes. Check that page for your travel date. This guide does not guess whether ETIAS is already in force for you.

A tourist entry is not a trade licence and not the Digital Nomad Program. This guide will not tell you that freelancing, or remote work for a foreign employer, is allowed on a tourist stamp.

EU and EEA citizens, and Swiss citizens, are not the audience for these long-stay visas. Free-movement registration, if you stay, is a different path. A Czech trade licence can still be a business registration for someone who wants to invoice locally. That is not an immigration visa. If you are an EU citizen, skip the two third-country routes below and read the trade-licensing rules only if you will actually do business in Czechia.

### Path A: trade licence, then a long-term visa for business

This is the path the dataset label is pointing at. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs publishes it on the [long-term visa for entrepreneurship](https://mzv.gov.cz/jnp/en/information_for_aliens/long_stay_visa/entrepreneurship.html) page. The summary here follows that page. Verify it before you rely on it.

It is for third-country nationals who intend to run a business, be self-employed, or be a statutory organ (or a member of one) of a company. Third-country nationals, on that page, means citizens of states outside the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, and Switzerland. The visa is not for family members of EU, EEA, or Swiss citizens who hold, or have applied for, a residence card as a family member.

You lodge the application in person at a Czech embassy or consulate, by territorial competence, with an interview and fingerprints. On the form, purpose of stay is **Business**. The purpose document is a registry extract: a trade licence extract (živnostenský list), a company-register extract, or another extract the page allows. The Ministry of the Interior decides. If it approves, the embassy that took the file issues the visa. The page says a long-term visa can be issued for a **maximum of one year**.

If the purpose continues, you can ask, before the visa expires, for an extension of up to one year at the local branch of the Ministry of the Interior, or for a change of status to a long-term residence permit. The page lists long-term residence purposes that include employment, study, scientific research, family, intra-company transfer, and investment, and residence for residents of other EU member states. The Interior's [purpose-of-stay page](https://www.mvcr.cz/mvcren/article/document-on-the-purpose-of-stay.aspx) now opens on an archive that says its content may not be current. That archived text says the business purpose, for someone trading under the Trade Licensing Act, is a statement from the Trade Register.

**What else that MFA page says to expect.**

- Supporting documents include accommodation, financial means, a criminal-record extract, photos, and the fee. This guide does not freeze a fee. Read the consulate's current list.
- The page requires proof of financial means. It does not print a single euro salary. Do not copy a monthly euro bar from a blog into your plan. Open the MFA page and the Interior's current funds guidance the week you file, and use the figure those pages are actually applying.
- Most supporting documents must be no older than 180 days, with the exceptions the page lists (passport, some registry documents, diplomas, a current photo). Documents must be in Czech, or submitted with an official translation into Czech. Foreign public documents must be legalized (apostille or superlegalization).
- The legal processing time is **90 days**. It can be extended to **120 days** in cases that need more examination.
- Travel medical insurance is required **when the visa is approved**, not when you apply. When you collect the visa, the page asks for an officially verified Czech translation of the policy and the general conditions, and proof of payment if they ask. Match the document to that instruction.
- The visa does not by itself guarantee entry. You still have to meet the Schengen Borders Code at the external border.
- A long-term visa lets the holder move in the Schengen area for a maximum of **90 days in any 180** for **non-gainful** purposes. It is not a work permit for Germany or Austria.

The legal sources named on the MFA page include Act No. 326/1999 Coll., on the residence of foreign nationals, and Act No. 455/1991 Coll., the Trade Licensing Act. The trade itself is declared at a trade licensing office. The MFA page points at the Ministry of Industry and Trade's trade register for that step.

For a later long-term residence permit for business, the Interior also asks for proof you have no arrears with the financial administration, customs, social security, and (if you are in the public system) your health insurance company. The Ministry of the Interior's [Information Portal for Foreigners](https://ipc.gov.cz/en/forms-and-documents/documents/proof-of-no-outstanding-debts/) says that, on the day you apply, that proof cannot be older than 30 days.

### Path B: the Digital Nomad Program, which is not the same visa

The Ministry of Industry and Trade runs a **Digital Nomad Program**. The public page, updated **1 July 2025**, is the [Digital Nomad Program](https://mpo.gov.cz/en/foreign-trade/economic-migration/programs-and-projects/digital-nomad-program--275799/). The rules in the [program text (July 2025 PDF)](https://mpo.gov.cz/assets/en/foreign-trade/economic-migration/programs-and-projects/2025/7/Digital-Nomad-EN.pdf) are what this summary follows. There is **no legal entitlement** to be included. Inclusion is not the visa. The Ministry of the Interior still decides the residence application. The Foreign Ministry runs the embassies.

The program is for citizens of **Australia, Brazil, India, Israel, Japan, Canada, the Republic of Korea, Mexico, New Zealand, Singapore, the United Kingdom, the United States, or Taiwan**. If your passport is not on that list, this program is not your route. Path A may still be, if you qualify for a business visa. Do not assume the city-page label "Freelance Visa" means you are in the program.

You also cannot be included if, on the day you apply or within the preceding year, you hold or held a Czech long-term visa for more than 90 days or a Czech long-term residence permit.

The program text splits participants in two. That split is the freelancer-versus-employee fork.

| Who you are | What the program text says you apply for | The limit that actually matters |
|---|---|---|
| Employee of a foreign employer | Visa for a stay of more than 90 days for **other** purposes | You stay employed abroad. You do not enter employment under Czech law. The employer has at least 50 employees worldwide. Remote work is in the contract or in the employer's sworn statement. The work is highly qualified IT or marketing |
| Freelancer | Visa for a stay of more than 90 days for **business** | You hold, or will hold, a Czech trade licence for IT-related business or marketing services. Not any trade. IT or marketing |

Both tracks are for a highly skilled IT or marketing specialist, with the education the program text describes (or, for IT, proven experience of at least 3 years). Both tracks require income of **at least 1.5 times the average gross annual salary announced by the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs**. For a freelancer, the client contract or the client's affidavit has to show IT or marketing work, a duration, and remuneration of at least that same 1.5 times figure when the total is spread across the months of the contract. This guide does not convert that formula into a monthly euro number. Open the current Ministry of Labour announcement the week you apply. A blog's euro salary is not this test.

**Length of inclusion, from the program text.** Inclusion of an employee of a foreign employer is valid for a **maximum of 1 year**. To stay past the visa, that person applies to the Ministry of Industry and Trade to extend inclusion, then (after a positive decision) to the Ministry of the Interior for a long-term residence permit for the same "other" purpose. Inclusion of a freelancer has **unlimited validity** on the program text. That still does not limit the state's ability to suspend or end the program, and the freelancer still has to apply for a long-term residence permit for business before the long-term visa expires.

**What you get if you are included.** A chance to file the long-term visa at a Czech embassy, and processing of that application within **45 days** of submission when the file is complete. The closest family (spouse, registered partner, and child) can be included if they apply at the same time. The ordinary entrepreneurship visa's legal clock, above, is 90 days and can run to 120. The 45 days is a program benefit, not the default for every freelancer.

**Order of steps, from the program text.** A freelancer contacts the trade office and declares the trade first. An employee skips that step. Then you apply to the Ministry of Industry and Trade for inclusion, at the address on the program page (`digitalnomad@mpo.gov.cz`), on the prescribed form. If you are included, the embassy gives you a date to file the residence application in person, with the full legal attachments again (criminal record, purpose of stay, accommodation, funds, and the rest), even if you already sent some of them to Industry and Trade. Documents are originals or notarized copies, and generally no older than 180 days, except the travel document and registry documents. The Interior decides. The program text says program participants report their place of stay to the Police of the Czech Republic within **3 working days** of arrival, unless the accommodation provider does it.

The program is implemented under Government Resolution No. 475 of 28 June 2023, in relation to Act No. 326/1999 Coll. Capacity for freelancer (business) visas is tied to the government regulation that caps how many business-visa applications an embassy may take in a year. The program text notes a specific cap at the embassy in Delhi for "other" stays. If you will file in India, read that note on the PDF. Do not assume every embassy has the same queue.

### Which path is actually yours

- **A few weeks, and your passport qualifies:** a Schengen short stay, after you count 90 days in 180 across the whole area. Neither long-stay path is required for that trip. Neither path is replaced by it.
- **Up to a year, self-employed, and you will hold a Czech trade licence:** the MFA entrepreneurship visa (path A). If you are also in the nationality list, in IT or marketing, and you win inclusion, the Digital Nomad Program (path B, freelancer track) is the accelerated version of a business visa, not a different career.
- **Up to a year, employed only by a foreign employer, no Czech job, IT or marketing, passport on the list, employer of 50 or more:** the Digital Nomad Program's employee track. Path A is the wrong form. A tourist stamp is also the wrong form.
- **A passport outside that list, or a job that is not IT or marketing:** not the program. Read path A only if you are actually going to do business in Czechia. Do not force a marketing label onto a different profession to fit the PDF.
- **A year because the city page says 365:** not a plan. 365 is the dataset field. The entrepreneurship visa, if granted, is up to one year, with an extension or a residence permit as a separate step.

### Tax residence, at a high level

Tax residence is separate from the visa. A long stay can raise tax questions in Czechia and at home. The Financial Administration's [page for individuals](https://financnisprava.gov.cz/cs/dane/dane/dan-z-prijmu/fyzicke-osoby/obecne-informace) describes a Czech tax resident as someone who has a home in Czechia or who usually stays here, and it says a double-tax treaty can change that result. Its [income-tax FAQ](https://financnisprava.gov.cz/cs/dane/dane/dan-z-prijmu/dotazy-a-odpovedi/dan-z-prijmu-fyzickych-osob/aktualne-k-dani-z-prijmu-fyzickych-osob-2024) describes "usually staying" as at least **183 days** in the calendar year, counting every started day. That is the administration's explanation of the statute. It is not a ruling on your contract, and a treaty can point the other way.

The flat-rate regime (paušální daň) is a real option for some self-employed people, with conditions and published payments that change by band and by year. The Financial Administration's [flat-rate page](https://financnisprava.gov.cz/cs/dane/dane/dan-z-prijmu/pausalni-dan/obecne-informace) is the source. This guide does not copy a koruna payment, does not say the regime fits a foreign remote worker, and does not tell you whether your foreign salary is Czech-source income. If Prague is most of your year, pay for advice from someone who will sign it. Nothing here is tax advice.

The insurance slot on the [Prague destination page](/destinations/prague) is where this site's cover links are built. This guide does not name a partner or invent a premium.

## Best months (May-Sep), rainy Nov-Dec, and short winter days

The friendliest months on the row are **May, Jun, Jul, Aug, and Sep**. Rainy months are **Nov and Dec**. Annual average temperature is **9°C**. That yearly figure hides the split that actually decides the trip: a long usable summer, and a winter a lot of people underestimate because the castle photos were taken in June.

**May to September** is the easy arrival. Terraces are open, the parks in Vinohrady are the point of the neighborhood, and you can tell whether you like the district before you sign a long lease. July and August are on the best-month list. They are also when the tourist crowds, already a listed trade-off, are thickest in Prague 1. Live in Vinohrady, Žižkov, Karlín, or Holešovice and visit the bridge. Do not judge the whole summer by an afternoon on Charles Bridge.

**Nov and Dec** are the rainy months on the row. They are also the start of the grey stretch. Cold winters are a listed trade-off, not a surprise you discover in a caption. Daylight gets short. This guide does not invent an hour count for sunset. It does tell you to stand in a flat in the afternoon before you sign a winter lease, and to ask about heating. If your work depends on leaving the apartment, test that against a real winter week before you commit the dark months.

**Pair the winter, do not pretend it away.** Many nomads do Prague from May through September and put the cold months somewhere else. [Living in Chiang Mai](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) is the dataset's Nov-Feb work city (solo **$850**, best months **Nov-Feb**, with burning season in Mar-Apr as the hard stop). [Living in Cape Town](/guides/living-in-cape-town) is the other winter-sun contrast (solo **$1,400**, best months **Oct-Mar**, safety **5.5**, power stability **4**). Those are different products. They are not cheaper Pragues. Read those guides when the month is the question. This page stays about Prague.

October is not on the best-month list and not on the rainy list. It is a shoulder. It can be a good arrival if you have already decided you like the cold coming. It is not the reason the row lists May-Sep.

## Daily life: trams and metro, beer and food, Czech, party streets and quiet ones

The daily texture is why people stay after the spreadsheet says yes. Architecture, beer, and the center of the map are the pros that show up in a normal week. "Affordable for Europe" and the pipes are the work week. You can have both. You cannot have them in the same room at 1am if you chose the room badly.

**Getting around.** Prague has three metro lines (A, B, C) and a tram network that does more of the daily work than visitors expect. PID (Prague Integrated Transport) is the ticket system for metro, trams, buses, and trolleybuses inside the city. PID's [airport page](https://pid.cz/en/travel-information/airport-and-stations/) lists the public links from Václav Havel Airport (PRG). Line **59** runs between the airport and Nádraží Veleslavín, where you change to metro A. That page lists about **35 minutes** to the city center, and a ticket of **50 CZK** (46 CZK in the PID app, 55 CZK by SMS). The Airport Express (AE) to the main station is listed at **200 CZK**, and the same page says 24-hour and 72-hour Prague tickets do not cover AE. Confirm on pid.cz the week you fly. A normal city ticket and the airport express are different products. Buy the ticket you will actually use. Do not assume one pass covers both.

**Beer and food.** Great beer culture is a listed pro, and it is a Tuesday, not a personality. A neighborhood pub and a lunch menu are how the $1,700 band stays intact. A restaurant on the Old Town square is how it does not. This guide does not invent a price for a beer. Pay in koruna. Some tourist menus print a euro price beside the Czech price. Look at the card terminal before you approve a conversion. Cards are normal in the center. Carry some koruna for a smaller pub or a market.

**Czech.** English proficiency on the row is **7**. Menus, cafes, and coworking front desks in Vinohrady and Karlín cope. A lease, a clinic form, a pharmacy label, and a message from a landlord often do not. The visa section is worse: official translations into Czech are part of the file, not a courtesy. A few phrases (dobrý den, děkuji, prosím, ano, ne) plus a translation app you have tested offline will carry more of the ordinary week than another cafe. The language barrier sits in the dataset cons because this is the week, not a quirk. The destination page's short trade-off list does not currently print it. The week still includes it.

**Party streets and quiet ones.** Nightlife on the row is **9**. That score lives in parts of Žižkov, in the center, and on streets you can choose not to sleep on. Vinohrady side streets, much of Karlín, and Holešovice are the quieter products. Go out when you want the city. Do not sign a lease because a courtyard was perfect on a Thursday.

**Community.** Community size on the row is **7**. Lisbon's is **9**. You can meet people in the first week if you go back to the same coworking room. You can also spend a month in Old Town and only meet people who are leaving on Sunday. There is no single official nomad office. Recurring desks and the same cafe three times are the scene.

**Money and admin.** Everyday prices and PID fares are in koruna. This guide does not publish an exchange rate. Your bank's rate that week is the rate. A longer stay wants a landlord who will put the lease in writing, because the visa file wants an address you can document. English at **7** covers the easy counters. It does not cover the trade office or the embassy checklist.

**Stay with Prague** when you want safety **8.8**, **200 Mbps**, reliability **9**, power **9**, coworking **High**, and **$1,700** is a number you can actually pay. You accept Czech, a winter plan, tourist crowds if you wander into Prague 1, and a visa that is a trade licence or a narrow Digital Nomad Program, not a stamp on arrival. Female safety is **8**. `lgbtFriendly` is **7**. Community is **7**.

**Look at [Budapest](/destinations/budapest)** when **$1,500** is the ceiling and you still want **200 Mbps**, reliability **9**, power **9**, and coworking **High**. Safety is **8.3** (female safety **7**). You save about **$200** a month against Prague and you give up the safety tie with Lisbon. Best months are **Apr-Jun** and **Sep-Oct**. Rainy months are **Nov-Dec**. Cold winters are on that row too. The visa field is a **Digital Nomad Visa**, **365 days**, which in Hungarian rules is the White Card: remote work for a foreign employer, and no gainful activity in Hungary. That is a different file from a Czech trade licence. Read the [Budapest living guide](/guides/living-in-budapest) before you treat the two 365s as the same product.

**Look at [Lisbon](/destinations/lisbon)** when you want the same **8.8** safety and the same **200 Mbps**, and you will pay **$2,200** for coast, English **8**, community **9**, female safety **8**, and `lgbtFriendly` **9**. The longer stay is **$1,800**. The nightly median is **$120**. The visa field says 365 days. That number is the initial visa field. Portugal's remote-work path leads to a **2-year** residence permit, renewable for 3-year periods (Lei 23/2007, art. 75). Read the [Lisbon living guide](/guides/living-in-lisbon) before you treat "digital nomad visa" as the same paperwork as Prague's Freelance Visa field. The question is whether community size and the coast are worth about **$500** a month to you. Rising rents are Lisbon's version of Prague's rising prices.

**Look at [Barcelona](/destinations/barcelona)** when you want **300 Mbps** and a beach, and you will pay **$2,500** and accept safety **7.5**. The longer stay on that row is **$2,000**. Coworking is **High**. Best months are **May, Jun, Sep, and Oct**. The visa field says 365 days. Spain's telework path is a consular visa of up to 1 year, then an in-country residence authorization of up to **3 years** (Ley 14/2013, art. 74 quinquies). Neighborhoods, rent, and that path are in [Living in Barcelona](/guides/living-in-barcelona). Do not make it the reason you are reading this one. If $2,500 does not fit, Prague at **$1,700** is one of the cheaper peers, and the visa is not Spain's.

**Look at [Valencia](/destinations/valencia)** when you want a smaller Spain beach city at **$1,900**, which is **$200** more than Prague, with **170 Mbps** and coworking **Med** rather than High. Safety on that row is **8.0**. Best months are **Apr-Jun** and **Sep**. October is on Valencia's rainy-month list, not its best-month list, and flooding risk is one of that row's cons. The October calendar and the Spain telework path are in [Living in Valencia](/guides/living-in-valencia). Do not treat a beach month as a copy of this visa section.

**Look at [Porto](/destinations/porto)** only as a coast check, not as a second topic. Solo **$1,800**, **200 Mbps**, safety **9.0**, coworking **Med**. The district choice and the wet-winter trade-off are in [Living in Porto](/guides/living-in-porto). If the question is "Lisbon's pipes without Lisbon's bill," Prague and Budapest are the comparisons this page is built for. Porto is a different country and a different visa.

If the European bill is still too high, [Living in Chiang Mai](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) and [Living in Bali](/guides/living-in-bali) are the other full guides in the set for a lower solo number. They are not districts of Prague. Different pipes, different visas, different months.

## First-week checklist and how to compare Prague on Nomad Spin

### Common mistakes

- Treating thirty nights at **$80** as if you were "living on $1,700"
- Sleeping in Old Town, or on a Žižkov bar street, and deciding the whole city is unworkable
- Reading **365** on the city page as a stamp you receive at the airport
- Treating the Digital Nomad Program as the same thing as a živnostenský list, or assuming every passport and every job qualifies
- Copying a euro income figure from a blog when the official test is 1.5 times a salary the Ministry of Labour announces
- Arriving in November and judging May by that week
- Assuming Lisbon's D8 paperwork, or Budapest's White Card, is what Czechia asks for
- Buying an Airport Express fare in your head and then finding your ordinary PID ticket does not cover that line

### Before you fly

- Read the [Prague destination page](/destinations/prague) and write down **$1,700**, **$1,300**, **$80**, **200 Mbps**, **8.8**, and **UTC+1**
- Pick the month from the row: **May, Jun, Jul, Aug, or Sep** unless you have a winter plan
- Pick the district for sleep first, and the district for going out second. Do not start in Old Town
- If you are on a Schengen stamp, write the 90-in-180 date across the whole Schengen area, not just Czechia
- If you want the year, decide which path is yours. Open the [MFA entrepreneurship page](https://mzv.gov.cz/jnp/en/information_for_aliens/long_stay_visa/entrepreneurship.html) for a trade licence. Open the [Digital Nomad Program](https://mpo.gov.cz/en/foreign-trade/economic-migration/programs-and-projects/digital-nomad-program--275799/) only if your passport and your job are on that page
- Buy health cover that matches the trip you are actually taking: a short Schengen stay, or the insurance the visa page asks for when the visa is approved
- Book the landing stay from the destination page. This guide does not embed a partner link

### In the first seven days

- Run a speed test in the room, at call time, upload included
- Ride line 59 or whichever airport link you actually paid for, and read the current fare on [PID's airport page](https://pid.cz/en/travel-information/airport-and-stations/)
- Get a local SIM and learn the hotspot once
- Do one real call from Impact Hub, Opero, or whichever room you confirmed is open
- Walk Old Town in the afternoon, then go home to Vinohrady, Karlín, Holešovice, or the quiet part of Žižkov
- Pay in koruna. Check the card terminal
- Learn five Czech phrases before you need the pharmacy
- If the flat is dark, loud, or on a shared Wi-Fi story you do not believe, move before you sign the long lease

### Compare it on the globe

The point of the dataset is to stop arguing from postcards.

1. Open [Nomad Spin](/) and set a solo budget around **$1,700**, internet at **150** or better, and safety at **8**.
2. Set the region to Europe. Spin.
3. Open [Prague](/destinations/prague) and [Lisbon](/destinations/lisbon) side by side. Same safety **8.8**. Same **200 Mbps**. **$1,700** against **$2,200**. That **$500** gap is the cost decision. Community **7** against **9** is the other one.
4. If Lisbon is still in the pile, read the [Lisbon living guide](/guides/living-in-lisbon) and decide whether the coast and the community are worth the premium. If the bill has to fall by about **$200** and you can live with safety **8.3**, open the [Budapest living guide](/guides/living-in-budapest).
5. If the month is November through February, open the [Chiang Mai living guide](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) or the [Cape Town living guide](/guides/living-in-cape-town) before you force Prague to be a winter beach. If you want a lower Asian bill and you have read the fiber warning, the [Bali living guide](/guides/living-in-bali) is the other contrast, not a substitute district.
6. If you still do not know which filters matter, go back to [how to choose a base](/guides/how-to-choose-next-nomad-base). If you know the month and not the city, use [where to go next by season](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season).

[Spin the globe](/) with those floors. It should lose when you need a mild winter, English for the lease, a beach, or a visa-free year.
