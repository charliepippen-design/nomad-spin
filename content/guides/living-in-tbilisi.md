## Is living in Tbilisi still worth it for digital nomads in 2026?

A straight answer on living in Tbilisi as a digital nomad: still a rational yes in 2026 if you want a cheap Europe-region base and a long stay. The row on [Tbilisi](/destinations/tbilisi) is the value case: solo month **$800**, longer stay **$550**, nightly Airbnb median **$30**, internet **60 Mbps** with reliability **7/10**, power stability **7**, coworking density **Med**, safety **8.0**. The same page tags it `adventure`, `foodie`, and `mountain`. Best months are **May, Jun, Sep, and Oct**. Rainy months are **Apr and Nov**. The visa field is **Visa Free**, up to **365** days. Time zone is **UTC+4**. Language on the row is Georgian. Annual average temperature is **13°C**.

The pros on that row are why people stay a year: incredibly affordable, one year visa-free, wine, architecture, and warm hospitality. The cons are why a casual booking goes wrong: cold winters, limited English outside the center, infrastructure gaps, air pollution in winter, and few direct flights. "Incredibly affordable" means the published bands are real if you live locally. It does not mean a new Vake apartment plus imported groceries still lands on **$550**.

Worth it if you want that mix and you can live with the cons. A weak default if you need polished Western European infrastructure, a mild winter, or a high LGBTQ+ score. The rest of this guide is how to read the row before you pay a deposit.

If budget, internet, and safety are not locked yet, start with [How to Choose Your Next Nomad Base](/guides/how-to-choose-next-nomad-base). If the open question is the month, use [Where to Go Next by Season](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season). The shortlist that still puts Tbilisi in the conversation is [Best Places for Digital Nomads in 2026](/guides/best-places-digital-nomads-2025). Use that list to find candidates. Use this page, and the live destination row, before you book.

### Plain answers

**How much does it cost to live in Tbilisi as a digital nomad?** Nomad Spin lists **$800** a month solo and **$550** long-term, with a median Airbnb of **$30** a night. On the published Europe-region comparisons, that is a strong value base. Winter heating and imported comforts can raise the bill. Local habits keep it near the band.

**Is Tbilisi internet good enough for video calls?** Often yes on central fiber, with more caveats than Lisbon. The row shows **60 Mbps**, reliability **7/10**, power **7/10**, and coworking density **Med**. Stress-test uploads and keep a mobile backup, especially outside premium buildings.

**What visa or stay options do digital nomads use in Tbilisi?** Nomad Spin lists **Visa Free** for up to **365** days (**UTC+4**) for passports on Georgia's official list. That runway is the draw. Work-permit and tax-residency rules can still apply. Verify official Georgia guidance for your passport and your work setup.

**Who should skip living in Tbilisi?** Skip it if you need polished EU infrastructure, mild winters, or a high LGBTQ+ score (dataset `lgbtFriendly` **3**, overall safety **8.0**). Limited English outside the center and few direct flights are listed cons. If comfort beats cost, read the [Lisbon living guide](/guides/living-in-lisbon). [Cape Town](/destinations/cape-town) is the English-speaking alternative, with its own safety and power scores.

**When are the best months to live in Tbilisi?** Friendliest months on the row: **May, Jun, Sep, Oct**. Rainy shoulder: **Apr, Nov**. Summers are workable. Cold winters and winter air pollution are the filter for a long stay.

## Who Tbilisi is for (and who should skip it)

Tbilisi fits people who want a year in a mountain city that is getting on with its own life, at a burn rate Western Europe will not match. It is a food city and an adventure city. Those tags are on the row. Picking the city means accepting the winter and the infrastructure, then choosing a neighborhood that matches how you actually work.

**It tends to fit if you:**

- Want the dataset's solo baseline (about **$800**) or the longer-stay figure (**$550**), and you will actually live like that
- Need **UTC+4**. In winter, a 9:00 London call (UTC+0) is 13:00 here, and a 9:00 US Eastern call (UTC-5) is 18:00. When the other city springs forward, that gap shrinks by an hour
- Want wine, walking, and a mountain city as the week, with coworking density **Med** as the fallback when the home line fails
- Can work with a mid-size scene (community **6**, nightlife **6**)
- Can put the trip inside **May, Jun, Sep, or Oct**, or you have a real plan for winter
- Can work with safety **8.0** and female safety **7**
- Are fine with English proficiency **4**: the center and a coworking room often work in English, a lease and a clinic desk often do not

**Skip it if polished infrastructure, a mild winter, or LGBTQ+ safety is a hard constraint.**

Also skip it, or pick a different city, if you:

- Need Lisbon-grade pipes and power. [Lisbon](/destinations/lisbon) is **200 Mbps**, reliability **9**, power **9**, at a solo month of **$2,200**. The [Lisbon living guide](/guides/living-in-lisbon) is that comparison in full
- Need a mild winter. Cold winters are a listed con, and **13°C** is a yearly average, not a January afternoon
- Need a high LGBTQ+ score. `lgbtFriendly` here is **3**. Lisbon's is **9**. Overall safety **8.0** does not answer that question
- Need English at the level of a lease, a bank, and a pharmacy in every neighborhood. The cons say limited English outside the center. [Cape Town](/destinations/cape-town) is English proficiency **9**, at solo **$1,400**, safety **5.5**, and power **4**. The [Cape Town living guide](/guides/living-in-cape-town) is the comfort-and-power version of that trade
- Need a beach. Nothing on this row is a beach product. [Bali](/destinations/bali) and the [Bali living guide](/guides/living-in-bali) are the beach comparison, with a different internet and visa story
- Need many nonstop flights. Few direct flights is a listed con. Build the connection into the plan
- Need the **$550** figure while sleeping in a new Vake apartment and eating every meal in Vera cafes

If you are still sorting those filters, the decision order is [how to choose a base](/guides/how-to-choose-next-nomad-base), not another neighborhood roundup.

## Real monthly cost bands (solo, long-term, short Airbnb)

The cost answer is the pair on the row: **$800** a month for a solo nomad, **$550** a month for a longer stay. Nomad Spin does not publish a third "comfortable" total on top of those. Do not replace them with a blogger's spreadsheet.

| Band | Figure | Field on the Tbilisi row | How to read it |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Longer stay | $550 / month | `financials.costLongTerm` | A local-rate month: monthly housing, local food, modest transport |
| Solo nomad baseline | $800 / month | `costUSD` (same as `costNomadSingle`) | The planning number for a solo remote worker |
| Nightly scouting | $30 / night | `financials.airbnbMedian` | What a short test stay tends to cost per night, separate from a monthly budget |

`rentIndex` is **12** (Chiang Mai **15**, Medellín **22**, Budapest **32**, Lisbon **55**). That is relative rent pressure inside the dataset, separate from a dollar rent. Tbilisi sits at the low end of this comparison set.

### The nightly median will quietly beat the monthly baseline

Thirty nights at the published **$30** median is **$900** in lodging before food. That is already above the entire **$800** solo month, and far above **$550**. If you "try the city" on nightly Airbnb for a month, you are off both published bands, however cheap the bakery is. The **$550** and **$800** figures assume a monthly place.

### What moves you off the band

No extra dollar totals here. These are the levers, in the order they actually matter.

| Choice | Stays near $550 to $800 | Leaves the baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Housing | A monthly rental in Saburtalo, or a simpler Vera or Chugureti place | Nightly Airbnb at the $30 median, or a new Vake apartment as the default |
| Food | Bakeries, markets, and neighborhood restaurants most days | Vera cafe lunches and imported groceries as the plan |
| Work | Home fiber, plus coworking on call days | All-day cafe hopping, plus a premium desk you do not use |
| Transport | Walking inside one neighborhood, metro for a planned hop, a taxi at night | Rideshares as the way you cross the river twice a day |
| Calendar | One neighborhood, **May, Jun, Sep, or Oct** | A non-refundable winter lease you did not mean to book, with heating you never asked about |

Annual average temperature on the row is **13°C**. That is a coarse yearly figure. It does not tell you January is mild, and it does not price the heating.

### Reality checks

- The **$800** number is a city baseline. A renovated flat in Vera or Vake is where that number goes if every meal is a cafe meal.
- The "incredibly affordable" pro is real next to [Lisbon](/destinations/lisbon) at **$2,200**. It is also the receipt that leaves the band when the habits are imported.
- Winter heating can lift a month that looked like **$550** in October. The dataset does not publish a heating line. Ask the landlord what the winter bill was, in writing, before you treat **$550** as the cold-month number.
- A cheaper room on a loud street, or up four flights with the internet in the stairwell, is a productivity tax. The dataset cannot see your building.
- Insurance is outside those three money fields. Budget it separately. This guide does not invent a premium.

## Neighborhoods that work: Vera, Vake, Sololaki, Chugureti, Saburtalo

The city is small enough to walk a lot of it and hilly enough that the wrong pin still wastes the week. There are no separate Nomad Spin destination pages for these neighborhoods. The canonical numbers live on [Tbilisi](/destinations/tbilisi). The trade-offs below are how people actually use the center. None of them come with a second rent figure. If a listing blows **$550** to **$800** on its own, the listing is the answer.

| Base | Best for | You give up |
|---|---|---|
| Vera | Week-one cafes, walkable errands, a first landing | The quietest nights, and sometimes the $550 band |
| Vake | A calmer residential month near a park | A bargain, if the building is new |
| Sololaki / Old Town edge | Architecture, baths, a short walk into the old center | Stairs, tourist pulse, uneven buildings |
| Chugureti / Fabrika | The social courtyard and a known coworking room | Sleep, if your window faces the courtyard |
| Saburtalo | A monthly price closer to the long-stay band | Stepping out the door into the scene |

### Vera

Vera is the default "I want the city without a project" landing. Cafes, small restaurants, and everyday errands sit in a walkable pocket between Rustaveli and the hills. You can do a work week on foot if your days stay inside the neighborhood, and you can reach the river and the old center without planning an expedition. If you arrive alone and want a social calendar in the first week, Vera is the easiest place in this set to get one.

**Trade-off:** you pay for that in rent and in food. This is where the **$800** baseline goes if lunch is always a laptop table. Some streets are lively late. A bedroom on those streets is a sleep problem, which is a work problem.

Pick Vera for the first two to four weeks while you hunt a monthly place. Skip it as a home base if you came to write and you already know cafes wreck your day. You can eat in Vera from a quieter apartment. You do not have to sleep there.

**Housing tell:** ask which street the bedroom faces, how the building is heated, and whether fiber is already in the unit. A cheap room over a bar is not a cheap month.

### Vake

Vake is the quieter, more residential version of a central month. Vake Park is the landmark people use to explain the neighborhood. Embassies, newer buildings, and a calmer evening sit up the hill from Vera. You can work from home here if the line in the apartment is real, and you can still get to Vera for dinner.

**Trade-off:** Vake is not the budget hack for people who found Vera expensive. A new building with a view is how the **$550** longer-stay figure disappears. Demand from people who want "nice and quiet" is the reason. Test the internet in the room. A pretty lobby with hallway Wi-Fi will lose a client call.

Best for people who want the food city nearby and a residential week. Pair it with coworking on heavy call days rather than with a parade of cafes.

### Sololaki and the Old Town edge

Sololaki sits on the edge of the old town: balconies, sulfur baths below, hills and stairs above. The architecture on the pros list is this part of the city. Lower streets take the tourist flow. A block uphill can be residential and steep. You live here because you want that texture, and because your knees and your suitcase agree.

**Trade-off:** "Old Town" is not one product. A room over the bath district is a weekend every night. A lane two turns up can be calm and a climb. Infrastructure gaps, which are a listed con, show up here as stairs, older wiring, and heating that is specific to the building. Read the cross streets, then walk them in daylight and after dark.

Best for people who will book a short test stay and only then sign a month. A weaker fit for someone who wants the first night to feel flat and obvious.

### Chugureti and Fabrika

Chugureti, on the Marjanishvili side of the river, is the creative landing. Fabrika is the converted sewing factory: courtyard, hostel, bars, and a coworking room people can name. Impact Hub Tbilisi publishes its memberships from inside Fabrika at 8 Egnate Ninoshvili Street. That is a useful week-one address because you can sleep nearby, work once in a real room, and see whether the social version of the city is the one you wanted.

**Trade-off:** the courtyard is a social product. A bedroom that faces it will learn the weekend. Coworking density on the city row is **Med**, so this block is a concentration, not a map of the whole city. Confirm the operator's current page before you plan the month around a desk that a 2023 list invented.

Best for a first landing and for people who want other remote workers in the same courtyard. A weaker fit if you need quiet by 10.

### Saburtalo

Saburtalo is the residential answer when Vera and Vake have blown the band. Metro, universities, ordinary city, more chance of a monthly rent that can coexist with **$550** to **$800**, a longer ride back to the cafe strip. Pick it if your work is the point and dinner in Vera is a plan, not a location.

**Trade-off:** English thins out, which the cons already say happens outside the center. You will use more Georgian, more translation, and more intention to get back to the social pocket. That is a fair trade for the band. It is a poor trade if you wanted the scene at the door.

## Internet, power, and coworking for video-call work

Usually yes, with a narrower margin than Lisbon. The city average is **60 Mbps**, reliability **7/10**, power stability **7**, coworking density **Med**. For a normal video-call job, that stack is enough when the line in your room is actually in that range. It is a thin stack for someone who uploads large files all day and has no second line.

It is still an average. It is not a promise about the apartment you have not tested. Reliability **7** is a step below Chiang Mai's **8** and a different product from Lisbon's **9**. Bali, in the dataset, shows **50 Mbps** and reliability **5**. The [Bali living guide](/guides/living-in-bali) is blunt about street-by-street fiber. Treat Tbilisi's 7/10 the same way: test the room. The [Chiang Mai living guide](/guides/living-in-chiang-mai) is the comparison for a city where neighborhood choice changes the work week more than the headline Mbps.

Power is also a different problem from Cape Town. [Living in Cape Town](/guides/living-in-cape-town) has to plan around `powerGridStability` **4**. Tbilisi's power score is **7**. A power bank covers a cafe and a short outage. You do not need a personal inverter strategy to have a work week here. Infrastructure gaps are still on the cons list, so ask about the building, not only the city score.

### A setup that holds calls

1. **Home line you have tested.** Ask which provider is already in the unit. Silknet and Magti are the residential brands landlords name. Run a speed test from the room, including the upload, at the hour you take calls. Hallway Wi-Fi and a cafe password are different products.
2. **A local SIM with real data.** Magti and Silknet are the practical local lines. If a call would hurt you to drop, keep a second SIM or an eSIM and learn the hotspot before the day you need it. The eSIM module on the [Tbilisi destination page](/destinations/tbilisi) is the site's booking path for that backup. This guide does not add a partner code.
3. **One coworking room you have already used for a call.** The row says coworking density is **Med**. That is a city score, not a guarantee that a famous name from an old list is open this month. Confirm the operator's current page, then do a trial morning.

### Coworking that is actually listed

**Impact Hub Tbilisi** is the name with a current public membership page, and that page places it inside Fabrika on Egnate Ninoshvili Street. Use it as a known room for a trial call. Read the rate card on their site the week you go. Those rates are the operator's, not Nomad Spin figures, and they are not part of the **$550** or **$800** bands.

Treat other famous names as open only when you can see them operating this month. Spaces here open, rebrand, and disappear. A trial morning beats a recommendation. Because density is **Med**, have the home line and the mobile backup before you assume a second coworking room is around the corner.

### Cafes

Vera and the Fabrika courtyard will let you open a laptop. That is not the same as a 45-minute call. Do the call once, at the hour you actually meet, before you tell a client the cafe is your office. Lunch rush and a playlist you do not control will lose to a room with a door.

### Checklist for a rental you might keep

- Speed test in the room, upload included, not a screenshot from the lobby
- Which provider, and whether anyone else on the booking can knock the line over
- A quiet corner with a door, if you talk for a living
- How the apartment is heated, and what last winter's bill looked like
- A named fallback (Impact Hub, or a second SIM) written down before you need it

## Visas and stay length: visa-free up to 365 days

This is operational orientation, not legal advice. Your passport decides whether the year exists. The number on our city page is the product field. The ordinance is the rule.

### What Nomad Spin currently shows

The Tbilisi row says **Visa Free**, **365** days, **UTC+4**. That is what you will see on [Tbilisi](/destinations/tbilisi). Use it as the product field. Then check that your passport is actually on the government's list.

### What the government published

Government of Georgia Ordinance No 255, "On Approval of the List of Countries Whose Citizens May Enter Georgia without a Visa" (5 June 2015), is the instrument. The consolidated text on the [Legislative Herald](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/2867361) includes a publication dated 24 February 2026. Note 1 of that text says citizens of the countries listed in the annex may enter and stay in Georgia without a visa for one full year. Note 2 says citizens of EU member states may enter with a travel document or with an identity card that shows name, surname, date of birth, and a photo.

This guide will not copy the annex. The list is the government's to amend, and partial copies drop countries. Open the current ordinance and the Georgian MFA consular site ([geoconsul.gov.ge](https://geoconsul.gov.ge/)) for your passport before you pay for a non-refundable year. If your passport is not on the annex, the 365-day field on our page does not apply to you. Use that consular site for the route that does.

### Insurance you may be asked to show at the border

Georgian embassies have published a notice, under the Law of Georgia on Tourism, that tourists may be asked to show health and accident insurance for the stay. Check the [Embassy of Georgia in Australia's notice](https://australia.mfa.gov.ge/en/news/629860-information-on-the-mandatory-health-insurance-policy-for-all-tourists-entering-georgia) for the current coverage amount, start date, and any exemptions before you travel. A remote worker on a visa-free stamp should assume the tourist rule applies until a Georgian official tells them otherwise.

Whatever amount that notice sets is an entry condition from the Georgian government. It is not a Nomad Spin price, and it is not a recommendation of a brand. The insurance slot below, and the insurance module on the destination page, are where you buy cover you have actually read.

### Work, and the right-to-work notice

A visa-free stay is permission to enter and remain. It is a separate question from permission to work.

The [Embassy of Georgia in Norway](https://norway.mfa.gov.ge/en/news/929284-new-rules-for-the-employed-self-employed-aliens-with-no-permit-for-permanent-residence-in-georgia-sh) has published a notice on amendments to the Law on Labour Migration. Check that notice and [labourmigration.moh.gov.ge](https://labourmigration.moh.gov.ge/) for the current start date, and for which local employment or entrepreneurial activity needs a "right to work" from the State Employment Support Agency, before you take a Georgian employer, a Georgian client, or a local registration.

Read that notice against your actual setup. Check the current Law on Labour Migration and the agency's own page for which remote-work cases it covers, and for any exemption or income rule, before you go. If a blog promises a clean "digital nomad visa" with a salary floor, that program is not in the sources above.

### Tax, at the level of a day count

Article 34 of the Tax Code of Georgia says a natural person is a Georgian resident for the entire current tax year if they actually stayed in Georgia for **183 or more days** in any continuous 12-calendar-month period ending in that tax year. The same article has exclusions, and it allows a separate residency route for a high-net-worth individual under conditions the Minister of Finance sets. This guide does not quote those conditions, does not quote tax rates, and does not repeat the percentage slogans that circulate about Georgia. A long stay can also create tax questions in your home country. Read [Article 34](https://matsne.gov.ge/en/document/view/1043717) and talk to a professional who will sign the advice.

Readers who want a territorial-tax residency project should use the [Paraguay tax residency guide](/guides/paraguay-tax-residency-remote-workers). That guide is about Paraguay. It does not describe Georgia.

### A simple decision frame

- **Your passport is on the annex, and you want up to a year of stay:** the visa-free rule in Ordinance No 255, after you re-read the current annex.
- **Your passport is not on the annex:** stop. Use the MFA checker. Do not book the year.
- **You will have a Georgian employer, Georgian clients, or a local business:** read the right-to-work notice linked above before you start.
- **You work only for foreign clients and you are unsure whether that is "activity in Georgia":** ask a Georgian lawyer, using the current statute, before you rely on a forum.
- **You will be in the country long enough to trip Article 34:** get tax advice at home and in Georgia. This page is not that advice.

## Best months, rainy shoulder, cold winters, and winter air pollution

The weather fields are short, and they are enough to avoid the expensive mistake.

| Window | What the Tbilisi row says | Practical read |
|---|---|---|
| May, Jun, Sep, Oct | `bestMonths` | The friendliest overlap of weather and walking. Higher demand for the good apartments |
| Apr, Nov | `rainyMonths` | Shoulder rain. Workable if you chose it, gloomy if you pretended it was May |
| Summer | Not listed as best or rainy | Workable heat in a mountain city. The row does not call it the peak |
| Winter | Cons: cold winters, air pollution in winter | The lifestyle filter. Heating, gray weeks, and air you should check live |

The [season guide](/guides/where-to-go-next-by-season) makes the same split: Tbilisi belongs with the Europe spring and autumn cluster, and a December arrival is a winter decision, not a best-month decision. **13°C** on the row is the annual average. It will not keep you warm in January.

### Winter and the air

Cold winters are a listed con, and so is air pollution in winter. This guide will not invent an AQI number, a "worst week," or a mask rule. The practical version is boring. If you have asthma, a cardiac condition, or a hard personal filter on air, check a live air-quality source the week you book, and treat a bad winter reading as a reason to choose another month or another city. Heating is the other half of the same season: ask what the building uses and what the bill did last winter. A beautiful Sololaki apartment with a space heater in every room is a different **$550** month.

People talk about late autumn sliding into the gray stretch and about pollution easing as the weather warms. You do not get to know which January you booked. The dataset puts winter air pollution in `cons` on purpose, outside `bestMonths`. Do not "extend a perfect October" into deep winter without reading the con.

Rainy **Apr** and **Nov** are the honest shoulder if you want lower demand and you can work through wet weeks. They are not winter. Do not mix the two up to talk yourself into January.

## Daily life: wine, food, hospitality, language, flights, and tax notes

### Food and wine

The `foodie` tag is earned, and it is also a way the **$800** month leaks. Bakeries, markets, and neighborhood kitchens are how you eat inside the band. Khinkali and khachapuri are the everyday dishes, and the wine culture on the pros list is something you can actually drink with dinner, not only on a day trip. Vera restaurants and imported groceries are how you eat like the month is a holiday. You can tell which life you are living by where lunch happens.

Hospitality on the pros list is real in the social sense: people host, meals run long, and a week can fill up if you say yes. It is not a promise about a government office.

### Language

The row's language is Georgian. English proficiency is **4**. The cons say limited English outside the center, and that matches the score. You can get through a cafe in Vera. A lease, a clinic form, and a Saburtalo pharmacy are a different product. A few phrases, and a translation app you have tested offline, belong in week one. Learn them before you need the pharmacy.

### Flights

The airport is TBS. Few direct flights is a listed con, so the honest plan is a connection and a buffer before your first call. Compare routings. Do not assume a nonstop from your home airport exists this season. The flight module on the [Tbilisi destination page](/destinations/tbilisi) is the site's comparison slot. This guide does not invent a carrier or a partner code.

### Getting around and staying safe

Safety on the row is **8.0**, female safety **7**. Those are high scores next to several other nomad cities, and they still mean neighborhood choice and night transport are part of the plan. Walk the block at night before you decide you live there. Use a taxi or a ride when the hill and the hour say so.

`lgbtFriendly` is **3**. That is the low end of the cities this guide compares. Overall safety **8.0** does not soften it. If that score is a hard filter, Tbilisi fails on purpose. Lisbon, at **9**, is the comparison in this set.

### Tax, again, only as a pointer

The day-count concept is in the visa section: Article 34, **183** days in a continuous 12-month period. Nothing in daily life changes that into a rate. If someone sells you a percentage over dinner, ask them to show the statute and then ask a professional.

## First-week checklist and how to compare Tbilisi on Nomad Spin

### Common mistakes

- Booking a month of nightly stays at the **$30** median and expecting the **$550** band to survive
- Treating **365** visa-free days as your personal rule before you check the annex for your passport
- Treating the visa-free stamp as permission to take Georgian clients
- Repeating a tax-rate slogan instead of reading Article 34 and calling a professional
- Landing in January because the flight was cheap and the winter cons went unread
- Choosing Vake for the park, then acting surprised that the long-stay **$550** figure did not survive the building
- Assuming a nonstop into TBS because a 2019 itinerary had one
- Running calls from a courtyard table because coworking density **Med** sounded like "plenty"

### Days 1-2

- Sleep. Walk one neighborhood in daylight and once after dark
- Buy the local SIM and test the hotspot
- Confirm the insurance you will be asked to show, against the embassy notice linked in the visa section
- Do not stack your heaviest call day on the afternoon you land, especially if the itinerary was two connections

### Days 3-4

- See more than one monthly place. Run the speed test in the room at call hour, upload included
- Ask how the place is heated
- Do one real call from a coworking room if the home line is still unknown
- Write down your actual stay limit from the stamp, and the date you must leave

### Days 5-7

- Commit to one neighborhood for two to four weeks, not to "Tbilisi" in the abstract
- Fix the work loop: home deep work, coworking on call days, one cafe that passed the call test
- Only then book the longer stay, the onward flight, and the insurance you will keep for the rest of the stay

### Booking placeholders

Still no partner IDs. These slots are last, after the week above, not before the flight is emotional. Use the stay, flight, and insurance modules on the [Tbilisi destination page](/destinations/tbilisi).
